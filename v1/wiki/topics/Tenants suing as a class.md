# Tenants suing as a class

Cases on whether tenants may sue for a class of tenants: standing, and whether their claims share enough to
be tried together. A question of court procedure; no section of part II in the stored text speaks to class
actions. K.D. Lewis reads part II on other issues, and Olen (981 So. 2d 515) cites 83.595 only in the dissent.

## Cases

- [[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]], Fla. 5th DCA 1984, reading the 1981 statute: Could the tenants sue as representatives of a class of all the tenants in the complex?
- [[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (981 So. 2d 515)]], Fla. 4th DCA 2008, no statute year stated: Did the tenant who paid the cancellation fee have standing to bring the class action? Did the trial court abuse its discretion in certifying the class?

## How they relate

- What the tenants' claims shared. In K.D. Lewis the tenants had withheld rent over the landlord's upkeep of their apartments: "While each tenant may have been affected by the omissions or non-compliance of the landlord, the extent, nature, and effect of such omission or non-compliance would unquestionably vary from apartment to apartment and from tenant to tenant." ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1034) In Olen the class were tenants charged fees under the landlords' lease: "We disagree, as the issue is not whether the three types of fees were the same, but whether or not Appellants’ practice of charging liquidated damages rather than actual damages violated Florida law or whether Appellants were required to credit tenants’ accounts with rent it received from re-letting the premises." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (981 So. 2d 515)]] at 520) Olen, a Fourth District case of 2008, does not cite K.D. Lewis, a Fifth District case of 1984.
- Standing and typicality in Olen, with a dissent. The majority: "Moss fell into the first category, arguing she was charged an illegal cancellation fee that she was forced to pay or be faced with the consequence of being reported to a collections agency." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (981 So. 2d 515)]] at 518) and "Further, with regards to typicality, Moss presented sufficient evidence proving that her claim is typical to the other proposed members in the class in that all the class members were charged liquidated damages rather than actual damages." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (981 So. 2d 515)]] at 520) The dissent: "Unlike the tenants charged for breaching their lease or failing to give notice, Moss was not violating any provisions of the lease." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (981 So. 2d 515)]] at 521)
- The same suit came back on the lease provisions themselves, and the later appeal cites this one: "Recently, in Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss, 981 So.2d 515 (Fla. 4th DCA 2008), we affirmed the circuit court’s ruling that a case or controversy existed and certifying a class action in this case." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558)]] at 559) That appeal is on [[Liquidated damages and early termination fees]].
- Two other case pages mention a class and are not listed here; neither has an issue on whether a class could proceed. [[Florida RS, Inc. v. Nelson]], on fees: "It seems axiomatic that the award of class action attorney's fees requires a class action." ([[Florida RS, Inc. v. Nelson]] at 55) [[Badaraco v. Suncoast Towers V Associates]], on 83.67: "The tenant sought to bring a class action on behalf of himself and other tenants, seeking damages for interruption of water and elevator services that occurred during renovations to convert a rental building into a condominium." ([[Badaraco v. Suncoast Towers V Associates]] at 502)
