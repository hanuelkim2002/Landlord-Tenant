# Constructive eviction

Cases on whether a landlord's acts or neglect amount to a constructive eviction, and on the tenant's
written notice before ending the rental agreement for the landlord's failure to comply, or before raising
it as a defense. Only Lakeway (83.60(1)) and Plakhov (83.56(1)) read a section of part II on it. Hankins (1931)
and Berwick (1962) predate the 1973 Act; Hankins, Berwick, and Caso are commercial leases, and in Ralston
the tenants ran a business on the premises; Kaplan reads part I (nonresidential); Walsh cites no section of
ch. 83.

Today's stored text:
- "If the landlord materially fails to comply with s. 83.51 (1) or material provisions of the rental agreement within 7 days after delivery of written notice by the tenant specifying the noncompliance and indicating the intention of the tenant to terminate the rental agreement by reason thereof, the tenant may terminate the rental agreement." (Fla. Stat. 83.56(1))
- "The defense of a material noncompliance with s. 83.51 (1) may be raised by the tenant if 7 days have elapsed after the delivery of written notice by the tenant to the landlord, specifying the noncompliance and indicating the intention of the tenant not to pay rent by reason thereof." (Fla. Stat. 83.60(1)(b))

## Cases

- [[Hankins v. Smith]], Fla. 1931, no statute year stated: Did the declaration state a claim against the lessor for a constructive eviction by his grantee after the lessor conveyed the property?
- [[Berwick Corp. v. Kleinginna Investment Corp.]], Fla. 3d DCA 1962, no statute year stated: Was there evidence of a constructive eviction, though the motel stayed open and always had guests? Must the damage be permanent to amount to a constructive eviction?
- [[Ralston, Inc. v. Miller]], Fla. 3d DCA 1978, no statute year stated: Did the trial court err in finding no constructive eviction before the City's order and no actual eviction by it, and in awarding rent for that time?
- [[Caso v. Stanley Nelson, Inc]], Fla. 4th DCA 1982, no statute year stated: Was there evidence enough for the jury to find that the landlord breached the lease and constructively evicted the tenant?
- [[Lakeway Management Co. of Florida v. Stolowilsky]], Fla. 3d DCA 1988, reading the 1985 statute: Could the tenants raise constructive eviction when their first written complaint came after the landlord's three-day notice?
- [[Kaplan v. McCabe]], Fla. 5th DCA 1988, reading the 1985 statute: Can a three-day notice to pay rent or quit be a constructive eviction?
- [[Walsh v. CAS, Inc]], Fla. 4th DCA 1994, no statute year stated: Does the judgment of eviction also bar a constructive eviction claim based on code violations that came before the eviction action?
- [[Plakhov v. Serova]], Fla. 4th DCA 2012, reading the 2008 and 2009 statutes: Was the tenant constructively evicted by the landlord's dealings with her mortgagee?

## How they relate

- Plakhov quotes the definition from [[Hankins v. Smith]] (Fla. 1931): "A ‘constructive eviction’ is an act which, although not amounting to an actual eviction, is done with the express or implied intention, and has the effect, of essentially interfering with the tenant's beneficial enjoyment of the leased premises." ([[Plakhov v. Serova]] at 1224)
- The definition at its source, and the cases that draw on it. Hankins, citing Corpus Juris: "A ‘constructive eviction’ is an act which, although not amounting to an actual eviction, is done with the express or implied intention, and has the effect, of essentially interfering with the tenant’s beneficial enjoyment of the leased premises." ([[Hankins v. Smith]] at 895) "It may constitute a constructive eviction if the landlord does any wrongful act ... or is guilty of any default or neglect whereby the leased premises are rendered unsafe, unfit, or unsuitable for occupancy in whole, or in substantial part, for the purposes for which they were leased." ([[Hankins v. Smith]] at 895) Berwick: "Constructive eviction has been authoritatively defined in Florida in the case of Hankins v. Smith, 103 Fla. 892, 138 So. 494" ([[Berwick Corp. v. Kleinginna Investment Corp.]] at 687) Ralston: "A constructive eviction has the effect of essentially depriving the tenant of the beneficial enjoyment of the leased premises as where they are rendered unsafe, unfit, or unsuitable for occupancy in whole or in substantial part for the purposes for which leased." ([[Ralston, Inc. v. Miller]] at 1069) Kaplan: "The Florida Supreme Court has recognized and defined the constructive eviction defense in Hankins v. Smith, 103 Fla. 892, 138 So. 494, 495 (1931)." ([[Kaplan v. McCabe]] at 1356) Plakhov quotes Hankins's second sentence as well: "Whatever inconvenience the Tenant experienced by being served in the foreclosure action did not rise to the level of constructive eviction because the unit was not “rendered unsafe, unfit, or unsuitable for occupancy in whole, or in substantial part.”" ([[Plakhov v. Serova]] at 1224)
- Water through the roof. Berwick (a motel) and Ralston (premises in a hotel) both concern rain coming through a roof the lease required the landlord to keep in repair. Berwick: "We take the meaning of ‘permanent’ as used in this definition to be that the deprivation of use may not be of short duration." ([[Berwick Corp. v. Kleinginna Investment Corp.]] at 687) Ralston follows Berwick: "In said case, the Appellate Court found the evidence sufficient to constitute a constructive eviction even though the damages then were not permanent in nature." ([[Ralston, Inc. v. Miller]] at 1070) "The facts in the instant case are strikingly similar to the aforesaid case, with the exception that in the instant case, the Appellants were, after a six (6) month period of constructive eviction, then, actually evicted by order of the City premised on the lessor’s failure to maintain the premises in a safe, suitable, and useable condition." ([[Ralston, Inc. v. Miller]] at 1070) Both also read a lease clause on water damage: [[Exculpatory clauses in a lease]].
- The landlord's conduct rather than the premises' condition. Caso (a shoe store) concerns a threatened lockout during a dispute over late rent: "The landlord’s express statement that the tenant was to be locked out the next day only served to confirm the tenant’s belief that he was being coerced into either executing a new lease or getting out." ([[Caso v. Stanley Nelson, Inc]] at 670) Kaplan (a warehouse leased for a bingo hall, under part I) concerns a three-day notice: "The facts of this action make it clear that a notice to pay rent or quit, standing alone and without other coercive or abusive-type conduct on the part of the landlord, cannot serve as a basis for constructive eviction." ([[Kaplan v. McCabe]] at 1357) Kaplan does not cite Caso. In Hankins, the acts were those of the lessor's grantee: "unless it does so appear, the lessor cannot be held liable for the acts of his grantee after lessor had parted with his title and ownership, especially if the lessee continued to attorn thereafter to the new landlord; therefore it was not error to sustain defendant’s demurrer." ([[Hankins v. Smith]] at 897)
- Notice to the landlord. Kaplan, reading part I, follows Richards v. Dodge, 150 So. 2d 477 (not in `cases/`): "Even assuming we would recognize an improperly filled-out notice to pay rent or quit as a basis for constructive eviction, McCabe would still not carry the day; timely notice must be given to the landlord of the objectionable act and a demand for rectification must be made." ([[Kaplan v. McCabe]] at 1357) Lakeway, on a condominium lease, cites part I's 83.20(2) for the landlord's three-day notice and part II's 83.60(1) for the tenant's written complaint: "Only after the lessor sent a three-day notice pursuant to Section 83.20(2), Florida Statutes (1985) did the tenant make a complaint in writing which would meet the condition precedent to urging constructive eviction. See Section 83.60(1), Florida Statutes (1985)." ([[Lakeway Management Co. of Florida v. Stolowilsky]] at 951) Plakhov points to 83.56(1) (2009): "Moreover, the Tenant never gave the Landlord the notice to cure required by section 83.56(1) which is a precondition to the tenant's ability to terminate the rental agreement for the failure of the landlord to comply" ([[Plakhov v. Serova]] at 1224) Plakhov also cites Richards v. Dodge, on another point: "Whether a constructive eviction has occurred is a “question[ ] of fact determinable by the trier of fact in light of the appropriate principles of law.”" ([[Plakhov v. Serova]] at 1224) Lakeway, Kaplan, and Plakhov do not cite one another. Today's 83.56(1) and 83.60(1)(b) are above; the tenant's notice is also on [[Tenant's notice of the landlord's noncompliance]].
- A claim after a judgment of eviction. Walsh cites no section of ch. 83: "To the extent that a portion of the count contains allegations of constructive eviction based on code violations which preceded the appellee’s (landlord’s) initiation of the county court eviction action, the action is not barred by the subsequent lawful eviction." ([[Walsh v. CAS, Inc]] at 562) Walsh and Kaplan both cite McCready v. Booth, 398 So. 2d 1000, and Sentry Water Systems, Inc. v. ADCA Corp., 355 So. 2d 1255 (neither in `cases/`): "An eviction, whether actual or constructive, must be wrongful or unlawful in order to be actionable." ([[Walsh v. CAS, Inc]] at 562) "In McCready v. Booth, 398 So.2d 1000, 1001 (Fla. 5th DCA 1981) this court cited with approval Sentry Water Systems, Inc. v. Adca Corp., 355 So.2d 1255, 1257 (Fla. 2d DCA 1978)" ([[Kaplan v. McCabe]] at 1356) Walsh does not cite Kaplan. Walsh's wrongful eviction issue is on [[Self-help and wrongful eviction]].
