# Points not raised in the trial court

Cases on arguments a party did not raise in the trial court, for example in its answer or in the pretrial
stipulation, and then raised on appeal, and on whether a point was kept for review at trial: motions for
directed verdict made and renewed, and counts a party left behind. Philpot, a condominium lease with an
option to purchase, cites no section of ch. 83.

## Cases

- [[Philpot v. Bouchelle]], Fla. 1st DCA 1982, no statute year stated: Did the lessor preserve the directed verdict issue for review?
- [[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]], Fla. 5th DCA 1984, reading the 1981 statute: Did the trial court err in directing a verdict against the tenants on their other counts?
- [[Plakhov v. Serova]], Fla. 4th DCA 2012, reading the 2008 and 2009 statutes: Was the landlord's notice of claim required when the tenant moved out early without notice? Was the lease proved, though it was not offered in evidence?

## How they relate

- The answer and the pretrial stipulation. Plakhov gives the same reason for two of the tenant's points. On the notice of claim, as the first of two reasons: "First, this defense was neither raised in his answer nor identified in the pretrial stipulation." ([[Plakhov v. Serova]] at 1223) On the lease: "However, in his answer, he admitted the validity of the written lease attached to the complaint and he failed to identify any problem with the lease in the pretrial stipulation." ([[Plakhov v. Serova]] at 1222)
- Motions made and renewed. In Philpot the lessor's directed verdict motions were in the record: "The record reveals that the appellant made a motion for directed verdict both at the close of the appellee’s case-in-chief and at the close of the case. Additionally, he renewed his motion at the charge conference after all the the evidence had been presented." ([[Philpot v. Bouchelle]] at 1342) "Therefore, the issue has been preserved." ([[Philpot v. Bouchelle]] at 1342) Philpot cites no section of ch. 83.
- Counts left behind. In K.D. the tenants argued on cross-appeal that a verdict had been directed against them on their counts other than negligence; when the trial court announced it would direct a verdict, their counsel had instead taken a voluntary dismissal of those counts. "The record, however, does not reflect that action by the court." ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1036) "While this procedure was irregular, no directed verdict was entered and the counts were not considered by the jury." ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1036) "We conclude that these claims were simply abandoned by the tenants and the error sought to be raised by the tenants was not preserved for appeal." ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1036) In a footnote: "Under Florida Rule of Civil Procedure 1.420, only an entire action may be voluntarily dismissed; there can be no partial dismissal and no dismissal of less than all causes of action." ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1036)
- Philpot and K.D. both concern directed verdicts: in Philpot the party who moved for one appealed, and in K.D. the party who said one was entered against it cross-appealed. None of the three cases cites another.
