# Applying an amended statute

Cases on whether a new or amended provision of part II reaches a lease signed, or a dispute that arose,
before the provision took effect.

Today's stored text:
- "If such a void and unenforceable provision is included in a rental agreement entered into, extended, or renewed after the effective date of this part and either party suffers actual damages as a result of the inclusion, the aggrieved party may recover those damages sustained after the effective date of this part." (Fla. Stat. 83.47(2))

## Cases

- [[Fuentes v. Owen]], Fla. 3d DCA 1975, no statute year stated: Was the lease's exculpatory clause void under a statute that took effect after the lease was made and the events occurred?
- [[Department of Business Regulation v. Stein]], Fla. 3d DCA 1976, reading the 1973 statute: Did the interest requirement reach deposits under leases signed before October 1, 1972?
- [[Xanadu of Cocoa Beach, Inc. v. Lenz]], Fla. 5th DCA 1987, reading the 1983 statute: Did the 1983 version of 83.48 apply to a rental agreement signed before it took effect?

## How they relate

- Stein, on interest: "In making this determination, we recognize the presumption that statutes operate prospectively unless legislative intent to the contrary clearly appears." ([[Department of Business Regulation v. Stein]] at 207)
- Xanadu, on fees treated as costs, compares a different date: "the relevant analysis does not compare the effective date of the statute providing for court costs with the date of the contract, if any, which may be the cause of action in the particular litigation, but compares the effective date of the statute providing for court costs with the date of the accrual of the cause of action" ([[Xanadu of Cocoa Beach, Inc. v. Lenz]] at 520)
- Xanadu does not cite Stein.
- Fuentes, on 83.47 of the 1973 Act and a lease made before it, in a footnote: "Under ch. 73—330, s 2 (appearing as s 83.47, Fla.Stat., F.S.A.) the exculpatory provision of this lease would be void. However, by a subsection thereof it would appear the statute is to be applied prospectively." ([[Fuentes v. Owen]] at 459) Fuentes names no subsection. Today's 83.47(2), above, speaks of a rental agreement entered into, extended, or renewed after the effective date of this part. Stein, a year later in the same court, does not cite Fuentes. Fuentes's other issue is on [[Exculpatory clauses in a lease]].
- The dates each case compares. Fuentes, the date of the lease and of the events: "In seeking reversal thereof the plaintiff contends first that the exculpatory clause in the lease should be held to be void by virtue of a statute so providing which was enacted and became effective agter the date of this lease and after the time of the occurrences upon which the complaint is based." ([[Fuentes v. Owen]] at 459) Stein, the date the leases were signed: "we agree that this Section ... does not require the payment of interest by appellees on security deposits created under written leases executed by them prior to October 1, 1972." ([[Department of Business Regulation v. Stein]] at 207) Xanadu, the date the cause of action accrued, quoted above. All three speak of applying a statute prospectively; Xanadu: "Therefore, the statute is being applied prospectively ... not retrospectively, and appellee's award of attorney's fees as costs under the statute below is affirmed and his motion for attorney's fees on appeal is granted." ([[Xanadu of Cocoa Beach, Inc. v. Lenz]] at 520)
