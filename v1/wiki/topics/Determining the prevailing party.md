# Determining the prevailing party

Cases on how a court decides which side prevailed, for fees or costs, when each side wins something or the
case ends by agreement. None of them cites a section of ch. 83: Kendall East (a home sale), Moritz (a home
purchase), and Prosperi (a mechanic's lien under 713.29) are not landlord-tenant cases, and Lucite, Anglia
Jacs, and Zhang are suits on a lease, Zhang's a commercial one.

Today's stored text:
- "In any civil action brought to enforce the provisions of the rental agreement or this part, the party in whose favor a judgment or decree has been rendered may recover reasonable attorney fees and court costs from the nonprevailing party." (Fla. Stat. 83.48)

## Cases

- [[Kendall East Estates, Inc. v. Banks]], Fla. 3d DCA 1980, reading the 1979 statute: Was the seller, whose judgment the buyers' counterclaim reduced, entitled to its costs under 57.041? Were the buyers entitled to fees under the sale contract for their counterclaim?
- [[Moritz v. Hoyt Enterprises]], Fla. 1992, no statute year stated: Which party is the prevailing party for attorney's fees when the party found to have breached the contract recovers the larger part of the money in dispute?
- [[Lucite Center, Inc. v. Mercede]], Fla. 4th DCA 1992, no statute year stated: Can a trial court find both parties to a lease in breach, return one party's security deposit, and deny fees to both because neither prevailed?
- [[Prosperi v. Code]], Fla. 1993, reading the 1977 and 1989 statutes: When a contractor fails to foreclose its lien but recovers a net judgment on the contract, must the net judgment rule decide who prevails for fees under 713.29, or does the Moritz test apply? Was the owner the prevailing party in this case, for trial and appellate fees?
- [[Anglia Jacs & Co. v. Dubin]], Fla. 4th DCA 2002, no statute year stated: Could the landlords' fee award include time spent on claims the tenant won or that became moot?
- [[Zhang v. D.B.R. Asset Management]], Fla. 3d DCA 2004, no statute year stated: Could the trial court find that neither side was the prevailing party for fees, after a summary judgment for the tenants that it found rested on the parties' agreement?

## How they relate

- The significant-issues test. Moritz took its case on conflict with Casavan v. Land O'Lakes Realty (not in `cases/`), "in which the Fifth District Court of Appeal held that the prevailing party, for the purpose of awarding attorney’s fees, is the party who recovers the larger portion of the sum in dispute." ([[Moritz v. Hoyt Enterprises]] at 808) Moritz: "It is our view that the fairest test to determine who is the prevailing party is to allow the trial judge to determine from the record which party has in fact prevailed on the significant issues tried before the court." ([[Moritz v. Hoyt Enterprises]] at 810) Lucite quotes those words ([[Lucite Center, Inc. v. Mercede]] at 493), and Anglia Jacs quotes them by way of Lucite ([[Anglia Jacs & Co. v. Dubin]] at 171). Zhang cites Moritz: "The “prevailing party” for purposes of awarding attorney’s fees is the party determined by the trial court to have prevailed on significant issues in the litigation." ([[Zhang v. D.B.R. Asset Management]] at 387)
- The words of Hensley v. Eckerhart (U.S. 1983, not in `cases/`) appear in three opinions. Moritz, as the builder's argument: "In Hensley, the United States Supreme Court held that the test is whether the party “ ‘succeed[ed] on any significant issue in litigation which achieves some of the benefit the parties sought in bringing suit.’ ”" ([[Moritz v. Hoyt Enterprises]] at 809) Malagon, a deposit case on [[Fees and costs in a suit over the deposit]]: "prevailing parties for attorney's fee purposes if they succeed on any significant issue in litigation which achieves some of the benefit the parties sought in bringing suit." ([[Malagon v. Solari]] at 353) Anglia Jacs, by way of a Fourth District case: "The test to determine prevailing party for purpose of attorney’s fee award is “whether the party succeed[ed] on any significant issue in litigation which achieves some of the benefit the parties sought in bringing suit.”" ([[Anglia Jacs & Co. v. Dubin]] at 172)
- A judgment reduced by a counterclaim. Kendall East, on costs under 57.041, a general costs statute: "This result is required notwithstanding the fact that the Banks reduced the amount of the plaintiff’s net judgment by the successful pursuit of their counterclaim." ([[Kendall East Estates, Inc. v. Banks]] at 1247) On fees under the sale contract's clause, as to the buyers: "In the first place, because they did not succeed in recovering a judgment in their favor, they were not the “prevailing party in (the) litigation” as required by the pertinent clause of the contract." ([[Kendall East Estates, Inc. v. Banks]] at 1247) Malagon cites Kendall East: "Kendall East Estates, Inc. v. Banks, 386 So.2d 1245, 1247 (Fla. 3d DCA 1980) (holding in part that plaintiff was party recovering judgment and thus entitled to an award of costs in its favor" ([[Malagon v. Solari]] at 354) Neither Moritz nor Prosperi cites Kendall East.
- Who recovered more money. In Moritz the buyers "had been found to have breached a contract but were still entitled to a judgment for the majority of the funds held on deposit." ([[Moritz v. Hoyt Enterprises]] at 808) Moritz: "Given the circumstances of this record, we find that the trial judge was within his discretion to grant Hoyt’s motion for attorney’s fees and costs." ([[Moritz v. Hoyt Enterprises]] at 810) Zhang: "Simply because a party has obtained some economic benefit as a result of litigation, does not necessarily mean that party has succeeded on the major issue in the case." ([[Zhang v. D.B.R. Asset Management]] at 387)
- The net judgment rule under 713.29. Prosperi follows Moritz while noting a difference: "Moritz is not directly on point because it did not involve a claim calling for the payment of attorney’s fees to the prevailing party combined with another claim which did not provide for the payment of attorney’s fees." ([[Prosperi v. Code]] at 1363) Prosperi: "The fact that the claimant obtains a net judgment is a significant factor but it need not always control the determination of who should be considered the prevailing party." ([[Prosperi v. Code]] at 1363) Prosperi and Kendall East both speak of equitable actions. Prosperi, on the 1977 amendment of 713.29: "the attorney’s fees awarded under that section should be taxed as part of costs “as allowed in equitable actions.”" ([[Prosperi v. Code]] at 1363) Kendall East, in a footnote on costs: "The rule is different in equitable actions in which the court has discretion as to whether, and in whose favor, costs should be assessed." ([[Kendall East Estates, Inc. v. Banks]] at 1247)
- Whether neither side can prevail. Lucite, in the Fourth District in 1992: "In a breach of contract action, one party must prevail." ([[Lucite Center, Inc. v. Mercede]] at 493) Anglia Jacs, in the same district in 2002, citing Lucite: "Generally, when an action for breach of contract contains alternative theories of liability for the same wrong, rather than separate and distinct claims, only one party can prevail in the same lawsuit." ([[Anglia Jacs & Co. v. Dubin]] at 171) Zhang, in the Third District in 2004, where the trial court found the case settled, does not cite Lucite: "A trial court may properly determine that neither party has prevailed in a contract action under compelling circumstances." ([[Zhang v. D.B.R. Asset Management]] at 387)
- None of these cases reads 83.48, whose stored text, above, speaks of "the party in whose favor a judgment or decree has been rendered" (Fla. Stat. 83.48). The cases that read it are on [[Prevailing party fees under 83.48]]. Anglia Jacs is also on [[How a fee award is computed]] and [[Pleading a claim for fees]]; Kendall East is also on [[Fee provisions in a contract or lease]].
