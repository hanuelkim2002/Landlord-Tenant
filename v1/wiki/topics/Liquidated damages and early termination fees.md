# Liquidated damages and early termination fees

Cases on a sum fixed in advance that a party pays on breach, or a fee for ending a lease early: whether such
a term is liquidated damages or a penalty, and the liquidated damages and early termination fee that part II
speaks to. Lefemine is a contract to buy a residence, not a lease, and cites no section of ch. 83.

Today's stored text:
- "Charge liquidated damages, as provided in the rental agreement, or an early termination fee to the tenant if the landlord and tenant have agreed to liquidated damages or an early termination fee, if the amount does not exceed 2 months’ rent, and if, in the case of an early termination fee, the tenant is required to give no more than 60 days’ notice, as provided in the rental agreement, prior to the proposed date of early termination." (Fla. Stat. 83.595(4))
- "“Early termination fee” means any charge, fee, or forfeiture that is provided for in a written rental agreement and is assessed to a tenant when a tenant elects to terminate the rental agreement, as provided in the agreement, and vacates a dwelling unit before the end of the rental agreement." (Fla. Stat. 83.43(6))
- "A rental agreement with a specific duration may provide that if a tenant fails to give the required notice before vacating the premises at the end of the rental agreement, the tenant may be liable for liquidated damages as specified in the rental agreement if the landlord provides written notice to the tenant specifying the tenant’s obligations under the notification provision contained in the rental agreement and the date the rental agreement is terminated." (Fla. Stat. 83.575(2))

## Cases

- [[Lefemine v. Baron]], Fla. 1991, no statute year stated: Is a default clause that lets the seller either keep the deposit as liquidated damages or sue for actual damages an enforceable liquidated damages clause, or a penalty? Was the forfeiture of a deposit of ten percent of the price unconscionable?
- [[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558)]], Fla. 4th DCA 2008, reading the 2007 statute: Was the lease's cancellation provision, letting a tenant end the lease early on payment of one month's rent, enforceable? Was the lease's default provision, setting three months' rent as liquidated damages for a tenant who leaves before the end of the term, enforceable? Did the trial court abuse its discretion in enjoining enforcement of the default provision?
- [[Wilson v. Terwillinger]], Fla. 5th DCA 2014, reading the 2012 statute: Is a liquidated damages addendum in the 83.595(4) form enforceable when the lease, a court-approved form, says nothing about liquidated damages?

## How they relate

- Lefemine, the Supreme Court on a home sale, states a two-part test: "First, the damages consequent upon a breach must not be readily ascertainable. Second, the sum stipulated to be forfeited must not be so grossly disproportionate to any damages that might reasonably be expected to follow from a breach as to show that the parties could have intended only to induce full performance, rather than to liquidate their damages." ([[Lefemine v. Baron]] at 328) In a footnote it speaks of leases: "The difference between a real estate sales contract and a lease does not affect the analysis of whether a given liquidated damages clause is valid under general principles of law pertaining to contracts and damages." ([[Lefemine v. Baron]] at 329) It describes earlier lease cases, none in `cases/`, among them Stenor: "In Stenor, Inc. v. Lester, 58 So.2d 673 (Fla.1951), this Court held that a lease provision which gave the lessor an option either to retain the deposit as liquidated damages or to apply it pro tanto against his actual damages constituted a penalty clause rather than an enforceable liquidated damages clause." ([[Lefemine v. Baron]] at 328)
- Olen applies Lefemine to the lease's default provision and parts from the circuit court's reading of Lefemine on the cancellation provision: "We agree with the circuit court’s conclusion that paragraph (16) violated Lefemine, rendering the liquidated damage provision of three months rent a nullity." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558)]] at 560) and "We disagree with the circuit court’s conclusion that this provision is “in violation of” Lefemine v. Baron, 573 So.2d 326 (Fla.1991)." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558)]] at 560)
- The option to choose. Lefemine: "The reason why the forfeiture clause must fail in this case is that the option granted to Baron either to choose liquidated damages or to sue for actual damages indicates an intent to penalize the defaulting buyer and negates the intent to liquidate damages in the event of a breach." ([[Lefemine v. Baron]] at 329) Olen, on the cancellation provision: "This paragraph did not permit the landlord the option of choosing liquidated damages or bringing suit for actual damages; if the tenant opted for the conditions of the cancellation, then the landlord was limited to one month’s rent as “liquidated damages,” and nothing more." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558)]] at 560)
- A fee for leaving early and damages for breach. Olen: "It is inartful to call a cancellation fee “damages”; if a tenant exercises his right to terminate early under paragraph (6), no default has occurred, so no “damages” are owed." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558)]] at 560) and "Section 83.595(1) does not apply because the “cancellation” agreement is not a surrender, abandonment, or writ of possession situation." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558)]] at 560) The dissent in the earlier appeal in the same suit, on the class: "Nothing in section 83.595, Florida Statutes, addresses the circumstance where a tenant is allowed, at the tenant’s election, to move out without breaching the lease." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (981 So. 2d 515)]] at 521) That appeal is on [[Tenants suing as a class]]. Today's 83.43(6), above, defines an early termination fee.
- The statute changed between Olen and Wilson. Olen read the 2007 text of 83.595, which it quotes with three options, and said: "The statute does not allow for the creation of a liquidated damages remedy to bypass the statutory provisions." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558)]] at 561) and "It is for the legislature, and not the courts, to expand section 83.595 remedies." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558)]] at 561) Wilson read the 2012 text, which has a liquidated damages remedy in 83.595(4): "This remedy is available only if the tenant and the landlord, at the time the rental agreement was made, indicated acceptance of liquidated damages or an early termination fee. The tenant must indicate acceptance of liquidated damages or an early termination fee by signing a separate addendum to the rental agreement containing a provision in substantially the following form:" ([[Wilson v. Terwillinger]] at 1124) Today's 83.595(4) has the same words, and its first sentence is above.
- Notice at the end of the term. Olen, in a footnote: "We note that the legislature specifically allowed for liquidated damages in section 83.575(2), Florida Statutes (2007), which does not apply to the situation covered by paragraph (16)." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558)]] at 561) Today's 83.575(2) is above, and today's 83.595(4) ends: "This subsection does not apply if the breach is failure to give notice as provided in s. 83.575" (Fla. Stat. 83.595(4)(b))
- Wilson, on a court-approved form lease that says nothing of liquidated damages, signed with an addendum: "We answer the question presented by holding that when a tenant and the landlord execute an addendum choosing the remedy of liquidated damages or an early termination fee pursuant to section 83.595, the remedy is enforceable if the addendum is executed at the same time as the lease (which does not make provision for the remedy) as part of the same transaction." ([[Wilson v. Terwillinger]] at 1125) In a footnote: "Of course, this holding assumes that the other provisions of the statute are complied with, including provisions relating to the sixty-day notice requirement and the amount of damages and fees." ([[Wilson v. Terwillinger]] at 1125) Wilson cites neither Olen nor Lefemine. The form lease is on [[Court-approved landlord and tenant forms]].
