# Fee provisions in a contract or lease

Cases on attorney's fees under a fee clause in a contract or a lease: which claims the clause reaches and who
recovers under it. Kendall East is a home sale case that cites no section of ch. 83; Lakeway is a suit on a
lease of a condominium unit, and its fee question turns on the lease.

Today's stored text:
- "In any civil action brought to enforce the provisions of the rental agreement or this part, the party in whose favor a judgment or decree has been rendered may recover reasonable attorney fees and court costs from the nonprevailing party." (Fla. Stat. 83.48)
- "The right to attorney fees in this section may not be waived in a lease agreement." (Fla. Stat. 83.48)

## Cases

- [[Kendall East Estates, Inc. v. Banks]], Fla. 3d DCA 1980, reading the 1979 statute: Was the seller entitled to fees under the sale contract for prosecuting its claim for the unpaid price? Were the buyers entitled to fees under the sale contract for their counterclaim?
- [[Lakeway Management Co. of Florida v. Stolowilsky]], Fla. 3d DCA 1988, reading the 1985 statute: Should the landlord have been awarded attorney's fees under the lease?

## How they relate

- The clauses and what they reach. Kendall East quotes its contract's clause: "(i)f either party is required to retain an attorney in order to enforce any right hereunder or to assure compliance herewith by the other, the prevailing party in any such litigation shall be entitled to . . . reasonable attorney’s fees." ([[Kendall East Estates, Inc. v. Banks]] at 1246) On the seller's claim for the unpaid price: "The action to recover an unpaid portion of the purchase price specified in the purchase and sale agreement ... was plainly one to “enforce a right (under) or to assure compliance” with a provision of that contract." ([[Kendall East Estates, Inc. v. Banks]] at 1247) On the buyers' counterclaim under a separate punch list agreement: "The attorney’s fee provision, by its own terms, therefore did not apply." ([[Kendall East Estates, Inc. v. Banks]] at 1247) Lakeway does not quote its lease's clause: "The lessor having prevailed and the lease providing for attorney’s fees in such instances, the trial court should have awarded such a fee." ([[Lakeway Management Co. of Florida v. Stolowilsky]] at 951)
- Who recovers under a clause. Kendall East, on the buyers: "In the first place, because they did not succeed in recovering a judgment in their favor, they were not the “prevailing party in (the) litigation” as required by the pertinent clause of the contract." ([[Kendall East Estates, Inc. v. Banks]] at 1247) Lakeway, sending the case back for trial: "with instructions that if the lessor prevails it be awarded attorney’s fees pursuant to the lease." ([[Lakeway Management Co. of Florida v. Stolowilsky]] at 951) How courts decide who prevailed: [[Determining the prevailing party]], where Kendall East also appears.
- The statute's wording on lease clauses. Lakeway, reading the 1985 statutes, does not cite 83.48 on fees; it cites Xanadu: "See & compare, Xanadu of Cocoa Beach, Inc. v. Lenz, 504 So.2d 518 (Fla. 5th DCA 1987)" ([[Lakeway Management Co. of Florida v. Stolowilsky]] at 951) Xanadu, in a footnote, on the wording before 1983: "Prior to the 1983 amendment, section 83.48, Florida Statutes (1981) provided for an award of attorney's fees to a tenant only when the rental agreement contained a provision allowing attorney's fees to the landlord." ([[Xanadu of Cocoa Beach, Inc. v. Lenz]] at 519) The 1977 text, as the Supreme Court quoted it: "If a rental agreement contains a provision allowing attorney's fees to the landlord when he is required to take any action to enforce the rental agreement, the court may also allow reasonable attorney's fees to the tenant when he prevails in any action by or against him with respect to the rental agreement." ([[Lewis v. Guthartz]] at 224) Today's text, above, is worded differently and adds the sentence on waiver in a lease agreement. The rest of that history: [[Prevailing party fees under 83.48]].
- Part I, and what each case reads. Kendall East's costs ruling reads 57.041, a general costs statute, and no section of ch. 83. Lakeway's other issue, constructive eviction, cites 83.20(2), in part I (nonresidential), for the landlord's three-day notice, alongside 83.60(1) of part II: "Only after the lessor sent a three-day notice pursuant to Section 83.20(2), Florida Statutes (1985) did the tenant make a complaint in writing which would meet the condition precedent to urging constructive eviction." ([[Lakeway Management Co. of Florida v. Stolowilsky]] at 951) The two cases do not cite each other.
