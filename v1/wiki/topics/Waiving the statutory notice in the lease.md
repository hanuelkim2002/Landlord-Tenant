# Waiving the statutory notice in the lease

Cases on whether a lease clause does away with the written notice a statute requires before an eviction.
Both cases listed read 83.20(2), in part I, not part II: Moskos was decided in 1971, before the 1973 Act,
and Clark concerns a nonresidential lease of 638 acres of land.

Today's stored text:
- "The notice requirements of subsections (1)-(3) may not be waived in the rental agreement." (Fla. Stat. 83.56(4))
- "A provision in a rental agreement is void and unenforceable to the extent that it ... Purports to waive or preclude the rights, remedies, or requirements set forth in this part." (Fla. Stat. 83.47(1))

## Cases

- [[Moskos v. Hand]], Fla. 4th DCA 1971, no statute year stated: Was the three-day notice needed when the tenant had agreed in the lease to waive all notice?
- [[Clark v. Hiett]], Fla. 2d DCA 1986, reading the 1983 statute: Did the lease's default and termination clause waive the statutory three-day notice?

## How they relate

- Clark cites Moskos: "There is authority for the proposition that the three-day notice provided in section 83.20(2) can be expressly waived in the lease. Moskos v. Hand, 247 So.2d 795 (Fla. 4th DCA 1971)." ([[Clark v. Hiett]] at 775) Moskos, on its lease: "Here the notice was not necessary because the tenant in the lease expressly waived all notice required by law." ([[Moskos v. Hand]] at 796) Clark, on its own: "However, there is nothing in the instant lease which either expressly or impliedly waives the necessity of this notice." ([[Clark v. Hiett]] at 775)
- Bell, a residential case under part II that is not listed on this topic, cites both cases: "This court has previously held that a three-day notice under an earlier version of a landlord tenant statute, section 83.20(2), Florida Statutes (1969), may be expressly waived in a lease" ([[Bell v. Kornblatt]] at 114) In a footnote it reads 83.47(1)(a) against a Moskos waiver: "Section 83.47(1)(a), Florida Statutes (1995), renders a waiver under Moskos v. Hand, 247 So.2d 795 (Fla. 4th DCA 1971), inapplicable to residential tenancies, since it provides that a provision in a rental agreement is void to the extent that it “purports to waive ... the rights, remedies, or requirements set forth in this part.”" ([[Bell v. Kornblatt]] at 115)
- Part I and today's part II. Bell, in a footnote: "Section 83.20(2), Florida Statutes (1995), is substantially the same as the 1969 version of the statute, which did not contain any part expressly covering residential tenancies." ([[Bell v. Kornblatt]] at 115) Today's text of 83.56(4), above, speaks to waiver of the notice in its own sentence; no case in the corpus quotes that sentence. The notice itself: [[Three-day notice for nonpayment of rent]].
