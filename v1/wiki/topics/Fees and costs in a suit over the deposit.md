# Fees and costs in a suit over the deposit

Cases on court costs and attorney's fees in a suit over the deposit, and on the deposit section's own fee sentence, 83.49(3)(c). Fees
under the general fee section have their own page: [[Prevailing party fees under 83.48]].

Today's stored text:
- "If either party institutes an action in a court of competent jurisdiction to adjudicate the party's right to the security deposit, the prevailing party is entitled to receive his or her court costs plus a reasonable fee for his or her attorney." (Fla. Stat. 83.49(3)(c))

## Cases

- [[Guthartz v. Lewis]], Fla. 3d DCA 1981, reading the 1977 statute: Did 83.49(3)(c) authorize fees?
- [[Lewis v. Guthartz]], Fla. 1982, reading the 1977 statute: Were fees recoverable under 83.49(3)(c) when the dispute was over how large a deposit could be required?
- [[Durene v. Alcime]], Fla. 3d DCA 1984, reading the 1981 statute: Is the tenant who recovered the deposit entitled to fees?
- [[Malagon v. Solari]], Fla. 4th DCA 1990, reading the 1989 statute: Is the tenant who recovers the deposit the prevailing party when the landlord also recovers on a counterclaim?
- [[Casey v. Picinich]], Fla. 4th DCA 1990, reading the 1987 statute: Is the tenant entitled to costs?

## How they relate

- Which suits the sentence reaches. The Supreme Court: "That section applies to suits instituted for the recovery of security deposits in which both the landlord and tenant claim entitlement thereto." ([[Lewis v. Guthartz]] at 224) Durene, two years later, on a tenant who sued for her deposit: "Section 83.49(3)(c) clearly states that when a party is obligated to adjudicate his right to the security deposit and prevails, that party is entitled to costs and a reasonable fee for his attorney." ([[Durene v. Alcime]] at 1210)
- Who prevails when both sides win something. Malagon: "This is true even though appellee recovered damages on her counterclaim, since appellants' award of damages was in excess of appellee's." ([[Malagon v. Solari]] at 354)
- Casey awards "the amount of his apportioned costs, if any." ([[Casey v. Picinich]] at 77) It does not cite 83.49(3)(c).
