# Self-help and wrongful eviction

Cases on a landlord's lockout, removal of a tenant's belongings, or interruption of a utility, and on a
tenant's claim that an eviction was wrongful. Badaraco and Grant read 83.67. Mann is a federal civil rights
case that reads no section of part II; Jacobs names an 83.67 claim that was not tried and decides negligence
issues; Walsh cites no section of ch. 83.

Today's stored text:
- "The landlord shall not recover possession of a dwelling unit except: (a) In an action for possession under subsection (2) or other civil action in which the issue of right of possession is determined" (Fla. Stat. 83.59(3)); paragraphs (b) to (d) go on to surrender, abandonment, and the death of the last remaining tenant.
- "A landlord of any dwelling unit governed by this part shall not prevent the tenant from gaining reasonable access to the dwelling unit by any means, including, but not limited to, changing the locks or using any bootlock or similar device." (Fla. Stat. 83.67(2))
- "A landlord who violates any provision of this section shall be liable to the tenant for actual and consequential damages or 3 months’ rent, whichever is greater, and costs, including attorney’s fees." (Fla. Stat. 83.67(6))

## Cases

- [[Walsh v. CAS, Inc]], Fla. 4th DCA 1994, no statute year stated: Does a county court judgment of eviction for nonpayment bar the tenant's later claim that the eviction was wrongful? Does the judgment of eviction also bar a constructive eviction claim based on code violations that came before the eviction action?
- [[Badaraco v. Suncoast Towers V Associates]], Fla. 3d DCA 1996, reading the 1995 statute: Does 83.67 give each tenant three months' rent for every interruption of water or elevator service during renovations that were not meant to force tenants out?
- [[Mann v. Hillsborough County Sheriff's Office]], M.D. Fla. 1996, no statute year stated: Did the landlords act under color of state law, for a § 1983 claim, because deputies were present during the entry and when the tenants were kept out? Could the sheriff be liable under § 1983 for failing to train and supervise deputies in landlord-tenant disputes?
- [[Jacobs v. Westgate]], Fla. 3d DCA 2000, reading the 1997 statute: Was the tenant entitled to a directed verdict on the landlord's and the neighbor's liability in negligence for the handling of his property? Was the tenant entitled to a directed verdict on the defense of comparative negligence, and does that defense include mitigation of damages?
- [[Grant v. GHG014, LLC]], Fla. 4th DCA 2010, no statute year stated: Was a motion for immediate possession, made before the case was at issue, a request for a temporary mandatory injunction? Did the trial court abuse its discretion in denying the injunction because a landlord-tenant relationship had not been proved?

## How they relate

- Walsh is the one case here on an eviction by judgment. The tenant's wrongful eviction count came after a county court judgment of eviction for nonpayment, and the court cites no section of ch. 83: "An eviction, whether actual or constructive, must be wrongful or unlawful in order to be actionable." ([[Walsh v. CAS, Inc]] at 562) "A tenant who has been lawfully evicted pursuant to statutory procedure is precluded, in a separate action, from attacking that eviction as wrongful." ([[Walsh v. CAS, Inc]] at 562) Its second issue, on code violations that came before the eviction action, is on [[Constructive eviction]].
- Badaraco reads 83.67 (1995) on water and elevator service interrupted during a condominium conversion, and turns to the statute's legislative history: "Rather, although the face of the statute might appear to support the existence of such an unlikely cause of action, the legislative history clearly demonstrates that the Legislature intended to discourage and penalize self-help eviction practices." ([[Badaraco v. Suncoast Towers V Associates]] at 503) "Thus, because the interruptions were not intended to effect self-help eviction, the trial court properly dismissed the tenant’s complaint." ([[Badaraco v. Suncoast Towers V Associates]] at 503)
- The 1995 wording Badaraco quoted, and today's. The utility sentence then read: "(1) No landlord of any dwelling unit governed by this part shall cause, directly or indirectly, the termination or interruption of any utility service furnished the tenant" ([[Badaraco v. Suncoast Towers V Associates]] at 503) Today: "A landlord of any dwelling unit governed by this part shall not cause, directly or indirectly, the termination or interruption of any utility service furnished the tenant" (Fla. Stat. 83.67(1)) The damages sentence Badaraco quoted was then subsection (4): "(4) A landlord who violates the provisions of this section shall be liable to the tenant for actual and consequential damages or 3 months’ rent, whichever is greater, and costs, including attorney’s fees." ([[Badaraco v. Suncoast Towers V Associates]] at 503) Today it is subsection (6), above, and says any provision where the 1995 text said the provisions.
- Grant concerns a claimed lockout under 83.67(2), with no year stated, and turned on whether there was a tenancy at all: "The threshold question for purposes of issuing the injunction was whether a landlord-tenant relationship ever came into existence." ([[Grant v. GHG014, LLC]] at 1067) It set out what a party seeking a temporary injunction must show: "A party seeking a preliminary or temporary injunction must establish that: (1) irreparable injury will result if the injunction is not granted, (2) there is no adequate remedy at law, (3) the party has a clear legal right to the requested relief, and (4) the public interest will be served by the temporary injunction." ([[Grant v. GHG014, LLC]] at 1067) Today's stored text has a sentence on irreparable harm, which Grant does not mention: "A violation of this section constitutes irreparable harm for the purposes of injunctive relief." (Fla. Stat. 83.67(7)) Grant's tenancy question is also on [[Whether there is a tenancy]].
- Mann is a federal suit under § 1983 and reads no section of part II. It recounts a state court order under 83.67 against the landlords, who changed the locks while their own eviction suit was pending: "In addition, Judge Honeywell entered an order against the Creasys, for violation of Florida Statute § 83.67 for preventing the tenant reasonable access to the dwelling and removing the tenant’s personal property. The Manns were awarded the statutory remedy of three (3) months rent amounting to $2400.00." ([[Mann v. Hillsborough County Sheriff's Office]] at 965) The federal question was the deputies' part in the lockout: "Although these actions might constitute wrongs to the Plaintiff, they do not rise to the level of a conspiracy or joint action necessary to show color of state law required to bring a § 1983 action." ([[Mann v. Hillsborough County Sheriff's Office]] at 968)
- Jacobs, like Mann, involves a tenant's belongings removed without a judgment; it names an 83.67 claim (1997) that was not tried and decides the negligence, jury instruction, and evidence issues: "Together, they moved all of Jacobs’ belongings into the street where the garbage was ordinarily left and covered it with a tarp. Westgate then changed the garage door code as well as all of the locks to the house." ([[Jacobs v. Westgate]] at 1178) "Westgate had no court order and no legal authority to move Jacobs’ property out." ([[Jacobs v. Westgate]] at 1179) Today's stored text speaks to a tenant's personal property in its own words: "the landlord shall not remove the tenant’s personal property from the dwelling unit unless such action is taken after surrender, abandonment, recovery of possession of the dwelling unit due to the death of the last remaining tenant in accordance with s. 83.59 (3)(d), or a lawful eviction" (Fla. Stat. 83.67(5))
- None of the five cites another of them, and none cites 83.59.
