# Rent paid into the court registry

Cases on the rent a tenant pays into the court registry while an action for possession is pending: who
must pay it, how the amount is set, what follows when it is not paid, and what becomes of it when the action
ends. Every case here reads 83.60(2), in versions from 1979 to 2014, and its wording changed over that time.

Today's stored text:
- "In an action by the landlord for possession of a dwelling unit, if the tenant interposes any defense other than payment, including, but not limited to, the defense of a defective 3-day notice, the tenant shall pay into the registry of the court the accrued rent as alleged in the complaint or as determined by the court and the rent that accrues during the pendency of the proceeding, when due." (Fla. Stat. 83.60(2))
- "Failure of the tenant to pay the rent into the registry of the court or to file a motion to determine the amount of rent to be paid into the registry within 5 days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, after the date of service of process constitutes an absolute waiver of the tenant’s defenses other than payment, and the landlord is entitled to an immediate default judgment for removal of the tenant with a writ of possession to issue without further notice or hearing thereon." (Fla. Stat. 83.60(2))
- "If a motion to determine rent is filed, documentation in support of the allegation that the rent as alleged in the complaint is in error is required." (Fla. Stat. 83.60(2))

## Cases

- [[McGuire v. Nelson]], Fla. 5th DCA 1980, reading the 1979 statute: Did the circuit court depart from the essential requirements of the law by dismissing the tenant's appeal as raising no justiciable issue?
- [[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]], Fla. 5th DCA 1984, reading the 1981 statute: Did the tenants have to deposit the rent into the court registry when they also counterclaimed for damages and injunctive relief?
- [[Olszewska v. Ferro]], Fla. 3d DCA 1991, reading the 1989 statute: Was a default for not paying rent into the court registry proper when the complaint claimed no rent in arrears and the rate had not been determined?
- [[First Hanover v. Vazquez]], Fla. 3d DCA 2003, reading the 2002 statute: Must tenants who have their own claims against the landlord pay the rent into the court registry to keep possession, and if they do not, is the landlord entitled to a default and a writ of possession?
- [[Frey v. Livecchi]], Fla. 4th DCA 2003, reading the 1999 statute: Did the court have to hold an evidentiary hearing on whether the occupants held under a contract for sale before ordering them to pay into the court registry?
- [[Grimm v. Huckabee]], Fla. 1st DCA 2005, reading the 2003 statute: Could the court evict under 83.60(2) without an evidentiary hearing on whether the occupants held under a contract for sale?
- [[Stanley v. Quest International Investment]], Fla. 4th DCA 2010, reading the 2009 statute: Must a tenant who claims the three-day notice was defective or missing deposit the rent into the court registry to raise that defense?
- [[Noimbie v. Harvey]], Fla. 4th DCA 2014, reading the 2011 and 2013 statutes: After dismissing the eviction action with prejudice, could the county court decide the parties' claims to the rent the tenant had paid into the court registry?
- [[Stephens-Williams v. Johnson]], Fla. 4th DCA 2016, reading the 2014 statute: Could the tenant have a hearing on her challenge to the three-day notice when she neither paid rent into the registry nor moved to determine rent within five days of service?

## How they relate

- Tenants with claims of their own. In K.D., the tenants had withheld rent and counterclaimed: "He loses only his right to retain possession of the premises if he fails to pay the rent to the landlord or into the registry of the court." ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1035) First Hanover follows K.D. and quotes it at length: "We find the same analysis applicable to the instant dispute." ([[First Hanover v. Vazquez]] at 1191) It repeats K.D.'s caveat: "This determination comes with the same caveat observed in K.D., 445 So.2d at 1035, that the tenants’ cause of action is not lost to them, rather they lose only their right to retain possession of the premises by their failure to pay the rent to the landlord or into the registry of the court." ([[First Hanover v. Vazquez]] at 1191) Both discuss the Third District's Freedman v. Geiger, 314 So. 2d 189 (not in `cases/`). K.D.: "While this language is capable of the construction urged by the tenants, we think the holding should be limited to its facts." ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1036) First Hanover: "We, like the K.D. court, conclude that our earlier decision in Freedman v. Geiger, 314 So.2d 189 (Fla. 3d DCA 1975), poses no conflict with this analysis." ([[First Hanover v. Vazquez]] at 1191)
- Occupants who said they were buyers. In Frey and Grimm the occupants claimed a contract to buy the home, and the courts turned to the part II exclusion for occupancy under a contract of sale: "The resolution of this factual dispute would determine whether section 83.60 is applicable." ([[Frey v. Livecchi]] at 898) Grimm follows Frey: "We find the reasoning of Frey persuasive." ([[Grimm v. Huckabee]] at 610) And: "Similarly, here, because appellants were claiming a right of occupancy under a purchase and sales contract, an evidentiary hearing was required to determine the basis for appellants’ right of occupancy before the trial court ordered eviction." ([[Grimm v. Huckabee]] at 610) The exclusion's wording then and today: [[Whether there is a tenancy]].
- How much, and notice of the requirement. In Olszewska the complaint claimed no rent in arrears and the lease gave two rates: "This disparity requires the trial court to determine the appropriate rental rate." ([[Olszewska v. Ferro]] at 12) And: "There is no evidence that such a hearing took place, nor is there any evidence that Olszewska received adequate notice of the requirement to place the proper amount into the circuit court registry." ([[Olszewska v. Ferro]] at 12) The 1989 text it quoted reads: "The court shall notify the tenant of such a requirement." ([[Olszewska v. Ferro]] at 11) The 2009 text Stanley quoted reads: "The clerk shall notify the tenant of such requirement in the summons." ([[Stanley v. Quest International Investment]] at 673) Today's text has that same sentence: "The clerk shall notify the tenant of such requirement in the summons." (Fla. Stat. 83.60(2)) Olszewska cites none of the other cases here.
- A defective three-day notice. Stanley answered a question certified by the county court: "The tenant is required to deposit the disputed rent into the court registry to assert any defense other than payment. The plain language of the statute requires it. The court correctly entered the default judgment." ([[Stanley v. Quest International Investment]] at 674) It relies on [[Bell v. Kornblatt]]: "We have previously held the notice requirement unnecessary to establish subject matter jurisdiction." ([[Stanley v. Quest International Investment]] at 673) As Stanley quotes the 2009 text, these words run together: "any defense other than payment, the tenant shall pay into the registry of the court the accrued rent" ([[Stanley v. Quest International Investment]] at 673). Today's text, above, sets between them "including, but not limited to, the defense of a defective 3-day notice" (Fla. Stat. 83.60(2)). The notice itself: [[Three-day notice for nonpayment of rent]].
- Paying, or moving to determine the rent, in time. The 1981 sentence K.D. quotes speaks of a failure to pay: "Failure of the tenant to pay the rent into the registry of the court as provided herein constitutes an absolute waiver of the tenant’s defenses other than payment, and the landlord is entitled to an immediate default." ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1035) The 2009 sentence Stanley quotes also names a motion: "Failure of the tenant to pay the rent into the registry of the court or to file a motion to determine the amount of rent to be paid ... constitutes an absolute waiver of the tenant’s defenses other than payment" ([[Stanley v. Quest International Investment]] at 673). In Stephens-Williams, reading the 2014 text, the tenant's motion came six days after service: "Unfortunately for the tenant, she neither paid the disputed rent into the registry, nor requested a hearing to determine the amount of rent within five days of the date of service of process of the complaint." ([[Stephens-Williams v. Johnson]] at 577) Today's text, above, reads "within 5 days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, after the date of service of process" (Fla. Stat. 83.60(2)). Stephens-Williams does not cite Stanley.
- The opening words and the default, 1981 to 2002. The 1981 text K.D. quotes opens: "In an action by the landlord for possession of a dwelling unit based upon nonpayment of rent, if the tenant interposes any defense other than payment" ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1035). The 1989 text Olszewska quotes opens without the words on nonpayment: "In an action by the landlord for possession of a dwelling unit, if the tenant interposes any defense other than payment" ([[Olszewska v. Ferro]] at 11). The default grew in its wording: 1981, "and the landlord is entitled to an immediate default." ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1035); 1989, "and the landlord is entitled to an immediate default without further notice or hearing thereon." ([[Olszewska v. Ferro]] at 12); 2002, "the landlord is entitled to an immediate default judgment for removal of the tenant with a writ of possession to issue without further notice or hearing thereon." ([[First Hanover v. Vazquez]] at 1190) Today's text, above, uses the 2002 words.
- After the action ends. In Noimbie the county court dismissed the action with prejudice and then divided the money in the registry: "Once the action for possession was dismissed with prejudice, the tenant’s obligation to post rent evaporated and the tenant was entitled to the return of the funds she deposited." ([[Noimbie v. Harvey]] at 608) Its reasoning starts from the statute's opening words: "A tenant’s statutory obligation to place rent into the court registry arises within the context of “an action by the landlord for possession of a dwelling unit” where the “tenant interposes any defense other than payment.”" ([[Noimbie v. Harvey]] at 607) Noimbie cites none of the other cases here. The hearing and the court's jurisdiction: [[Eviction procedure]].
- McGuire, the earliest case here, did not decide a question about 83.60(2) itself. The tenant had not deposited the rent and, on appeal to the circuit court, attacked the section for the first time: "In the second point, petitioner contended, for the first time, that section 83.60(2), Florida Statutes (1979) denies access to the courts and is therefore unconstitutional." ([[McGuire v. Nelson]] at 43) The Fifth District quashed the circuit court's dismissal of that appeal: "While we express no opinion on the correctness of the petitioner’s argument, we do not believe that the appeal totally failed to raise a justiciable issue and we think that by denying the petitioner the opportunity to have her appeal heard on the merits, the circuit court departed from the essential requirements of the law." ([[McGuire v. Nelson]] at 43) K.D., from the same court four years later, does not cite McGuire. The review itself: [[Appeals in eviction cases]].
