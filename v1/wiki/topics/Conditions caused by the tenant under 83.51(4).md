# Conditions caused by the tenant under 83.51(4)

Cases on 83.51(4), the subsection on conditions created or caused by the tenant, the tenant's family, or
others on the premises with the tenant's consent, and on when a jury may be instructed on it.

Today's stored text:
- "The landlord is not responsible to the tenant under this section for conditions created or caused by the negligent or wrongful act or omission of the tenant, a member of the tenant’s family, or other person on the premises with the tenant’s consent." (Fla. Stat. 83.51(4))

## Cases

- [[Charlemagne v. Francis]], Fla. 4th DCA 1997, reading the 1993 statute: Was it error to instruct the jury on section 83.51(4) in a suit brought only in common law negligence? Was there evidence that persons moving furniture in with the tenant's consent caused the ridges, to support the instruction?

## How they relate

- Charlemagne quoted the 1993 text: "The landlord is not responsible to the tenant under this section for conditions created or caused by the negligent or wrongful act or omission of the tenant, a member of his family, or other person on the premises with his consent." ([[Charlemagne v. Francis]] at 159) Today's text, above, reads the tenant's family and the tenant's consent where the 1993 text read his family and his consent. On the subsection's reach: "Section 83.51(4), which was read to the jury, is applicable only to exclude liability of the landlord under section 83.51, which describes statutory warranties." ([[Charlemagne v. Francis]] at 160) and, setting it beside the defenses to a common law claim: "These defenses would result in the allocation of responsibility between all parties found negligent, unlike the statutory exclusion of liability, which is absolute." ([[Charlemagne v. Francis]] at 160)
- Not yet in the corpus: no other opinion in `cases/` cites 83.51(4). Charlemagne quotes the continuing duty from [[Mansur v. Eubanks]] and cites [[Paterson v. Deeb]]. [[Torres v. Garcia]], not listed here, is another carpet case; it cites no section of ch. 83 and relies on Mansur. There the condition arose during the tenancy: "During the three and one-half to four years that the tenant resided in the premises, the carpet at the bedroom door became somewhat pressed down as a result of the foot traffic in and out of the bedroom." ([[Torres v. Garcia]] at 95) The negligence claims these cases sit in: [[Landlord's liability for injuries on the premises]].
