# Punitive damages against a landlord

Cases on whether a landlord's breach of contract, or a landlord's tort against a tenant, can
support punitive damages. No section of part II speaks of punitive damages in today's stored text.

## Cases

- [[Guthartz v. Lewis]], Fla. 3d DCA 1981, reading the 1977 statute: Can punitive damages rest on the landlord's breach of contract alone?
- [[Lewis v. Guthartz]], Fla. 1982, reading the 1977 statute: Must tenants plead and prove an independent tort to recover punitive damages for a landlord's breach of contract?
- [[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]], Fla. 5th DCA 1984, reading the 1981 statute: Did the evidence support punitive damages against the landlord?
- [[Paterson v. Deeb]], Fla. 1st DCA 1985, reading the 1973 and 1981 statutes: Did the amended complaint state a claim for punitive damages?

## How they relate

- The Supreme Court answered the question the Third District certified: "We answer the certified question “yes” and approve the decision of the district court." ([[Lewis v. Guthartz]] at 223)
- Both read the claim as one on the FHA regulatory agreement, not the rental agreements: "The gravamen of the Tenants' claim was embedded solely in terms of the regulatory agreement." ([[Lewis v. Guthartz]] at 224)
- Contract and tort. Lewis, on a claim in contract: "It is now a well-settled rule in Florida that punitive damages are not recoverable in a breach of contract action, absent an accompanying independent tort." ([[Lewis v. Guthartz]] at 223) The two later cases came to the court on counts in tort. K.D. Lewis, after a jury verdict on negligence counts: "We have reviewed the evidence and conclude that it fails to rise to this level. Therefore, the court should have granted the landlord’s motion to set aside the verdicts on punitive damages." ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1038) Paterson, on a complaint dismissed with prejudice after an intruder attacked the tenant: "Since plaintiff’s amended complaint is in tort, not in contract, it cannot be said as a matter of law that plaintiff, under the allegations of her complaint, could not adduce evidence to prove the requisite malice based on defendants’ intentional wrongdoing, entire want of care or attention to duty, and great indifference to plaintiff’s personal safety" ([[Paterson v. Deeb]] at 1221) Neither K.D. Lewis nor Paterson cites Guthartz or Lewis.
- The standard each states. K.D. Lewis cites Winn & Lovett Grocery Co. v. Archer, 126 Fla. 308 (not in `cases/`): "Punitive damages are justified where torts are committed with fraud, actual malice, or deliberate violence or oppression, or when the defendant acts wilfully, or with such gross negligence as to indicate a wanton disregard of the rights of others." ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1038) Paterson applies Griffith v. Shamrock Village, 94 So. 2d 854 (Fla. 1957) (not in `cases/`), which Guthartz and Lewis also cite: "The rule as stated in Griffith v. Shamrock Village remains viable." ([[Paterson v. Deeb]] at 1221) On the landlord's duties under the statute: "Defendants’ breach of duty to provide locks and security and to keep the common areas in a safe condition is, for the reasons stated, tantamount to a willful tort." ([[Paterson v. Deeb]] at 1221)
- The tenants' theories in K.D. Lewis. On their claim for the lost value of their apartments: "We conclude, however, that the tenants are incorrect and that they are arguing a contract theory of recovery rather than tort." ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1037) That claim, and the written notice in 83.56 the court discussed with it, are on [[Tenant's notice of the landlord's noncompliance]].
- Part II's damages section. Paterson, in a footnote, compares 83.55 as the Florida Law Revision Council recommended it with the bill as passed: "damages caused by the non-compliance, but punitive damages are not recoverable under this part." ([[Paterson v. Deeb]] at 1217) "The limitation on punitive damages was deleted in the final bill, suggesting that punitive damages may be recovered in appropriate cases." ([[Paterson v. Deeb]] at 1217) The 1981 text Paterson quoted: "if either the landlord or the tenant fails to comply with the requirements of ... this part, the agrieved party may recover damages caused by the noncompliance." ([[Paterson v. Deeb]] at 1216) Today's stored text, which does not mention punitive damages: "If either the landlord or the tenant fails to comply with the requirements of the rental agreement or this part, the aggrieved party may recover the damages caused by the noncompliance." (Fla. Stat. 83.55)
