# How the landlord holds the deposit and advance rent

Cases on where and how a landlord keeps a tenant's deposit or advance rent, who holds it, and the written
notice of how it is held.

Today's stored text:
- "Whenever money is deposited or advanced by a tenant on a rental agreement as security for performance of the rental agreement or as advance rent for other than the next immediate rental period, the landlord or the landlord's agent shall either" (Fla. Stat. 83.49(1))
- "This subsection does not apply to any landlord who rents fewer than five individual dwelling units. Failure to give this notice is not a defense to the payment of rent when due." (Fla. Stat. 83.49(2))

## Cases

- [[Nardi v. Continental National Bank]], Fla. 3d DCA 1990, reading the 1987 statute: Could the landlords sue the bank for taking the deposits their agent held?
- [[Plakhov v. Serova]], Fla. 4th DCA 2012, reading the 2008 and 2009 statutes: Did the landlord's failure to give notice of how the deposit was held excuse the rent?
- [[Atlantis Estate Acquisitions, Inc. v. DePierro]], Fla. 4th DCA 2013, no statute year stated: Was a year's rent paid in one lump sum advance rent that had to be held separately?

## How they relate

- Who holds the money. Nardi, on deposits a property manager kept: "It was a matter of the landlords' discretion to hire an agent, Geovest, to hold the funds." ([[Nardi v. Continental National Bank]] at 309) and "It is true that the tenants had beneficial ownership interests in the same security deposits" ([[Nardi v. Continental National Bank]] at 309)
- What must be held separately. Atlantis, on a lump-sum year's rent: "Because it was not advance rent, the landlord did not have an obligation under section 83.49(1) to segregate the rental payment or to post a bond to secure the tenants' rent payment." ([[Atlantis Estate Acquisitions, Inc. v. DePierro]] at 893)
- The notice of how the money is held. The 2008 wording Plakhov quoted: "[f]ailure to provide this [section 83.49(2) ] notice shall not be a defense to the payment of rent when due." ([[Plakhov v. Serova]] at 1223) Today's text, above, reads "is not a defense" (Fla. Stat. 83.49(2)).
- Left open in Plakhov: "We do not reach the issue of a tenant's remedy where the landlord fails to comply with section 83.49(2), Florida Statutes (2008)." ([[Plakhov v. Serova]] at 1223)
- The interest that depends on how the money is held has its own page: [[Interest on the deposit]].
