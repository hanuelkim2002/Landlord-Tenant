# Retaliatory eviction

Cases on a tenant's defense, or claim, that a landlord's action for possession was brought in retaliation
against the tenant, and on the landlord's showing of good cause.

Today's stored text:
- "It is unlawful for a landlord to discriminatorily increase a tenant’s rent or decrease services to a tenant, or to bring or threaten to bring an action for possession or other civil action, primarily because the landlord is retaliating against the tenant." (Fla. Stat. 83.64(1))
- "Evidence of retaliatory conduct may be raised by the tenant as a defense in any action brought against him or her for possession." (Fla. Stat. 83.64(2))
- "In any event, this section does not apply if the landlord proves that the eviction is for good cause. Examples of good cause include, but are not limited to, good faith actions for nonpayment of rent, violation of the rental agreement or of reasonable rules, or violation of the terms of this chapter." (Fla. Stat. 83.64(3))

## Cases

- [[Salmonte v. Eilertson]], Fla. 1st DCA 1988, no statute year stated: Could the tenant defend the eviction as retaliatory after admitting that he breached the lease?

## How they relate

- Salmonte is the only case on this topic so far. The tenant raised retaliation both ways: "The tenant asserted retaliatory eviction as an affirmative defense and as a basis for a counterclaim. The tenant admitted, however, the unauthorized pets, rug, and other violations." ([[Salmonte v. Eilertson]] at 180) The court states no year for 83.64(3) and quotes only its words on good cause: "That defense, however, could not be raised because Section 83.64(3), Florida Statutes, provides that the defense does not apply when the landlord proves that the eviction is for good cause, and further defines good cause to include “violation of the rental agreement.”" ([[Salmonte v. Eilertson]] at 180) Today's 83.64(3), above, lists among its examples of good cause "violation of the rental agreement or of reasonable rules" (Fla. Stat. 83.64(3)).
- Not yet in the corpus: no case page speaks to the rest of 83.64, such as the tenant's good faith or the meaning of discrimination: "In order for the tenant to raise the defense of retaliatory conduct, the tenant must have acted in good faith." (Fla. Stat. 83.64(1)) "“Discrimination” under this section means that a tenant is being treated differently as to the rent charged, the services rendered, or the action being taken by the landlord, which shall be a prerequisite to a finding of retaliatory conduct." (Fla. Stat. 83.64(4)) Salmonte cites none of the other case pages.
