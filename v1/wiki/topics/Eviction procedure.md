# Eviction procedure

Cases on how an action for possession runs in the trial court: the time to answer under the summary
procedure, defaults, equitable defenses, and what the court may decide once the action is dismissed. Only
Noimbie reads part II. Malt is a commercial warehouse lease and cites no section of ch. 83; Crocker is an
unlawful entry counterclaim under chapter 82 between a country club and its golf professional, and reads the
summary procedure statute, 51.011, not ch. 83.

Today's stored text:
- "The landlord is entitled to the summary procedure provided in s. 51.011, and the court shall advance the cause on the calendar." (Fla. Stat. 83.59(2))
- "The landlord must be given an opportunity to cure a deficiency in a notice or in the pleadings before dismissal of the action." (Fla. Stat. 83.60(1)(a))

## Cases

- [[Malt v. R.J. Mueller Enterprises]], Fla. 4th DCA 1981, no statute year stated: Did the trial court err in refusing to evict the tenant?
- [[Crocker v. Diland Corporation]], Fla. 5th DCA 1992, reading the 1989 statute: Under the summary procedure of section 51.011, does a motion to dismiss for failure to state a cause of action toll the time to answer? Was the counterclaimant entitled to a default when the other side filed only a motion to dismiss, after five days but before the default hearing?
- [[Noimbie v. Harvey]], Fla. 4th DCA 2014, reading the 2011 and 2013 statutes: After dismissing the eviction action with prejudice, could the county court decide the parties' claims to the rent the tenant had paid into the court registry? Did the hearing at which the county court divided the registry funds deny the tenant due process, and was that a ground for certiorari?

## How they relate

- Three different settings. Malt is a circuit court suit over a warehouse lease, in which the landlord sued to evict and the tenant counterclaimed; Crocker is a counterclaim for unlawful entry of a pro shop; Noimbie is a residential eviction for nonpayment of rent in county court. None of the three cites another.
- Equitable defenses to eviction. Malt cites no statute; it cites Palm Corporation v. 183rd Street Theater Corporation, 344 So. 2d 252 (not in `cases/`): "The landlord admitted never paying any rent on this sublease and the trial court could, thus, have properly considered those as equitable defenses to eviction." ([[Malt v. R.J. Mueller Enterprises]] at 1176) And: "Under these circumstances, the landlord was not entitled to insist upon eviction by strictly applying the lease." ([[Malt v. R.J. Mueller Enterprises]] at 1176)
- The summary procedure and the time to answer. Crocker reads the 1989 text of 51.011, the statute today's 83.59(2), above, names for the landlord: "All defenses of law or fact shall be contained in defendant’s answer which shall be filed within 5 days after service of process." ([[Crocker v. Diland Corporation]] at 1098) The court: "It was obliged to file all its defenses, including failure to state a cause of action, within five days and by failing to do so was exposed to entry of a default." ([[Crocker v. Diland Corporation]] at 1100) It departs from county court eviction decisions that allowed motion practice under 51.011: "Although we greatly respect the views of the learned county judges who work with this statute almost daily, the procedural scheme set forth in section 51.011 seems straightforward." ([[Crocker v. Diland Corporation]] at 1099) On the default itself, it turned to the rule on defaults: "Under the rule governing defaults, if a party who has appeared fails to timely plead, but does plead or otherwise defend before the hearing on the motion for default, a default is improper." ([[Crocker v. Diland Corporation]] at 1100)
- After a dismissal. In Noimbie the county court dismissed the eviction and then held a hearing on the money in the registry: "With no case pending before the county court, the court lost jurisdiction to adjudicate the parties’ dispute over the amount of rent owed." ([[Noimbie v. Harvey]] at 608) And: "Here, the situation was aggravated by the county court’s holding of an evidentiary hearing without adequate notice to the tenant in a lawsuit where no count of the complaint sought damages; the action was for eviction only." ([[Noimbie v. Harvey]] at 608) In a footnote: "We note that had the county court reserved jurisdiction, it could have considered post-judgment, non-merits matters like taxable costs and attorney’s fees." ([[Noimbie v. Harvey]] at 608)
- Dismissal and the 2013 amendment. Noimbie read the 2011 text and, in a footnote, the 2013 text: "This provision did not apply in 2011, so dismissal was the proper remedy. Under the 2013 statute, a landlord who files an eviction action with a defective three-day notice might serve a new three-day notice and serve an amended complaint." ([[Noimbie v. Harvey]] at 607) Today's 83.60(1)(a), above, has the sentence Noimbie quotes from the 2013 text. The money in the registry: [[Rent paid into the court registry]]. Eviction or ejectment when the occupant claims an interest in the property: [[Whether there is a tenancy]]. Review of these orders: [[Appeals in eviction cases]].
