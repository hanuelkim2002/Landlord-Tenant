# Investment & Income Realty, Inc. v. Bentley

Fifth District, Judge Upchurch, on two questions the Orange County Court certified as of great public importance, after it dismissed the landlord's eviction action as premature. The landlord appealed.

## Facts

- "On February 5, 1985, the landlord mailed the tenant a notice of overdue rent and demanded payment by February 11, 1985, which was four days, excluding a weekend, from the date of mailing." (at 220)
- "When no payment was received, the landlord filed an action for eviction on February 15, 1985." (at 220)
- "After calculating additional time for mailing, the trial court concluded that the landlord was not entitled to file for eviction because his cause of action had not yet accrued." (at 220)

## Does the civil procedure rule that adds time for service by mail apply to a three-day notice the landlord mails under 83.56(3)?

Topic: [[Three-day notice for nonpayment of rent]]

The issue in the court's words: "Does rule 1.090(e) of the Florida Rules of Civil Procedure apply to the delivery of the notice to pay rent or vacate that is mailed under the authority of Florida Statute 83.56(3)?" (at 220)

Rule:
- "We agree with the trial court and answer the question in the affirmative." (at 220)
- "Having elected to mail the notice, the landlord had to allow the mailing time required by rule 1.090(e)." (at 220)
- "Since the landlord failed to comply with the notice requirements, this action was properly dismissed." (at 220)

Reasoning:
- "When a party to a civil action conducts service by mail, an additional five days must be added to the period of time designated for response or compliance." (at 220)
- "A statutory cause of action cannot be commenced until the claimant has complied with all the conditions precedent." (at 220)
- "We conclude that this holding will not contradict the legislative intent of expediting landlord/tenant disputes but will result in tenants who receive notice by mail being treated similarly to those who receive notice by the other forms of delivery." (at 220)

## Also said

- The court declined the second certified question: "We decline to answer the second certified question as to whether section 83.56(5), Florida Statutes (1983) precludes a landlord from inserting a provision in the lease which allows him to accept late or partial rent from a tenant without waiving his right to evict." (at 220)
- "Since the case was dismissed on procedural grounds, the decision of the lower court did not rest upon this question." (at 221)
- On the ways of delivering the notice: "Under this section, the landlord had three choices: 1) hand delivery, 2) mailing, 3) or leaving a copy at the tenant’s usual place of residence." (at 220)

## Statute read

- The court quoted the 1983 text of 83.56(4): "The delivery of the written notices required by subsections (1), (2) and (3) shall be by mailing or delivery of a true copy thereof or, if the tenant is absent from his last or usual place of residence, by leaving a copy thereof at the residence." (at 220)

## Disposition

"Accordingly, the trial court’s order of dismissal is ... AFFIRMED" (at 221)
