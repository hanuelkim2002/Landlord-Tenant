# Noimbie v. Harvey

Fourth District, Judge Gross writing, on the tenant's petition for certiorari to review a circuit court appellate decision that affirmed a county court judgment. The county court dismissed the landlord's eviction action and then divided the rent the tenant had paid into the court registry; the landlord made no appearance in the district court.

## Facts

- "In the county court, landlord Christopher Harvey filed a one-count complaint for tenant eviction against tenant Joyce Noimbie, alleging nonpayment of rent pursuant to section 83.56(3), Florida Statutes (2011)." (at 607)
- "Along with her answer, the tenant posted $960 in withheld rent into the registry of the court and moved the court to determine how much she needed to deposit into the court registry under section 83.60(2), Florida Statutes (2011)." (at 607)
- "After a hearing not noticed as an evidentiary hearing, during which the tenant again pressed the jurisdictional matter, the court heard testimony and ordered $322 to be disbursed to the tenant, with the balance of the funds in the registry to be turned over to the landlord." (at 607)

## After dismissing the eviction action with prejudice, could the county court decide the parties' claims to the rent the tenant had paid into the court registry?

Topic: [[Rent paid into the court registry]], [[Eviction procedure]]

Rule:
- "Once the action for possession was dismissed with prejudice, the tenant’s obligation to post rent evaporated and the tenant was entitled to the return of the funds she deposited." (at 608)
- "With no case pending before the county court, the court lost jurisdiction to adjudicate the parties’ dispute over the amount of rent owed." (at 608)

Reasoning:
- "A tenant’s statutory obligation to place rent into the court registry arises within the context of “an action by the landlord for possession of a dwelling unit” where the “tenant interposes any defense other than payment.”" (at 607)
- "The trial court dismissed the eviction lawsuit for failing to state a cause of action but nonetheless granted the landlord damages on the merits." (at 608)
- The court quoted Cowne v. Weber, 257 So. 2d 105 (Fla. 4th DCA 1972): "He wins the battle and before he is ready or advised he finds he has lost the war." (at 608)

## Did the hearing at which the county court divided the registry funds deny the tenant due process, and was that a ground for certiorari?

Topic: [[Eviction procedure]]

Rule:
- "We grant the petition for writ of certiorari and quash the decision of the circuit court affirming a county court judgment since the county court acted without jurisdiction to award damages following a hearing that violated due process." (at 607)
- "The failure to correct a court action taken without jurisdiction, after a hearing that violated the tenant’s due process rights, is a departure from the essential requirements of law that resulted in a miscarriage of justice." (at 607)

Reasoning:
- "Here, the situation was aggravated by the county court’s holding of an evidentiary hearing without adequate notice to the tenant in a lawsuit where no count of the complaint sought damages; the action was for eviction only." (at 608)
- "The standard of review for a petition for writ of certiorari filed from a decision of the circuit court rendered in its appellate capacity is whether the petitioner has been denied due process, or whether the circuit court departed from the essential requirements of law in such a way as to cause a miscarriage of justice." (at 607)

## Also said

- In a footnote, on the 2013 amendment to 83.60(1): "This provision did not apply in 2011, so dismissal was the proper remedy. Under the 2013 statute, a landlord who files an eviction action with a defective three-day notice might serve a new three-day notice and serve an amended complaint." (at 607)
- In a footnote: "We note that had the county court reserved jurisdiction, it could have considered post-judgment, non-merits matters like taxable costs and attorney’s fees." (at 608)

## Statute read

- The court quoted the 2011 text of 83.60(2): "within the context of “an action by the landlord for possession of a dwelling unit” where the “tenant interposes any defense other than payment.”" (at 608)
- In a footnote, the court quoted the 2013 text of 83.60(1)(a): "We note that effective July 1, 2013, section 83.60(1)(a), Florida Statutes (2013), provides that the “landlord must be given an opportunity to cure a deficiency in a notice or in the pleadings before dismissal of the action.”" (at 607)

## Disposition

"Writ of certiorari granted, the circuit court’s appellate opinion is quashed, and the case is returned to the circuit court for further proceedings consistent with this opinion." (at 608)
