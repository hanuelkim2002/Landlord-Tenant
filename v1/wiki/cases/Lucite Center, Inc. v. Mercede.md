# Lucite Center, Inc. v. Mercede

Fourth District (Gunther, J.), on an appeal by Lucite, which had sued for the return of its security deposit under a lease, and a cross-appeal by Mercede, from a final judgment that found both parties in breach and denied fees to both.

## Facts

- "Lucite Center, Inc. brought a one count complaint against John Mercede for breach of a lease agreement, seeking the return of its security deposit and damages. Mercede counterclaimed for damages, alleging that Lucite breached the lease agreement." (at 493)
- From the trial court's judgment: "Neither party met their burden of proof sufficiently to be deemed a “prevailing party.”" (at 493)
- From the trial court's judgment: "Plaintiff shall recover his security deposit of $7,700.00." (at 493)

## Can a trial court find both parties to a lease in breach, return one party's security deposit, and deny fees to both because neither prevailed?

Topic: [[Determining the prevailing party]]

The issue in the court's words: "The issue is whether a trial court can find both parties to a contract in breach, award one party the return of its security deposit, find that neither party prevailed, and then deny attorney’s fees and costs to both parties on the grounds that neither was a prevailing party." (at 493)

Rule:
- "In a breach of contract action, one party must prevail." (at 493)
- "In conclusion, we hold that the trial court erred in finding that both parties breached the lease agreement, in returning Lucite’s security deposit, and in determining that neither party was the prevailing party." (at 493)
- "Thus, on remand, the trial court in the instant case shall determine which party breached, which party prevailed on the significant issues and award attorney’s fees accordingly." (at 493)

Reasoning:
- The court quoted its earlier decision in Miller v. Reinhart: "Either appellant or appellees breached the contract. The breach by one party to a contract releases the other party from performing any future contractual obligations." (at 493)
- "Likewise, in the instant case, either Lucite or Mercede breached the contract and either Lucite or Mercede is entitled to attorney’s fees and costs under the contract." (at 493)

## Related

- Follows, and quotes, Miller v. Reinhart, 548 So. 2d 1176 (not in `cases/`): "In a breach of contract action, one party must prevail. See Miller v. Reinhart, 548 So.2d 1176, 1177 (Fla. 4th DCA 1989)." (at 493)
- Quotes the prevailing-party test from [[Moritz v. Hoyt Enterprises]]: "“[T]he fairest test to determine who is the prevailing party is to allow the trial judge to determine from the record which party has in fact prevailed on the significant issues tried before the court.” Moritz v. Hoyt Enters., Inc., 604 So.2d 807, 810 (Fla. July 23, 1992)." (at 493)
- Cites, inside its quote from Miller, Katz v. Van Der Noord, 546 So. 2d 1047 (not in `cases/`): "Either appellant or appellees is entitled to attorney fees under the contract. Accord Katz v. Van Der Noord, 546 So.2d 1047 (Fla.1989)." (at 493)
- [[Anglia Jacs & Co. v. Dubin]] cites this case: "Generally, when an action for breach of contract contains alternative theories of liability for the same wrong, rather than separate and distinct claims, only one party can prevail in the same lawsuit. Lucite Ctr., Inc. v. Mercede, 606 So.2d 492, 493 (Fla. 4th DCA 1992)" ([[Anglia Jacs & Co. v. Dubin]] at 171)

## Disposition

"Therefore, we reverse the final judgment. On remand, the trial court is instructed to reconsider its findings in accordance with this opinion." (at 493)
