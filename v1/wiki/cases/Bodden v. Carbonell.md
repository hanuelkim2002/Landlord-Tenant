# Bodden v. Carbonell

Second District, Chief Judge Boardman, on the laundry operator's appeal from a judgment for the new owner of the apartment complex after a bench trial. The appellee filed no brief and did not argue.

## Facts

- "On March 1, 1975 appellant entered into a written agreement with Desmont, Inc., owner of the apartment complex Woodcrest Apartments, located at 1348 Dottie Drive, Tampa, Florida." (at 928)
- "In February 1976 appellee notified appellant that he was to remove equipment from the laundry room. Appellant did not remove the equipment and in April 1976 appellee placed the machines in storage." (at 928)
- "The trial court further found that since the agreement was a license and not a lease it could be terminated at the will of either party." (at 928)

## Was the agreement for the apartment complex laundry room a lease or a license?

Topic: [[Whether there is a tenancy]]

Rule:
- "We have read the cases which were relied upon by the trial court in reaching its decision and believe they are distinguishable from the case before us." (at 928)
- "In addition, in the instant case appellant had exclusive possession of a particularly described area in the apartment complex, and the parties to the agreement clearly intended to enter into a lease." (at 929)

Reasoning:
- "In Wash-O-Matic and Kaypar the courts held the agreements to be licenses because the sole purpose of the agreements was to supply equipment and did not grant a right of exclusive possession to the premises where the equipment was placed." (at 928)
- "In Polner and the case before us periodic rent was to be paid on the premises, the agreement was for a definite term of years, and the agreement was referred to as a lease." (at 929)
- "The evidence shows that appellee purchased the apartment complex with actual knowledge of the existence of the agreement that had been entered into between appellant and appellee’s predecessor in title and, more significantly, appellee thereafter accepted rental payments from appellant under the payment provisions of the lease." (at 929)

## Could the new owner rely on the lease's lack of one witness under 689.01 as a defense?

Topic: [[Accepting or refusing rent]]

Rule:
- In a footnote: "Although the lease does not comply with s 689.01, Fla.Stat. (1975) for lack of one witness to the five-year agreement, appellee is estopped by his conduct in accepting rental payments to assert this as a defense." (at 929)

Reasoning: none stated beyond the case it cites.

## Also said
