# Paterson v. Deeb

First District, Judge Zehmer writing, on the tenant's appeal from the dismissal with prejudice of her amended complaint against the apartment's owners and their insurers. She sought compensatory and punitive damages after an unknown intruder attacked her inside the leased premises.

## Facts

- "The hallways and stairwell were common areas under the sole control of defendants and were poorly lighted." (at 1213)
- "She specifically complained that the only lock to the front door of the building was broken and that the rear door of the building had no lock or other security device." (at 1213)
- "Defendants’ rental agent discussed plaintiff’s requests with defendants and informed plaintiff that her requests for repairs and improved security would not be met because the building was to be demolished and improvements were impractical as a waste of money to defendants." (at 1213)

## Did the amended complaint state a cause of action in negligence for the attack, though it alleged no prior similar crime on the premises?

Topic: [[Landlord's liability for criminal attacks on tenants]], [[Landlord's maintenance duties under 83.51]]

The issue in the court's words: "(1) Whether the allegations were sufficient to allege a cause of action for negligence" (at 1214)

Rule:
- "Since the purpose of the statutory duty to provide locks is the prevention of criminal acts by unauthorized persons having unrestrained access to the premises, we conclude that the landlord’s duty to reasonably provide protection from criminal attack does not have to be implied from prior similar occurrences on the leased premises." (at 1217)
- "In view of the obvious purpose of the statutory duty to provide locks and security to prevent potential damages caused by the acts of unwarranted trespassers on the leased premises, we expressly decline to require as the essential predicate to liability allegation and proof that the landlord had actual or constructive knowledge of prior similar criminal acts committed on the premises." (at 1218)
- "These allegations, considered with the alleged violations of the statutory duty to provide locks and keys and to maintain the common areas in a safe condition, are sufficient to state a cause of action in negligence." (at 1220)

Reasoning:
- "It is clear that the Florida Residential and Landlord-Tenant Act imposes definite obligations upon the landlord which continue throughout the term of the lease and which did not exist under the common law." (at 1216)
- "Certainly the principal reason, if not the only reason, for furnishing locks and keys is to prevent a human being from making an unauthorized entry into the premises." (at 1217)
- "We are not willing to give the landlord one free ride, as it were, and sacrifice the first victim’s right to safety upon the altar of foreseeability by slavishly adhering to the now-discredited notion that at least one criminal assault must have occurred on the premises before the landlord can be held liable." (at 1218)

## Was the criminal attack an independent intervening cause that cut off the landlord's liability as a matter of law?

Topic: [[Landlord's liability for criminal attacks on tenants]], [[Proximate cause of injuries on the premises]]

Rule:
- "Likewise, where the duty breached by a landlord has as one of its principal purposes the prevention of entry by unauthorized persons, the deliberate act of such person in committing sexual battery upon a tenant does not constitute an “independent intervening cause” that cuts off the chain of legal causation and insulates the landlord from liability." (at 1218)
- "We conclude, therefore, that the issue of foreseeability in this case is not a question of law essential to the element of the landlord’s duty, but is a question of fact for the jury on the element of causation." (at 1220)

Reasoning:
- "We conclude, however, that the type harm suffered by the plaintiff in this case has so frequently resulted from the same type negligence, to wit, not providing security locks on doors and windows to prevent unauthorized entry by criminals, that in the field of human experience the same type of result may be expected again." (at 1218)
- "Where reasonable persons might differ, the ultimate determination of foreseeability and legal cause are questions for the jury." (at 1218)

## Is a landlord's violation of 83.51 negligence per se, or evidence of negligence?

Topic: [[Landlord's maintenance duties under 83.51]]

Rule:
- "A landlord’s violation of the act is at least evidence of negligence." (at 1216)
- In a footnote: "We do not consider this section to be a “strict liability statute” in the sense that it protects a particular class of persons from their inability to protect themselves" (at 1217)

Reasoning:
- In the same footnote: "nor is it a penal statute designed to protect a particular class of persons from a particular injury or type of injury, as was the statute in De Jesus, since no criminal penalties are imposed for violating section 83.51." (at 1217)

## Does the 83.51 duty to provide locks and keys reach the building's outside entrances and the hallway bathroom door?

Topic: [[Landlord's maintenance duties under 83.51]]

Rule:
- In a footnote: "Appellees argue that section 83.51 does not require the landlord to provide “locks and keys” for outside entrances to the building or the upstairs hallway bathroom door because these areas are not a part of the “dwelling unit.” We disagree." (at 1217)

Reasoning:
- "The term “dwelling unit” is defined in section 83.43(2) to mean a structure or part of a structure that is rented for use as a residence, and is broad enough to include the bathroom and common areas." (at 1217)

## Did the amended complaint state a claim for punitive damages?

Topic: [[Punitive damages against a landlord]]

The issue in the court's words: "(2) Whether the allegations were sufficient to state a claim for punitive damages" (at 1214)

Rule:
- "We also conclude that the trial court erred in dismissing the claim for punitive damages." (at 1220)
- "Since plaintiff’s amended complaint is in tort, not in contract, it cannot be said as a matter of law that plaintiff, under the allegations of her complaint, could not adduce evidence to prove the requisite malice based on defendants’ intentional wrongdoing, entire want of care or attention to duty, and great indifference to plaintiff’s personal safety" (at 1221)

Reasoning:
- "Defendants were under a statutory mandate to act in good faith in performing every duty under the act." (at 1220)
- "Defendants’ breach of duty to provide locks and security and to keep the common areas in a safe condition is, for the reasons stated, tantamount to a willful tort." (at 1221)

## Also said

- In a footnote on the contract allegations: "Although the statutory obligations created by section 83.51 are by implication a part of the landlord’s duties under the lease agreement" (at 1214)
- In the same footnote: "we consider the breach of implied contract allegations only to the extent they may give rise to a duty of care or warranty of habitability cognizable in tort, and decline to consider the sufficiency of the complaint to state a cause of action in contract." (at 1214)
- In a footnote on 83.55 as the Council recommended it: "The limitation on punitive damages was deleted in the final bill, suggesting that punitive damages may be recovered in appropriate cases." (at 1217)
- On the implied warranty of habitability, which it left open: "We do not consider whether the facts alleged in the amended complaint are sufficient to state a cause of action for breach of implied warranty of habitability recognized by the Supreme Court in Mansur v. Eubanks, 401 So.2d 1328 (Fla.1981)." (at 1220)
- In a footnote on evidence of crimes nearby: "While the evidence need not relate to offenses occurring on the premises or within the block where the apartment is located, we do not venture any opinion on the admissibility of specific evidence that may be offered at trial." (at 1218)

## Statute read

- The court quoted 83.51(2)(a) as in effect in 1981: "landlord of a dwelling unit ... shall, at all times during the tenancy, make reasonable provision for ... locks and keys [and] the clean and safe condition of common areas." (at 1216)
- It quoted 83.44 (1981): "an obligation of good faith in the performance or enforcement of every duty under the act." (at 1216)
- It quoted 83.54 and 83.55 (1981): "any right or duty declared in this part is enforceable by civil action" (at 1216)
- "if either the landlord or the tenant fails to comply with the requirements of ... this part, the agrieved party may recover damages caused by the noncompliance." (at 1216)
- In a footnote, it quoted 83.55 as the Florida Law Revision Council recommended it: "damages caused by the non-compliance, but punitive damages are not recoverable under this part." (at 1217)
