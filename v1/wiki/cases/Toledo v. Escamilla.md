# Toledo v. Escamilla

Third District, Judge Rothenberg writing, on the occupant's petition for certiorari to review a circuit court appellate opinion that affirmed a county court judgment of eviction. She had lived in the condominium unit with her former boyfriend, who later sold it to the plaintiff.

## Facts

- "Ms. Toledo answered the complaint asserting that she lived with her former boyfriend and their son in the subject property, and that after her former boyfriend left, she and her son remained in the property." (at 1029)
- "Believing that she was a part owner of the property, Ms. Toledo continued to pay the mortgage, association fees, and maintenance, and she also made improvements to the property. Several years later, without Ms. Toledo’s knowledge, her former boyfriend sold the property to Mr. Escamilla." (at 1029)
- "The county court entered a default judgment against Ms. Toledo for failing to place rent into the court’s registry." (at 1029)

## Was the occupant a tenant under the Act, so that the county court could proceed by eviction?

Topic: [[Whether there is a tenancy]]

Rule:
- "Thus, a landlord/tenant relationship is a condition precedent to applying this statutory remedy." (at 1030)
- "Therefore, as Ms. Toledo is not a “tenant” as defined by the Act, the county court lacked subject matter jurisdiction." (at 1030)

Reasoning:
- "In the instant case, it is undisputed that there was no written “rental agreement.” Moreover, it is also undisputed that after Ms. Toledo’s former boyfriend moved from the condominium unit, Ms. Toledo continued to live there for approximately four years." (at 1030)
- "Thus, any oral agreement between Ms. Toledo and her former boyfriend cannot be considered a “rental agreement” as defined in section 83.43(7), as the oral agreement, if any, between Ms. Toledo and her former boyfriend was not for a duration of less than one year." (at 1030)

## Once the occupant answered that she was not a tenant and claimed an equitable interest in the property, which remedy and which court did the case belong to?

Topic: [[Whether there is a tenancy]]

Rule:
- "We also find that when Ms. Toledo asserted in her answer that she was not a tenant and that she had an equitable interest in the property, ejectment, not eviction, was the proper remedy, and the matter should have been transferred to the circuit court." (at 1030)
- "Thus, the county court did not have subject matter jurisdiction to determine whether Ms. Toledo had an equitable interest in the condominium unit." (at 1030)

Reasoning:
- "The circuit court has “exclusive original jurisdiction” over ejectment actions." (at 1030)
- Citing Vasquez v. Futch, 7 Fla. L. Weekly Supp. 100a (Fla. 19th Cir. Ct. 1999): "Once the [party in possession of the real property] raised the issue of a competing claim to title as an affirmative defense, it could no longer be assumed that the case was a landlord/tenant matter within the jurisdiction of the county court." (at 1030)

## Could a county court's lack of subject matter jurisdiction be reviewed on second-tier certiorari?

Rule:
- "We find that the circuit court failed to apply the correct law in affirming the county court’s judgment, as the county court lacked subject matter jurisdiction to enter the judgment of eviction." (at 1029)
- "Because the county court lacked subject matter jurisdiction to resolve the issues before it, the decision reached by the county court constitutes fundamental error reviewable by this court on a petition for certiorari." (at 1030)

Reasoning:
- "We begin our analysis by acknowledging that a district court’s scope of review on second-tier certiorari is whether the circuit court afforded procedural due process and whether it applied the correct law." (at 1029)
- The court quoted Stel-Den of America, Inc. v. Roof Structures, Inc., 438 So. 2d 882 (Fla. 4th DCA 1983): "An incorrect decision on subject matter jurisdiction is fundamental error. It constitutes a departure from the essential requirements of law, sufficient to justify invocation of [district] court’s certiorari jurisdiction." (at 1031)

## Also said

- In a footnote: "The appellate court issued a stay, but Ms. Toledo was evicted because the Sheriff’s office had already issued the writ of possession." (at 1029)

## Statute read

- The court quoted the 2004 text of 83.59(2): "A landlord ... applying for the removal of a tenant shall file in the county court of the county where the premises are situated a complaint describing the dwelling unit and stating the facts that authorize its recovery.... The landlord is entitled to the summary procedure provided in s. 51.011[F.S. 1971], and the court shall advance the cause on the calendar." (at 1029)
- And the 2004 definitions: "Section 83.43(4), Florida Statutes (2004), defines “tenant” as “any person entitled to occupy a dwelling unit under a rental agreement.”" (at 1030)
- "Moreover, section 83.43(7) defines “rental agreement” as “any written agreement, or oral agreement if for less duration than 1 year, providing for use and occupancy of premises.”" (at 1030)

## Disposition

"Therefore, as the county court lacked subject matter jurisdiction, we grant the petition, quash the circuit court’s opinion, and instruct the circuit court to enter an order reversing the county court’s judgment of eviction and to transfer Mr. Escamilla’s action to the circuit court pursuant to Florida Rule of Civil Procedure 1.060(a)." (at 1031)
