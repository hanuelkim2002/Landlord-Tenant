# Wolf v. State

Second District, Judge Kelly, on a criminal defendant's appeal from his conviction for burglary of the home where he had rented a room. He argued that the trial court should have given a special jury instruction reciting parts of ch. 83. Judge Peters concurred specially; Chief Judge Davis dissented.

## Facts

- "The victim, Richard Schmid, rented a bedroom in his home to Mr. Wolf on a month-to-month basis." (at 1204)
- "After the first month, Mr. Wolf failed to timely pay his rent and the victim asked him to leave. He gave Mr. Wolf until the next day to move out, which Mr. Wolf did, leaving his house key but taking most of his belongings with him. The victim had the locks changed the same day Mr. Wolf left." (at 1204)
- The requested special instruction began: "A landlord cannot terminate a tenancy on the spot by an immediate “verbal eviction.”" (at 1205)

## Did the trial court abuse its discretion by refusing a special jury instruction that recited parts of ch. 83 on ending a tenancy?

Topic: [[Ending a tenancy]], [[Landlord and tenant law in criminal cases]]

The issue in the court's words: "In this appeal he argues that the trial court abused its discretion when it denied his request for a special jury instruction that was a recitation of various subsections of chapter 83, Florida Statutes, the Florida Residential Landlord–Tenant Act." (at 1204)

Rule:
- "We conclude that the trial court acted well within its discretion when it found Mr. Wolf had failed to establish that he was entitled to the requested instruction." (at 1204)
- "First, it is self-evident that the special instruction would have been confusing to the jury." (at 1207)
- "Second, we also agree with the trial court that Mr. Wolf did not meet his burden to demonstrate that the standard jury instruction did not adequately cover his theory of defense." (at 1207)

Reasoning:
- "In order to establish entitlement to a special jury instruction, a defendant “must prove: (1) the special instruction was supported by the evidence; (2) the standard instruction did not adequately cover the theory of defense; and (3) the special instruction was a correct statement of the law and not misleading or confusing.”" (at 1207)
- "Yet, despite this argument, counsel rejected element 3 of the standard jury instruction as inapplicable. Thus, whatever defense Mr. Wolf thought the “unlawful eviction” provided, he apparently did not believe it meant he was licensed to enter the home." (at 1208)
- "Mr. Wolf’s rejection of element 3 of the standard instruction is problematic for an additional reason—without any reference to license or consent to enter the premises as a defense to burglary, it is hard to imagine what the jury was supposed to glean from the special instruction." (at 1208)

Dissent:
- "And Schmid’s attempt to simply assert actual physical control over the premises does not, in and of itself, terminate the lease agreement or the rights conveyed thereunder." (at 1212)
- "Schmid did not give Wolf the statutorily required notice that he was terminating the lease or file a complaint for possession; therefore he did not legally terminate the rental agreement with Wolf." (at 1212)
- "That is, if he held a legal right to possess/occupy the property and if that legal right had not been terminated in the statutorily prescribed manner, then Wolf was licensed to enter under section 810.02(1)(b)(1) and could present a defense that he did not commit burglary." (at 1213)

## Also said

- Judge Peters, concurring specially: "Arguably, a special instruction containing specific, applicable provisions of the Florida Residential Landlord and Tenant Act together with such other principles of law as may be required to make any such special instruction both accurate and complete could have been given to augment the standard instruction. However, no such instruction was presented or requested." (at 1208)
- Chief Judge Davis, dissenting, on abandonment: "I do recognize that a tenant can abandon or waive his possessory interest rights by his actions or words." (at 1212)
- And, on the motion for judgment of acquittal: "Based on the same reasoning, I conclude that whether Wolf’s actions exhibited an abandonment of his possessory interest is a question of fact for the jury to determine and that the trial court was correct to deny Wolf’s motion for judgment of acquittal and to submit the issue to the jury." (at 1213)

## Statute read

- The dissent quoted 83.57(3), on ending a month-to-month tenancy: "[fifteen] days’ notice prior to the end of any monthly period." (at 1210)

## Disposition

"Because we conclude that the trial court did not abuse its discretion when it found Mr. Wolf failed to establish his entitlement to the special jury instruction, we affirm." (at 1208)
