# Ralston, Inc. v. Miller

Third District, Associate Judge Grady L. Crawford (Ret.) writing, on the tenants' appeal from a judgment dismissing their counterclaim against the landlord and a later judgment for the landlord on his complaint for rent. The leased premises were in the Opa Locka Hotel, which the City declared unsafe.

## Facts

- "This cause originated when the appellee, plaintiff below, filed an amended complaint as the owner of the Opa Locka Hotel, claiming rent due, attorneys’ fees, an accounting, balance due on a promissory note, and damages." (at 1067)
- "The first fissures in the dome permitting water to pour through occurred in January, 1975." (at 1069)
- "For the succeeding five (5) months, the landlord did nothing with regard to the condition and the continuing deterioration resulting in larger and increased orifices in the dome and roof through which rainwater continued to come into the building and into the leased premises in question, until July, 1975, when the City of Opa Locka declared the leased premises in question to be unsafe for human habitation and, by emergency order, locked out the appellants." (at 1069)

## Was the judgment dismissing the counterclaim a final order, so that the tenants' appeal from it came too late?

Rule:
- "The rule of inter-relationship of the parties and of the claims is clearly applicable in this cause." (at 1067)
- "The trial court’s judgment, dismissing the appellant’s counterclaim, did not constitute an end to the judicial labor between the same parties relating to the lease and issues of liabilities under provisions of the lease which were in conflict." (at 1067)

Reasoning:
- "The complaint and the countercomplaint are grounded on the lease between the parties and the alleged breaches thereof." (at 1067)
- The court quoted S.L.T. Warehouse Company v. Webb, 304 So.2d 97, 99: "Piecemeal appeals will not be permitted where claims are inter related and involve the same transaction and the same parties remain in the suit." (at 1067)

## Did the lease's printed clauses on water damage relieve the lessor of the typed covenant to keep the roof and structure in repair?

Topic: [[Interpreting the lease]], [[Exculpatory clauses in a lease]]

Rule:
- "Paragraph 23 of the lease, especially written and inserted in the lease by the parties, is clear and unambiguous." (at 1069)
- "The lessor is required to maintain the roof and exterior of the premises and the structure in good repair and condition. It is uncontroverted that the lessor failed to comply with these obligations." (at 1069)

Reasoning:
- "The obvious contradictions between paragraph 23 on one hand, and paragraphs 2 and 13 on the other hand, are manifest. In respect to this type of contradiction, the law is well settled." (at 1069)
- The court quoted Allegheny Mutual Casualty Co. v. State, 176 So.2d 362: "There is the rule, applicable and directly controlling here, that where a part of a contract is written or typed and part is printed, and the written or typed and the printed part are apparently inconsistent or there is reasonable doubt as to the sense and meaning of the total instrument, the words in writing or typing will control." (at 1069)
- The court quoted Berwick Corp. v. Kleinginna Investment Corp., 143 So.2d 684: "This provision of the lease cannot be construed to destroy the effect of the requirement that the landlord keep the roof sound and watertight." (at 1070)

## Was the lessee bound to make the repairs itself when the lessor did not?

Rule:
- "There is no law which supports the lower court’s finding that the lessee was obligated to make the repairs in the absence of the lessor’s so doing." (at 1070)

Reasoning:
- "The word “may ”, when given its ordinary meaning, denotes a permissive term rather than the mandatory connotation of the word “shall”" (at 1070)
- The court quoted paragraph 23 of the lease: "In the event lessor fails to make the repairs within a reasonable time after written notice the lessees may cause repairs to be made and may deduct the cost thereof from the next ensuing rental payments." (at 1070)

## Did the trial court err in finding no constructive eviction before the City's order and no actual eviction by it, and in awarding rent for that time?

Topic: [[Constructive eviction]]

Rule:
- "The lower court failed to find any constructive eviction for the period of April through July, when no rent was paid, and the court failed to find actual eviction and termination of the lease by reason of the City’s declaration that the building was unsafe and the City’s action in dispossessing the appellants, the sole remaining tenant." (at 1070)
- "The facts in the instant case are strikingly similar to the aforesaid case, with the exception that in the instant case, the Appellants were, after a six (6) month period of constructive eviction, then, actually evicted by order of the City premised on the lessor’s failure to maintain the premises in a safe, suitable, and useable condition." (at 1070)

Reasoning:
- "The rule in Florida is that any disturbance of the possession of a tenant by the landlord that renders the premises unfit for occupancy, for the purposes for which they were demised or that deprives the tenant of the beneficial enjoyment of the premises, amounts to a constructive eviction." (at 1069)
- "The evidence reflects that beginning in January, 1975, the intrusions of water by reason of rain and ever increasing holes in the roof, seriously interfered with the possession and use of the premises and with the operation of a viable business by the appellants" (at 1070)
- "In said case, the Appellate Court found the evidence sufficient to constitute a constructive eviction even though the damages then were not permanent in nature." (at 1070)

## Related

- Follows on constructive eviction and quotes its reading of a water-damage clause [[Berwick Corp. v. Kleinginna Investment Corp.]]: "As in the matter before this court, the landlord in Berwick sought to avoid damages by the defense of a provision of the lease that the lessee was required to take the risk of damage to the property by reason of water, bursting pipes, gas leaks, water system or sewerage leaking, etc." (at 1070)
- Cites for its description of constructive eviction [[Hankins v. Smith]]: "A constructive eviction has the effect of essentially depriving the tenant of the beneficial enjoyment of the leased premises as where they are rendered unsafe, unfit, or unsuitable for occupancy in whole or in substantial part for the purposes for which leased." (at 1069)
- Quotes on when an order is final for appeal S.L.T. Warehouse Co. v. Webb, 304 So. 2d 97 (not in `cases/`): "Generally, the test employed by the appellate court to determine finality of an order, judgment or decree is whether the order in question constitutes an end to the judicial labor in the cause, and nothing further remains to be done by the court to effectuate a termination of the cause as between the parties directly affected." (at 1067)
- Quotes on typed terms against printed terms Allegheny Mutual Casualty Co. v. State, 176 So. 2d 362 (not in `cases/`): "The reason for the according of greater effect to the written or typed part as against the printed portion if they are inconsistent, is that the written or typed words are the immediate language and terms selected by the parties themselves to express their meaning, while the printed form is intended for general use without reference to particular objects and aims." (at 1069)
- Quotes on a typewritten term against a printed or stamped one Nat Harrison Associates, Inc. v. Florida Power & Light, 162 So. 2d 298 (not in `cases/`): "Therefore, the typewritten paragraph should control over the printed matter." (at 1069)

## Disposition

"The judgment, dismissing counterclaim, entered July 13, 1976, as well as the judgment in favor of the appellee, entered March 29, 1977, are hereby reversed and the cause remanded to the trial court for further proceedings." (at 1070)
