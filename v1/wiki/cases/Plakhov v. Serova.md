# Plakhov v. Serova

Fourth District, per curiam, on the tenant's appeal from a judgment for unpaid rent after a non-jury
trial. The tenant raised several issues; the court took each in turn.

## Facts

- "The unit was the only real estate which the Landlord owned in Florida." (at 1222)
- "The lease did not limit the type of damages to which the security deposit could be applied." (at 1222)
- "The Tenant moved out of the unit in April 2009." (at 1222)
- "The trial judge entered a final judgment for the Landlord for $16,700, computed as follows: $26,400 rent owed-$7500 rental payments-$2200 security deposit." (at 1222)

## Was the landlord's notice of claim required when the tenant moved out early without notice?

Topic: [[Landlord's notice of claim on the deposit]], [[Points not raised in the trial court]]

Rule:
- "This argument fails for two reasons. First, this defense was neither raised in his answer nor identified in the pretrial stipulation. Second, the Landlord's obligation to provide this notice was excused by the Tenant's failure to give the seven-day notice implicated when a “tenant ... vacates or abandons the premises prior to the expiration of the term specified in the written lease.”" (at 1223)

Reasoning:
- "In this situation, the Landlord and the Tenant are free to litigate over their entitlement to the security deposit without leaping any procedural hurdles." (at 1223)
- "The lease did not limit how the security deposit could be applied, so the trial judge properly applied it to reduce the amount of damages owed by the Tenant." (at 1223)
- "The Tenant thus had an opportunity to establish his “right” to the security deposit within the meaning of section 83.49(5), but was unable to do so." (at 1223)

## Did the landlord's failure to give notice of how the deposit was held excuse the rent?

Topic: [[How the landlord holds the deposit and advance rent]]

Rule:
- "The Landlord owned but one rental unit in Florida, so she was excused from the subsection's notice requirements." (at 1223)

Reasoning:
- "However, that subsection “does not apply to any landlord who rents fewer than five individual dwelling units.”" (at 1223)
- "In any event, the statute provides that “[f]ailure to provide this [section 83.49(2) ] notice shall not be a defense to the payment of rent when due.”" (at 1223)

Left open: "We do not reach the issue of a tenant's remedy where the landlord fails to comply with section 83.49(2), Florida Statutes (2008)." (at 1223)

## Was the tenant constructively evicted by the landlord's dealings with her mortgagee?

Topic: [[Constructive eviction]]

Rule:
- "Whatever inconvenience the Tenant experienced by being served in the foreclosure action did not rise to the level of constructive eviction because the unit was not “rendered unsafe, unfit, or unsuitable for occupancy in whole, or in substantial part.”" (at 1224)
- "Moreover, the Tenant never gave the Landlord the notice to cure required by section 83.56(1) which is a precondition to the tenant's ability to terminate the rental agreement for the failure of the landlord to comply “with s. 83.51(1) or material provisions of the rental agreement.”" (at 1224)

Reasoning:
- The court quoted the Florida Supreme Court's definition: "A ‘constructive eviction’ is an act which, although not amounting to an actual eviction, is done with the express or implied intention, and has the effect, of essentially interfering with the tenant's beneficial enjoyment of the leased premises." (at 1224)
- "Whether a constructive eviction has occurred is a “question[ ] of fact determinable by the trier of fact in light of the appropriate principles of law.”" (at 1224)

## Was the lease proved, though it was not offered in evidence?

Topic: [[Points not raised in the trial court]]

Rule:
- "However, in his answer, he admitted the validity of the written lease attached to the complaint and he failed to identify any problem with the lease in the pretrial stipulation." (at 1222)

Reasoning: none stated beyond the sentence above.

## Could the tenant rely on the landlord's mortgage rider to attack the lease?

Rule:
- "However, the mortgage did not vest any rights in the Tenant because the Tenant was not an intended third party beneficiary of it." (at 1223)

Reasoning:
- "A party is an intended beneficiary only if [both]parties to the contract clearly express ... an intent to primarily and directly benefit the third party or a class of persons to which that party claims to belong." (at 1223)

## Related

- Distinguishes [[Durene v. Alcime]]: "The Tenant's reliance on Durene v. Alcime, 448 So.2d 1208 (Fla. 3d DCA 1984), is unavailing, since that case involved a situation where the landlord's obligation to give the section 83.49(3)(a) notice was not excused by the Tenant's failure to give the notice required by section 83.49(5)." (at 1223)
- Quotes the constructive eviction definition from [[Hankins v. Smith]] (Fla. 1931).

## Disposition

"This is an appeal from a final judgment entered after a non-jury trial that awarded damages to a residential landlord for unpaid rent. We affirm." (at 1222)
