# Cupeiro v. Baron

Third District (Schwartz, C.J.), on the client's appeal from a money judgment after a non-jury trial. The attorney had bought an aircraft to lease to his client under an alleged oral agreement, and the client made none of the payments.

## Facts

- "After a non-jury trial, the appellee Baron, an attorney, won a money judgment against a client, the appellant Cupeiro, for the breach of an alleged oral agreement under which Baron was to purchase an aircraft for $200,000 and lease it to Cupeiro for two years at a rental of $15,000 per month." (at 370)
- "After a single month of non-payment—during which Cupeiro never even saw, much less took possession of the plane—Baron began efforts, apparently on his own account, to dispose of it." (at 370)

## Could the attorney enforce the alleged lease and sale agreement against his client?

Rule:
- "In our view, the circumstances of the alleged contract ... completely fail to meet the standards by which an agreement entered into between an attorney and his client must be assessed." (at 370)
- "Particularly because the record does not contain the required clear and convincing evidence of Baron’s full disclosure to his client of the ramifications of the transaction, see Abstract & Title Corp. v. Cochran, 414 So.2d 284, 285 (Fla. 4th DCA 1982), we cannot enforce this supposed agreement." (at 371)
- "Applying these standards, not only does the “fairness” of the transaction not affirmatively appear, its direct antithesis is manifest upon this record." (at 371)

Reasoning:
- "If considered, as seems most likely in terms of the commercial realities of the situation, as a financing device, the return of $360,000 to Baron on a $200,000 investment over two years amounts to a clearly excessive interest rate of 40% per year." (at 371)
- "Treated alternatively as a lease and sale provision, the net profit to the attorney would amount—and as enforced by the court did amount—to no less than 80%." (at 371)
- The court quoted Gerlach v. Donnelly, 98 So. 2d 493 (Fla. 1957): "Business transactions between attorney and client are and always ought to be subject to the closest scrutiny because of this underlying relationship." (at 371)

## Also said

- In a footnote, on a point the court did not need to reach: "Although we need not reach the question, we note, as an alternative basis for reversal, that the measure of damages adopted below was incorrect." (at 371)
- The same footnote continues, citing 83.595(1)(a) with a see also: "Since there was no showing that Baron’s repossession of the aircraft and his subsequent efforts to dispose of it were taken on Cupeiro’s account, rather than his own, Baron’s damages are limited to $15,000, the one month’s rent due before Baron terminated the lease." (at 371)
- In a footnote, on the statute of frauds: "Our disposition of the cause makes it unnecessary to resolve the difficult question of whether the oral contract which, because it was of two years duration is clearly subject to the statute of frauds" (at 371)

## Related

- Quotes at length on dealings between attorney and client Gerlach v. Donnelly, 98 So. 2d 493 (not in `cases/`): "Gerlach v. Donnelly, 98 So.2d 493, 498 (Fla.1957)" (at 371)
- Cites, in a footnote, on the measure of damages Wolf v. Buchman, 425 So. 2d 182 (not in `cases/`): "Wolf v. Buchman, 425 So.2d 182 (Fla. 3d DCA 1983)." (at 371)

## Disposition

"Accordingly, we conclude that the judgment below must be reversed and the cause remanded to dismiss the complaint." (at 371)
