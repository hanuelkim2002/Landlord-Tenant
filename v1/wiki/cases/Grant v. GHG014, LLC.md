# Grant v. GHG014, LLC

Fourth District, Judge Warner writing, on the appeal of three people who said they were tenants from an order denying their motion for a mandatory injunction to put them back in possession of an apartment. They alleged a lockout under 83.67(2); the putative landlord denied that any tenancy existed.

## Facts

- "The appellants, who allege they were tenants of the appellees, appeal the trial court’s order denying their motion for a mandatory injunction." (at 1066)
- "They sought the immediate possession of a residential apartment, contending that the appellees violated section 83.67(2), Florida Statutes, by changing the locks of the apartment." (at 1066)
- "Appellants filed for an injunction a day after filing their complaint alleging their entitlement to possession of the premises." (at 1067)
- "The putative landlord and its agent filed an answer denying that they had entered into a landlord-tenant relationship with the appellants." (at 1067)
- "The court held an evidentiary hearing on the request for injunction and afterwards determined that appellants had not proved the existence of a tenancy." (at 1067)

## Was a motion for immediate possession, made before the case was at issue, a request for a temporary mandatory injunction?

Topic: [[Self-help and wrongful eviction]]

Rule:
- "The court considered the requested relief as a motion for temporary injunction, because trial had not been set. We agree with the court’s assessment." (at 1067)
- "As appellants were seeking a mandatory injunction before a final hearing, the relief they were seeking is properly categorized as a “temporary mandatory injunction.”" (at 1067)

Reasoning:
- "The motion for injunction could not be considered one for a permanent injunction, because the motion was made before the cause was at issue." (at 1067)

## Did the trial court abuse its discretion in denying the injunction because a landlord-tenant relationship had not been proved?

Topic: [[Whether there is a tenancy]], [[Self-help and wrongful eviction]]

The issue in the court's words: "The threshold question for purposes of issuing the injunction was whether a landlord-tenant relationship ever came into existence." (at 1067)

Rule:
- "The tenants had the burden to establish their claim by evidence “clear and free from reasonable doubt,” which they did not do." (at 1067)
- "The evidence of the relationship was controverted, and it was within the trial court’s discretion to weigh the credibility of the witnesses. No abuse of discretion has been demonstrated." (at 1067)

Reasoning:
- "Mandatory injunctions, which compel an affirmative action by the party enjoined, are looked upon with disfavor, and the courts are even more reluctant to issue them than prohibitory ones." (at 1067)
- The court quoted Spradley v. Old Harmony Baptist Church, 721 So. 2d 735 (Fla. 1st DCA 1998): "Issuance of mandatory injunctions before final hearing is disfavored and should be granted only in ‘rare cases where the right is clear and free from reasonable doubt.’" (at 1067)
- "A party seeking a preliminary or temporary injunction must establish that: (1) irreparable injury will result if the injunction is not granted, (2) there is no adequate remedy at law, (3) the party has a clear legal right to the requested relief, and (4) the public interest will be served by the temporary injunction." (at 1067)

## Disposition

"The trial court considered their request as one for a temporary injunction and denied the same, finding that they had not proved by clear and convincing evidence that a landlord-tenant relationship had been created. We affirm." (at 1067)
