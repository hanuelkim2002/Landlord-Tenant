# Menendez v. Palms West Condo. Ass'n

First District, Judge Padovano writing, on the appeal of a tenant shot in his apartment, and his parents, from a final summary judgment for the condominium association that leased and managed the unit and for the unit's two owning couples.

## Facts

- "On April 18, 1995, Julio heard a knock at the front door of the apartment. He opened the door and allowed two or more people to come inside." (at 59)
- "After Julio opened the door, someone in the apartment shot him in the head." (at 60)
- "The door to the apartment was equipped with an operable lock but it did not have a viewing device such as a peephole or doorscope." (at 60)

## Did the landlord and owners owe the tenant a duty to protect him from a criminal attack by a third person, given the crime rate at and near the complex?

Topic: [[Landlord's liability for criminal attacks on tenants]]

Rule:
- "To impose such a duty, the tenant must allege and prove that the landlord had actual or constructive knowledge of prior similar acts committed on invitees on the premises." (at 61)
- "While that may be true, the general incidence of crime in the area was not sufficient to put the defendants on constructive notice of a particular risk." (at 61)
- "In short, the plaintiffs failed to show that any of the prior incidents were similar to the attack in this case." (at 61)

Reasoning:
- "As we explained in Paterson, a “landowner should not be required to take precautions against a sudden attack which the landowner has no reason to anticipate.”" (at 61)
- "The record does not disclose whether any of the burglaries occurred while the victim was at home or whether any of the crimes would have been prevented by the installation of a doorscope." (at 61)

## Was the absence of a doorscope a defect or dangerous condition that the landlord had a duty to correct?

Topic: [[Landlord's liability for injuries on the premises]], [[Landlord's liability for criminal attacks on tenants]]

Rule:
- "Based on these principles, we conclude that the absence of a doorscope was not a defect or dangerous condition." (at 61)
- "This duty is limited, however, to conditions that are inherently unsafe or dangerous conditions that are not readily apparent to the tenant." (at 61)

Reasoning:
- "Unlike a faulty gas line or a defective stove, the absence of a doorscope is not inherently dangerous. If the lack of such a device exposes the tenant to some risk, it is a risk that can only be established by other evidence." (at 61)
- "Furthermore, the absence of the doorscope in the front door of the apartment was not a hidden or concealed condition. To the contrary, the physical features of the front door were as apparent to the tenant as they were to the landlord." (at 61)

## Did the Residential Landlord and Tenant Act impose a duty to install a doorscope?

Topic: [[Landlord's liability for criminal attacks on tenants]], [[Landlord's maintenance duties under 83.51]]

Rule:
- "In the present case, however, there is no evidence that the defendants violated any statutory provision relating to the security of the premises." (at 62)
- "This argument must fail, however, because the “Residential Landlord and Tenant Act”, Chapter 83, Florida Statutes, Part II, does not require a landlord to assume such a duty." (at 62)

Reasoning:
- "Whether the apartment could be locked is immaterial, because the individuals at the door were allowed inside. In any event, the apartment was secured by an operable lock as required by statute." (at 62)
- "The duty in Paterson was based on a violation of an express provision of the statute. No such violation occurred here." (at 62)

## Could the managing association be liable for breach of the lease, or for failing to keep the common areas safe under 83.51(2)(a)?

Topic: [[Landlord's maintenance duties under 83.51]]

Rule:
- "Palms West cannot be liable on the theory that it breached the lease agreement, because the lease contained no provision that either expressly or by implication required the installation of a doorscope." (at 62)
- "Nor can Palms West be liable on the theory that it failed to maintain safe conditions in the common areas of the complex as required by section 83.51(2)(a), Florida Statutes." (at 62)

Reasoning:
- "The attack on Julio Menendez, Jr. did not occur in a common area of the Palms West complex." (at 62)

## Related

- Follows on the duty to protect against crime, and distinguishes on the statutory duty [[Paterson v. Deeb]]: "The duty in Paterson was based on a violation of an express provision of the statute. No such violation occurred here." (at 62)
- Cites for the landlord's duty to repair and quotes on the landlord's better position [[Mansur v. Eubanks]]: "In these circumstances, we cannot say that the landlord was “in a much better position than the tenant to guard against [a] dangerous condition.”" (at 62)
- Cites as an example of a landlord's duty to repair Bosket v. Broward County Housing Authority, 676 So. 2d 72 (not in `cases/`): "Likewise, in Bosket v. Broward County Housing Authority, 676 So.2d 72 (Fla. 4th DCA 1996), the court held that the landlord had a duty to provide the tenant with an operable fire extinguisher and to install readable knobs on a kitchen stove." (at 61)
- Cites as a contrasting example Fitzgerald v. Cestari, 569 So. 2d 1258 (not in `cases/`): "In contrast, the court held in Fitzgerald v. Cestari, 569 So.2d 1258 (Fla.1990), that a landlord had no duty to put decals or other markings on a sliding glass door, because a safety precaution of that nature could have been taken just as easily by the tenant." (at 61)
- Cites, with Whelan v. Dacoma Enterprises, Inc., 394 So. 2d 506 (Fla. 5th DCA 1981), on the general duty Czerwinski v. Sunrise Point Condominium, 540 So. 2d 199 (not in `cases/`): "Florida courts have held that a landlord has no general duty to protect a tenant from criminal attacks by third persons." (at 61)

## Disposition

"For these reasons we conclude that the trial court correctly entered summary judgment for the defendants." (at 62)
