# Clark v. Hiett

Second District, Acting Chief Judge Grimes, on the tenants' petition for certiorari to review a circuit court order that reversed the county court's judgment for them in the landlord's eviction suit for nonpayment of rent. The court calls it a nonresidential landlord-tenant dispute.

## Facts

- "Pursuant to a lease agreement dated January 13, 1977, respondent (Hiett) leased to petitioners (Clarks) 638 acres of land in Manatee County." (at 774)
- "In the meantime, on August 29, 1984, Hiett had filed a suit for eviction for nonpayment of rent in county court." (at 774)
- "The county court subsequently entered judgment for the Clarks because the Hietts had not served on the Clarks the three-day written notice required by section 83.20(2), Florida Statutes (1983)." (at 774)

## Did the lease's default and termination clause waive the statutory three-day notice?

Topic: [[Waiving the statutory notice in the lease]], [[Three-day notice for nonpayment of rent]]

Rule:
- "However, there is nothing in the instant lease which either expressly or impliedly waives the necessity of this notice." (at 775)
- "This provision has nothing to do with the waiver of the statutory notice." (at 775)

Reasoning:
- "The requirement of paragraph 4 to give written notice of any default other than the payment of rent is to alert the lessee to the nature of his failure of performance before putting him in default." (at 775)
- "Subsection (c) simply gives the lessor the option to institute a suit to terminate the lease." (at 775)

## Having sued for eviction in county court, did the landlord have to give the three-day notice?

Topic: [[Three-day notice for nonpayment of rent]]

Rule:
- "The three-day notice under section 83.20(2) was a condition precedent to relief." (at 775)
- "Since Hiett failed to give the three-day notice, the county court properly entered judgment against him. The circuit court departed from the essential requirements of law in holding otherwise." (at 775)

Reasoning:
- "By having brought suit for eviction in county court, Hiett became bound by the statutory provisions applicable to that proceeding." (at 775)
- "Moreover, Hiett did not purport to terminate the lease by giving notice to the Clarks but rather elected to pursue the statutory landlord and tenant proceedings." (at 775)

## Statute read

- The court quoted section 83.20(2), Florida Statutes (1983): "Where such person holds over without permission as aforesaid, after any default in the payment of rent pursuant to the agreement under which the premises are held, and 3 days’ notice in writing, requiring the payment of the rent or the possession of the premises, has been served by the person entitled to the rent on the person owing the same." (at 774)

## Related

- Cites [[Moskos v. Hand]]: "There is authority for the proposition that the three-day notice provided in section 83.20(2) can be expressly waived in the lease. Moskos v. Hand, 247 So.2d 795 (Fla. 4th DCA 1971)." (at 775)
- Distinguishes 6701 Realty, Inc. v. Deauville Enterprises Inc., 84 So. 2d 325 (not in `cases/`): "6701 Realty was a suit in circuit court, so the question of whether the lessor was required to give notice under section 83.20(2) was irrelevant." (at 775)
- Cites the earlier appeal between the same parties, Hiett v. Clark, 453 So. 2d 50 (not in `cases/`): "This court affirmed the judgment and issued its mandate on July 24, 1984. Hiett v. Clark, 453 So.2d 50 (Fla. 2d DCA 1984)." (at 774)
- [[Bell v. Kornblatt]] cites, with Moskos v. Hand this case: "See also Clark v. Hiett, 495 So.2d 773 (Fla. 2d DCA 1986)." ([[Bell v. Kornblatt]] at 114)

## Disposition

"We grant the petition for certiorari and direct that the circuit court affirm the judgment of the county court." (at 775)
