# Shell v. Foulkes

Fourth District, Judge Farmer, treating the tenant's appeal as a petition for certiorari to review a circuit court decision in its appellate capacity. The county court had entered a default against the tenant in an eviction and later dismissed his motion for relief from it; the tenant appeared for himself and the landlord did not appear.

## Facts

- "After being sued, he filed a motion to determine the amount of rent to be paid into the court registry." (at 439)
- "With no final judgment having been entered in the eviction case, he tried to appeal the default to the Circuit Court, filing two separate notices of appeal." (at 440)
- "On the first filing, Judge Moe affirmed the default; on the second filing, Judge Luzzo reversed it." (at 440)

## Could the circuit court, as an appellate court, review a county court default entered without a final judgment?

Topic: [[Appeals in eviction cases]]

Rule:
- "The Circuit Court exceeded its appellate jurisdiction." (at 440)
- "The Circuit Court should have dismissed the appeals for lack of appellate jurisdiction." (at 440)
- "To repeat, the Circuit Court lacked jurisdiction over the attempts to appeal the non-final order of the County Court entering the default. Consequently the Mandate of the Circuit Court affirming the default had no effect." (at 440)

Reasoning:
- "An order merely entering a default without a consequent final judgment is not a final order." (at 440)
- "As for general law, nothing in Chapters 26 or 83, part II, Florida Statutes, purports to give Circuit Courts appellate jurisdiction to review non-final orders merely entering a default." (at 440)

## Was the county court's dismissal of the tenant's motion for relief from the default under rule 1.540(b) appealable, and did the county court have to hear the motion?

Topic: [[Appeals in eviction cases]]

Rule:
- "This was a departure from the essential requirements of law because it is appealable as a final order." (at 440)
- "The only possible outcome for the later appeal to the Circuit Court was to reverse the dismissal and direct the County Court to consider the motion for relief on the merits." (at 440)
- "The County Court should give petitioner a hearing on the merits of his rule 1.540(b) motion and decide whether petitioner has shown any basis for relief from the default." (at 441)

Reasoning:
- In a footnote: "The County Court continued to have jurisdiction over the eviction action and should not have dismissed the motion for relief from the default." (at 441)
- In a footnote: "The appeals dealt only with whether a default was duly and regularly entered, not with whether there had been a meritorious showing under rule 1.540(b) to vacate the default." (at 441)

## Also said

- In a footnote, the court described 83.60(2): "See § 83.60(2), Fla. Stat. (2008) (failure of tenant to file motion to determine amount of rent to be paid into the registry entitles landlord to immediate default judgment; when tenant challenges amount of rent alleged tenant must furnish documentation as to the amount)." (at 440)
- In the same footnote: "Tenant raises a substantial argument that the text of the statute allows a default only if a tenant fails to file such a motion, not that documentary proof must be filed with the motion. Although we may conclude that such an argument presents a substantial basis to reverse a decision of the trial court, we refrain from actually deciding that issue herein." (at 440)
- "In so doing, the County Court is free to address the issue in the first instance as to the correct interpretation of § 83.60(2), Fla. Stat. (2008)." (at 441)

## Disposition

"Writ granted; decision quashed." (at 441)
