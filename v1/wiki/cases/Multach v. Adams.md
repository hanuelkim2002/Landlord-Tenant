# Multach v. Adams

Fourth District, Judge Walden, on the commercial landlord's appeal from a judgment for the tenant of a retail store after a non-jury trial on claims for past-due rent and damage to the building.

## Facts

- "Multach, lessor, sued Adams, lessee, for past due rent and for damages inflicted upon the demised commercial property, all under the terms of a written lease." (at 1255)
- "It was undisputed that Adams had paid no rent since October, 1980." (at 1255)
- "The evidence clearly showed that Adams placed two holes in the exterior wall, one for an air conditioner and one for a garage door." (at 1255)

## Did the landlord's refusal of the tenant's late tender of rent waive or discharge the rent owed?

Topic: [[Accepting or refusing rent]]

Rule:
- "We hold that the refusal of the tender did not constitute a waiver or a forgiveness of the debt." (at 1255)
- "Thus, Multach is entitled to judgment against Adams for past due rent without interest." (at 1255)

Reasoning:
- "As a general rule, tender of performance is not the equivalent of actual performance." (at 1255)
- "In those cases the tender of performance will not operate as a discharge of the debt nor does the refusal to accept the money tendered operate as a discharge of the debt." (at 1255)
- "However, the refusal to accept a proper tender will prevent the collection of interest or other damages because the failure to receive payment is due to the promisee’s own action." (at 1255)

## Was the tenant liable under the lease for the holes in the wall and the damaged floor?

Topic: [[Tenant's liability for damage to the premises]]

Rule:
- "Such damages were contrary to the express terms of the lease, and particularly Paragraph Twelfth." (at 1255)
- "Multach is entitled to judgment on this account." (at 1255)

Reasoning:
- "In addition, the tile floor was damaged by Adams when he used the building for a purpose contrary to the provisions of the lease." (at 1255)
- "These items were not repaired and constituted damages to the building." (at 1255)

## Did the landlord waive the lease terms on the alterations by accepting rent after them?

Topic: [[Accepting or refusing rent]]

Rule:
- "In final reference to this point concerning the changes in the building, the trial court found that Multach waived her right to enforce that portion of the written contract. We have examined the record and can find no basis for waiver." (at 1255)
- "Therefore, it is not applicable to the instant controversy." (at 1256)

Reasoning:
- "And indeed Multach did accept rent following, for instance, the installation of the garage door." (at 1256)
- "However, this statute is applicable only to the rental of a dwelling unit and mobile home lot." (at 1256)

## Statute read

- The court quoted the 1981 text of 83.56(5): "If the landlord accepts rent with actual knowledge of a noncompliance by the tenant or accepts performance by the tenant of any other provision of the rental agreement that is at variance with its provisions, ... the landlord or tenant waives his right to terminate the rental agreement or to bring a civil action for that noncompliance, but not for any subsequent or continuing noncompliance." (at 1256)

## Related

- Cites, on damage to the premises [[Stegeman v. Burger Chef Systems]]: "Multach is entitled to judgment on this account. Stegeman v. Burger Chef Systems, Inc., 374 So.2d 1130 (Fla. 1st DCA 1979)." (at 1255)
- Cites, on tender of performance Sharp v. Williams, 192 So. 476 (not in `cases/`): "See also Sharp v. Williams, 192 So. 476 (Fla. 1939); 11 Fla.Jur.2d, Contracts, § 210." (at 1255)

## Disposition

"We reverse and remand for further proceedings consistent herewith." (at 1256)
