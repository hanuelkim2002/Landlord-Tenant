# Zhang v. D.B.R. Asset Management

Third District, per curiam (Cope, Levy, and Gersten, JJ.), on the tenants' appeal from orders denying them prejudgment interest, fees, and costs after a summary judgment in a commercial landlord-tenant action.

## Facts

- "This case involves a commercial landlord-tenant action for possession and damages." (at 387)
- "However, the landlord agreed that if no violation were found, the proper amount due would be $25,459.20, but not the $50,000 construction allowance sought by the tenants. The landlord stated it did not wish to evict the tenant." (at 387)
- The trial court found: "This Court therefore finds that this case was resolved by way of a settlement. There is no prevailing party in a settled case." (at 387)

## Could the trial court find that neither side was the prevailing party for fees, after a summary judgment for the tenants that it found rested on the parties' agreement?

Topic: [[Determining the prevailing party]]

Rule:
- "Here, the trial court determined the parties had settled, and specifically found there was no prevailing party for purposes of entitlement to fees." (at 387)
- "Under these circumstances, clearly there was no abuse of discretion in the trial court’s well reasoned decision." (at 388)

Reasoning:
- "The record supports the trial court’s findings that the parties did agree to a resolution of the disputed issues. Significantly, although the tenants’ use was found not to violate the restrictive clause in the lease, the landlord did prevail in defense of the counterclaim that sought an excessive amount for the construction contribution." (at 388)
- "Simply because a party has obtained some economic benefit as a result of litigation, does not necessarily mean that party has succeeded on the major issue in the case." (at 387)

## Related

- Cites, for the significant-issues test, [[Moritz v. Hoyt Enterprises]]: "The “prevailing party” for purposes of awarding attorney’s fees is the party determined by the trial court to have prevailed on significant issues in the litigation. See Moritz v. Hoyt Enter., Inc., 604 So.2d 807 (Fla.1992)" (at 387)
- Quotes KCIN, Inc. v. Canpro Inv., Ltd., 675 So. 2d 222 (not in `cases/`): "Moreover, “an attorney’s fee award is not required each time there is litigation involving a contract providing for prevailing attorney’s fees.” KCIN, Inc. v. Canpro Inv., Ltd., 675 So.2d 222 (Fla. 2d DCA 1996)." (at 387)
- Cites Miller v. Jacobs & Goodman, P.A., 820 So. 2d 438 (not in `cases/`): "A trial court may properly determine that neither party has prevailed in a contract action under compelling circumstances. See Miller v. Jacobs & Goodman, P.A., 820 So.2d 438 (Fla. 5th DCA 2002)." (at 387)
- Cites Smith v. Adler, 596 So. 2d 696 (not in `cases/`): "In making the determination as to which party has prevailed, a trial court is to focus on the “result obtained.” See Smith v. Adler, 596 So.2d 696 (Fla. 4th DCA 1992)." (at 387)

## Disposition

"Accordingly, we affirm the orders below in all respects." (at 388)
