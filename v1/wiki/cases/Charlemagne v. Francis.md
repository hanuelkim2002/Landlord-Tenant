# Charlemagne v. Francis

Fourth District, Judge Warner writing, on the injured plaintiff's appeal from a jury verdict for the property owners in a common law negligence suit over carpet he claimed was defective. The plaintiff lived in the apartment with the tenant, who had cared for him.

## Facts

- "Bernadette Hyachinthe leased one of the appellees’ apartments and moved in one week later. She noticed immediately that the carpet was old and needed to be replaced and informed the apartment manager who promised to replace it." (at 158)
- "Approximately three weeks after moving into the apartment, appellant’s toes got stuck in the ridges of the bedroom carpet, causing him to fall and break his hip." (at 159)
- "He stated that if furniture movers had pulled the furniture across the carpet, this action could have caused the ridges, but he admitted that he had no personal knowledge regarding how the furniture was actually moved into the apartment." (at 159)

## Was it error to instruct the jury on section 83.51(4) in a suit brought only in common law negligence?

Topic: [[Conditions caused by the tenant under 83.51(4)]], [[Landlord's liability for injuries on the premises]]

Rule:
- "We hold that it was error to give the statutory exculpation of liability instruction in a common law negligence action." (at 159)
- "Section 83.51(4), which was read to the jury, is applicable only to exclude liability of the landlord under section 83.51, which describes statutory warranties." (at 160)
- "It was error to instruct the jury on an inapplicable statutory defense which could have misled the jury." (at 160)

Reasoning:
- "Although, under that section, the landlord has the duty to keep the floors in good repair, the appellant did not sue for a violation of that duty, which imposes definite obligations upon a landlord that did not exist under the common law." (at 160)
- "Instead, appellant sued only in common law negligence. Thus, the defenses available to the appellees were comparative negligence and negligence of others. These defenses would result in the allocation of responsibility between all parties found negligent, unlike the statutory exclusion of liability, which is absolute." (at 160)
- "Moreover, the appellees did not raise the statutory defense at trial in their affirmative defenses." (at 160)

## Was there evidence that persons moving furniture in with the tenant's consent caused the ridges, to support the instruction?

Topic: [[Conditions caused by the tenant under 83.51(4)]]

Rule:
- "Even if the statutory defense was applicable, there was simply no evidence presented as to the cause of the wrinkles or ridges." (at 160)
- "Therefore, it was error to instruct the jury that they could find that the condition was “created by the negligence of persons involved in the moving process who were on the premises with the tenant’s consent.”" (at 160)

Reasoning:
- "Although the carpet cleaner testified that the sliding of furniture across the carpet could have caused the ripples, there was no testimony that the furniture movers did this." (at 160)

## Did the two-issue rule bar the plaintiff from raising the instruction on appeal?

Rule:
- "We reject the appellees’ contention that application of the “two-issue rule” bars the appellant from raising this issue." (at 160)
- "In the instant case, the two-issue rule is inapplicable because the appellant claimed only one theory of liability against the appellees." (at 160)

Reasoning:
- "The supreme court further noted, however, that the two-issue rule applies to those actions which can be brought on two theories of liability, but where a single basis for damages applies." (at 160)

## Also said

- In a footnote, on the plaintiff's status: "Both parties have treated this case as one between landlord and tenant, even though appellant does not appear to have been a tenant, but instead the tenant’s invitee. He did not sign the lease and had no familial relationship with the tenant." (at 159)

## Statute read

- The court quoted the 1993 text: "The landlord is not responsible to the tenant under this section for conditions created or caused by the negligent or wrongful act or omission of the tenant, a member of his family, or other person on the premises with his consent." (at 159)
