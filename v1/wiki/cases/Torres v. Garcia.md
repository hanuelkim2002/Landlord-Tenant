# Torres v. Garcia

Third District, Judge Cope writing, on the landlord's appeal from a final judgment on a jury verdict for the tenant's sister, who fell at a party in the rented house. The jury found the landlord 75 percent and the tenant 25 percent negligent.

## Facts

- "Prior to turning over the premises, the landlord laid wall-to-wall carpet in the bedrooms and white ceramic tile in the hallway immediately outside the bedrooms. When this work was completed, the tile and the carpet were substantially level." (at 95)
- "During the three and one-half to four years that the tenant resided in the premises, the carpet at the bedroom door became somewhat pressed down as a result of the foot traffic in and out of the bedroom." (at 95)
- "Plaintiff wore high heels to the party and, in walking out of the bedroom, caught her heel on the edge of the tile and fell." (at 95)

## Was the landlord entitled to a directed verdict when the tenant never told the landlord of the pressed-down carpet?

Topic: [[Landlord's liability for injuries on the premises]]

Rule:
- "We conclude that the landlord’s motion for directed verdict should have been granted." (at 95)
- "Assuming that the pressed down condition of the carpet could be said to constitute a dangerous condition, the landlord was entitled to a directed verdict on authority of Mansur v. Eubanks, 401 So.2d 1328 (Fla.1981)." (at 95)

Reasoning:
- "The tenant did not consider this to be a problem and never complained to the landlord or asked the landlord to take any corrective action." (at 95)
- The court quoted Mansur: "After the tenant takes possession, the landlord has a continuing duty to exercise reasonable care to repair dangerous defective conditions upon notice of their existence by the tenant, unless waived by the tenant." (at 95)

## Was there a difference in level between the tile and the carpet when they were installed?

Topic: [[Landlord's liability for injuries on the premises]]

Rule:
- "Plaintiff contends, however, that there was a slight difference in level when the tile and carpet were originally installed. That argument is not supported by the evidence." (at 95)

Reasoning:
- "Both the landlord and the tenant testified that the tile and carpet were level when originally installed." (at 95)
- The court quoted Sari v. Aetna Casualty and Surety Co., 452 So. 2d 64 (Fla. 3d DCA 1984): "[T]he step-down between the tile floor of an apartment hallway and the carpeted, necessarily flexible, pile surface of the adjacent bedroom which came to the same level, and upon which the plaintiff fell, was so obvious that, as a matter of law, neither warning nor correction of the condition was required." (at 95)

## Also said

- In a footnote: "It may also bear mention that plaintiff had visited in the residence over 100 times previously, and had walked in and out of the same bedroom door numerous times without incident, and without registering any complaint with her brother or the landlord." (at 95)

"The final judgment is reversed and the cause remanded with directions to enter judgment in favor of the appellant landlord." (at 95)
