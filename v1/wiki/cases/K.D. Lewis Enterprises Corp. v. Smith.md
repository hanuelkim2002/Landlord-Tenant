# K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith

Fifth District, Judge Upchurch, on the apartment landlord's appeal from a jury verdict awarding its tenants compensatory and punitive damages for negligence, and the tenants' cross-appeal. The tenants had withheld rent; the landlord sued for possession and the tenants counterclaimed in contract and tort.

## Facts

- "The tenants withheld payment of rent claiming that the landlord did not maintain their apartments and that a rental increase was unfair." (at 1033)
- "The landlord moved to require the tenants to deposit the rent into the registry of the court pursuant to section 83.60(2), Florida Statutes (1981). When they failed to do so, the court issued writs of possession against Smith and Lang and entered a judgment of possession against Williams who had already vacated the premises." (at 1033)
- "In Lang’s case, clothes in the amount of $300 were damaged because of a leak in her closet. Williams testified she drove her car into a pothole in the parking area causing $100 in damage." (at 1037)

## Could the tenants sue as representatives of a class of all the tenants in the complex?

Topic: [[Tenants suing as a class]]

The issue in the court's words: "The first issue we consider is whether the court erred in refusing to permit the tenants to appear as representatives of a class composed of all the tenants in the complex." (at 1034)

Rule:
- "Therefore, we conclude that the trial court correctly refused to permit the tenants to appear as representatives of a class." (at 1034)

Reasoning:
- "While each tenant may have been affected by the omissions or non-compliance of the landlord, the extent, nature, and effect of such omission or non-compliance would unquestionably vary from apartment to apartment and from tenant to tenant." (at 1034)

## Did the tenants have to deposit the rent into the court registry when they also counterclaimed for damages and injunctive relief?

Topic: [[Rent paid into the court registry]]

The issue in the court's words: "The next question is whether the trial court correctly issued the writs of possession." (at 1034)

Rule:
- "Therefore, we have concluded that the trial judge was correct in issuing the writs of possession upon the refusal of Smith and Lang to deposit rent as required by the statute." (at 1035)
- "By so holding, we do not mean to imply that by failing to deposit rent, a tenant’s cause of action is lost to him. He loses only his right to retain possession of the premises if he fails to pay the rent to the landlord or into the registry of the court." (at 1035)
- "Any cause of action against the landlord to which he may be otherwise entitled is still available to him." (at 1035)

Reasoning:
- "As now framed, the landlord may recover his rent should the tenants’ suit have no legal basis. On the other hand, should the tenants’ suit have merit, the rent is not lost to them." (at 1035)
- "To follow the course suggested by the tenants would enable a devious tenant to live rent free during the litigation, if he could frame a legally sufficient pleading." (at 1035)
- "We see no more reason to expect a landlord to continue furnishing housing without rent than to expect an oil supplier to continue furnishing oil without payment during a period of litigation." (at 1035)

## Did the trial court err in directing a verdict against the tenants on their other counts?

Topic: [[Points not raised in the trial court]]

The issue in the court's words: "The next question we address is whether the court erred in directing a verdict against the tenants." (at 1036)

Rule:
- "We conclude that these claims were simply abandoned by the tenants and the error sought to be raised by the tenants was not preserved for appeal." (at 1036)

Reasoning:
- "The record, however, does not reflect that action by the court." (at 1036)
- "While this procedure was irregular, no directed verdict was entered and the counts were not considered by the jury." (at 1036)

## Could the landlord amend its answer shortly before trial to raise recoupment and waiver for lack of written notice of defects?

The issue in the court's words: "The first question we address regarding the damage award is whether the court erred in permitting the landlord to amend its answer to raise the affirmative defenses of recoupment and waiver due to a lack of written notice of defects." (at 1036)

Rule:
- "We therefore conclude that the decision was within the court’s discretion and there was no error." (at 1037)

Reasoning:
- "Amendments to pleadings are favored and should be liberally allowed in furtherance of justice and to ensure that each case is determined on its real facts." (at 1036)
- "The tenants have failed to allege, much less demonstrate, how they were prejudiced in their defense by the court’s order permitting the amendment." (at 1037)

## Could the tenants recover the lost value of their apartments in negligence without the written notice in 83.56, and did the evidence support the compensatory awards?

Topic: [[Tenant's notice of the landlord's noncompliance]]

The issue in the court's words: "The next question is whether the evidence supports a finding of compensatory damages." (at 1037)

Rule:
- "In summary, we reject the tenants’ contention that they could recover damages under a negligence theory for the diminution in value of their premises." (at 1038)
- "Of course, where a tenant or his property is injured by the negligence of the landlord, his cause of action requires no notice under the statute." (at 1038)
- "However, since there was some evidence of damage to the property of Lang and Williams, and the jury allowed a set-off in the exact amount of the compensatory damages, we affirm those awards. As to Smith, we hold that the trial court should have directed a verdict in favor of the landlord on the negligence count." (at 1038)

Reasoning:
- "We conclude, however, that the tenants are incorrect and that they are arguing a contract theory of recovery rather than tort." (at 1037)
- "To recover for diminishment in the value of the premises, it is necessary for the tenant to comply with section 83.56, Florida Statutes (1981), which provides as follows" (at 1037)
- "To accept the tenants’ argument that the notice under section 83.56 is not required because they framed their claim in negligence would defeat the legislative requirement of notice." (at 1038)

## Did the evidence support punitive damages against the landlord?

Topic: [[Punitive damages against a landlord]]

The issue in the court's words: "The final question is whether punitive damages were properly awarded." (at 1038)

Rule:
- "We have reviewed the evidence and conclude that it fails to rise to this level. Therefore, the court should have granted the landlord’s motion to set aside the verdicts on punitive damages." (at 1038)

Reasoning:
- "Punitive damages are justified where torts are committed with fraud, actual malice, or deliberate violence or oppression, or when the defendant acts wilfully, or with such gross negligence as to indicate a wanton disregard of the rights of others." (at 1038)

## Also said

- In a footnote on the tenants' partial voluntary dismissal: "Under Florida Rule of Civil Procedure 1.420, only an entire action may be voluntarily dismissed; there can be no partial dismissal and no dismissal of less than all causes of action." (at 1036)

## Statute read

- The court quoted section 83.60(1), Florida Statutes (1981): "The defense of a material noncompliance with s.83.51(1) [F.S.1973] may be raised by the tenant if 7 days have elapsed after the delivery of written notice by the tenant to the landlord as prescribed in s.83.56(4) [F.S.1973], specifying the noncompliance and indicating the intention of the tenant not to pay rent by reason thereof." (at 1034)
- And section 83.60(2): "In an action by the landlord for possession of a dwelling unit based upon nonpayment of rent, if the tenant interposes any defense other than payment, the tenant shall pay into the registry of the court, the accrued rent as alleged in the complaint or as determined by the court and the rent which accrues during the pendency of the proceeding, when due." (at 1035)
- And: "Failure of the tenant to pay the rent into the registry of the court as provided herein constitutes an absolute waiver of the tenant’s defenses other than payment, and the landlord is entitled to an immediate default." (at 1035)
- The court quoted section 83.56(1), Florida Statutes (1981): "If the landlord materially fails to comply with s.83.51(1) [F.S.1973] or material provisions of the rental agreement within 7 days after delivery of written notice by the tenant specifying the non-compliance and indicating the intention of the tenant to terminate the rental agreement by reason thereof, the tenant may terminate the rental agreement." (at 1037)

## Related

- Distinguishes [[Mansur v. Eubanks]]: "First, Mansur was not a tenant in the unit where he was injured. Second, the court merely abandoned the doctrine of caveat lessee. It did not create a new cause of action in tort for the diminution in value of the premises." (at 1038)
- Limits to its facts Freedman v. Geiger, 314 So. 2d 189 (not in `cases/`): "While this language is capable of the construction urged by the tenants, we think the holding should be limited to its facts." (at 1036)
- Cites, on the standard for punitive damages, Winn & Lovett Grocery Co. v. Archer, 126 Fla. 308 (not in `cases/`)
- [[First Hanover v. Vazquez]] follows, and quotes at length this case: "We find the same analysis applicable to the instant dispute." ([[First Hanover v. Vazquez]] at 1191)

## Disposition

"AFFIRMED in part, REVERSED in part." (at 1038)
