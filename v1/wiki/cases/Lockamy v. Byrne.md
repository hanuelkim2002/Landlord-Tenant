# Lockamy v. Byrne

First District, per curiam, affirming in one sentence a summary judgment for the landlords in the tenants' suit for a fall on steps at the rented house. Judge Ervin dissented in a written opinion.

## Facts

- From the dissent: "Mrs. Lockamy slipped on some brick stairs that led down three steps to a garage area in a house that she and her husband had rented from appellees." (at 372)
- One of the trial court's three alternative findings, as the dissent quoted them: "Assuming arguendo that the steps were defective or dangerous, the Plaintiffs herein waived the defects and dangerous condition, as evidenced by the Plaintiffs’ testimony and the lease agreement between Plaintiffs and Defendant herein." (at 373)
- Paragraph 13 of the rental agreement, as the dissent quoted it: "LIABILITY: AGENT/OWNER shall not be liable for TENANT’s personal injury or damage, or loss of TENANT’s personal property (furniture, jewelry, clothing, etc.) from theft, vandalism, fire, water, rainstorms, smoke, explosions, sonic booms, or other causes whatsoever." (at 375)

## Was summary judgment for the landlords correct on the tenants' claim for injuries from the fall on the steps?

Topic: [[Landlord's liability for injuries on the premises]], [[Exculpatory clauses in a lease]]

Rule:
- "We find that the trial court correctly granted summary judgment in favor of the landlords-appellees." (at 372)

Reasoning: none stated.

Dissent:
- "I submit that there is no requirement that the tenant give notice of any allegedly defective condition of the premises, if the landlord had actual knowledge of the condition." (at 373)
- "Additionally, after the passage of the Florida Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, Sections 83.40-.66, Florida Statutes (1973), the fact that the tenant’s knowledge is equal to that of the landlord’s can no longer be considered a bar to an action for damages." (at 373)
- "I am unprepared to say on the basis of the above testimony that the record forecloses any possible inference that the steps, at the time of Mrs. Lockamy’s fall, were not in a dangerous condition and that such condition did not contribute to her fall and subsequent injuries." (at 374)
- "In my judgment the lower court erred in failing to find as a matter of law that the rental agreement’s terms were unconscionable." (at 375)
- In a footnote to the dissent: "Nevertheless, given the strong indication of legislative disapproval of exculpatory language absolving a landlord completely from his duty to transfer a reasonably safe dwelling unit to his tenant, see Section 83.47, Florida Statutes, I would regard a clause such as the one before us as void and unenforceable." (at 375)
- "In the case at bar, the lessor was under a statutorily imposed duty to maintain the steps of the dwelling leased in good repair." (at 375)
- "I would therefore hold void and unenforceable the provision and agreement seeking to exonerate the landlord from any liability to the tenant caused by his own negligence, and would reverse the summary judgment as to all reasons stated by the trial court, and remand for further proceedings." (at 376)

## Statute read

- The dissent quoted section 83.45(1) (no year stated): "If the court as a matter of law finds a rental agreement or any provision of a rental agreement to have been unconscionable at the time it was made, the court may refuse to enforce the rental agreement, enforce the remainder of the rental agreement without the unconscionable provision, or so limit the application of any unconscionable provision as to avoid any unconscionable result." (at 375)
- The dissent quoted section 83.51(1)(b) in part (no year stated): "maintain the roofs, windows, screens, doors, floors, steps, porches, ... in good repair" (at 373)
- The dissent quoted section 83.47(1)(b) in part (no year stated): "[p]urports to limit or preclude any liability of the landlord to the tenant ... arising under law" (at 375)

## Related

- The dissent quotes it on the landlord's knowledge of a dangerous condition [[Paterson v. Deeb]]: "As we recognized in Paterson v. Deeb, 472 So.2d 1210, 1217 (Fla. 1st DCA 1985), “To state a cause of action in negligence, it should be alleged and proved that the landlord had actual or constructive knowledge of the violation said to be creating a dangerous condition for sufficient time to have it remedied." (at 373)
- The dissent quotes its construction of the Act [[Mansur v. Eubanks]]: "[T]he owner of a residential dwelling unit, who leases it to a tenant for residential purposes, has a duty to reasonably inspect the premises before allowing the tenant to take possession, and to make the repairs necessary to transfer a reasonably safe dwelling unit to the tenant unless defects are waived by the tenant." (at 373)
- The dissent relies on it and quotes it on exculpatory clauses, noting the limits of its holding [[John's Pass Seafood Co. v. Weber]]: "In John’s Pass Seafood Co. v. Weber, 369 So.2d 616 (Fla. 2d DCA 1979), the court commented that with the passage of Section 83.47, Florida Statutes (1977), the validity of an exculpatory provision for any purpose in a residential lease was in serious doubt." (at 375)
- The dissent's footnote cites it on a violation of the Act as evidence of negligence [[Bennett v. Mattison]]: "I am also aware that a landlord’s violation of the provisions of the Florida Residential Landlord and Tenant Act has been characterized as evidence of negligence." (at 375)
- The dissent likens the case to it Thompson v. Rock Springs Mobile Home Park, 413 So. 2d 1213 (not in `cases/`): "In my judgment this case is similar to the facts recited by the Fifth District Court of Appeal in Thompson v. Rock Springs Mobile Home Park, 413 So.2d 1213 (Fla. 5th DCA 1982), wherein the court concluded that the extent of the lessee’s injuries and her comparative negligence in causing them were issues which should be resolved by the jury" (at 373)

## Disposition

"We find that the trial court correctly granted summary judgment in favor of the landlords-appellees." (at 372)
