# Bennett M. Lifter, Inc. v. Varnado

Third District, Judge Ferguson writing, on the landlord's appeal from a judgment on jury verdicts for a tenant and her four-year-old son, who was burned by boiling water being carried to the bathroom while the house's water heater was out. The landlord sought judgment as a matter of law or a new trial.

## Facts

- "Louis Varnado, a four-year-old child, sustained serious bodily burns from boiling water carried in a pot by his grandmother when he collided with her." (at 1337)
- "At the time of the accident on Saturday, March 28, 1981, the electric hot water heater at the Varnado house had been inoperable for three days." (at 1337)
- "Although the grandmother, Mrs. Wilson, reported the problem to an employee of the defendant, no one was dispatched by the defendant to repair the heater between Wednesday afternoon and Saturday." (at 1337)

## Did the county housing code impose a duty on the landlord to provide hot water, and was its violation properly put to the jury as evidence of negligence?

Topic: [[Landlord's liability for injuries on the premises]]

Rule:
- "Addressing first defendant’s contention regarding lack of a duty, we affirm the trial court’s implicit determination that the minimum housing standards set forth in section 17–23, Metropolitan Dade County Code imposed a duty upon the landlord to provide hot running water in every basin, bathtub or shower." (at 1338)
- "The court properly left the matter to the trier of fact when it instructed the jury that violation of the code could be considered as evidence of negligence." (at 1338)

Reasoning:
- "Although the question whether a plaintiff is within the class which the ordinance was intended to protect is a question of fact, it cannot be disputed here that plaintiffs, as tenants, were within the protected class." (at 1338)
- The court quoted Enis v. Ba-Call Building Corp., 639 F.2d 359 (7th Cir. 1980): "[I]n personal injury cases where liability is grounded in a statute or ordinance violation, questions of whether a plaintiff comes within the class of persons intended to be protected by the statute or ordinance and whether the injury is of the kind generally intended to be prevented have been dealt with in terms of proximate cause and, as such, are subject to the determinations of the triers of fact." (at 1338)

## Should proximate cause have been taken from the jury and decided for the landlord as a matter of law?

Topic: [[Proximate cause of injuries on the premises]]

The issue in the court's words: "The main issue is whether the question of proximate cause should have been taken away from the jury and decided as a matter of law." (at 1337)

Rule:
- "It cannot be said in this case that the harm which resulted from the defendant’s action or omission is so bizarre or has happened so infrequently that “in the field of human experience” it could not have been reasonably anticipated." (at 1339)
- "Defendant has not demonstrated that the trial court committed error in submitting the proximate cause issue to the trier of fact." (at 1340)

Reasoning:
- "The intervention of independent intervening causes does not break the causal connection if the intervention of such forces was itself probable or foreseeable." (at 1339)
- "Actionable negligence may exist not only where a loss results directly from another’s negligence but also where the loss or injury, although not direct, results as a consequence so natural and ordinary as to be regarded as probable." (at 1339)
- "More important, given the nature of the proximate cause question in this case, the wiser course was to submit the question to the jury “to decide using their common sense upon appropriate instructions.”" (at 1340)

## Was it error to give a concurring cause instruction?

Topic: [[Proximate cause of injuries on the premises]]

Rule:
- "We disagree with defendant that it was error to give a concurring cause instruction." (at 1340)

Reasoning:
- "Both acts, however, occurred at the same time as defendant’s continuing act of failing to provide hot water." (at 1340)
- "Further the jury may have found, by its general verdict, that those acts were forces stimulated as a natural and ordinary response to the defendant’s omission, and as such were not intervening causes independent of defendant’s original tortious act." (at 1340)

## Was it an abuse of discretion to refuse the jury's request to reread the legal cause instructions?

Rule:
- "Neither is there a clear showing that the trial court abused its discretion in refusing to comply with the jury’s request that the instructions defining legal cause be reread." (at 1340)

Reasoning:
- "In this case the request for a rereading of the lengthy instructions came before deliberations actually commenced, as an apparent preparatory act, so there is not the slightest evidence of confusion." (at 1340)

## Does Standard Civil Jury Instruction 3.5(i), on a landlord's failure to repair a dangerous or defective condition after notice, depart from Mansur v. Eubanks?

Topic: [[Landlord's liability for injuries on the premises]], [[Landlord's maintenance duties under 83.51]]

Rule:
- "The revised instruction thus comports with the spirit of Mansur." (at 1341)
- "None of the procedural issues raised as grounds for a new trial require reversal." (at 1341)

Reasoning:
- "While Mansur addressed a condition which was both defective and dangerous, it did not purport to exonerate the landlord from responsibility for faulty injury-producing conditions which are not inherently dangerous." (at 1341)
- "The duty, noted the court, corresponds to the statutory warranty of habitability, found in section 83.51, Florida Statutes (1983), which requires a landlord to provide hot running water." (at 1341)
- "Further we must assume that the supreme court meant precisely what it said in adopting and promulgating the instruction." (at 1341)
