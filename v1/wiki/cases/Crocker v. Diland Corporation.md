# Crocker v. Diland Corporation

Fifth District, Judge Griffin writing, on a golf professional's petition for certiorari to review a circuit court order denying his motion for default on his counterclaim for unlawful entry under chapter 82. The parties were a country club and its golf professional, not a landlord and a tenant, but the court discusses county court eviction cases on the same summary procedure statute.

## Facts

- "Diland owned and operated Village Greens Country Club and Golf Course (the “Club”) and employed Crocker as the golf professional." (at 1097)
- "Count I of the counterclaim was an action for unlawful entry pursuant to Chapter 82, Florida Statutes. Crocker alleged that Diland unlawfully entered and unlawfully detained possession of the pro shop." (at 1097)
- "Diland filed nothing until July 31. On that date, Diland filed a “Motion to Dismiss Amended Counterclaim” seeking to dismiss the unlawful entry count and seven other counts for failure to state a cause of action." (at 1097)

## Under the summary procedure of section 51.011, does a motion to dismiss for failure to state a cause of action toll the time to answer?

Topic: [[Eviction procedure]]

Rule:
- "In short, we conclude that Diland was wrong in relying on its motion to dismiss to toll the time to file its answer. It was obliged to file all its defenses, including failure to state a cause of action, within five days and by failing to do so was exposed to entry of a default." (at 1100)
- "As in Gonzalez, there is nothing in rule 1.140 or elsewhere in the rules of civil procedure that specifically supersedes this critical element of the statutory summary procedure. Accordingly, the statute controls." (at 1099)

Reasoning:
- "There is no option in the summary procedure to file certain defenses by motion as is authorized by rule 1.140; all defenses (which would necessarily include the defense of failure to state a cause of action, see rule 1.140) must be filed within five days." (at 1099)
- "Because section 51.011(1) does not give a defendant the option to assert by motion the defense of failure to state a cause of action, the tolling that occurs upon the filing of an authorized rule 1.140 motion can never be triggered. In this way, the purpose of the statute, which is to expedite the legal process in such cases, is met." (at 1099)
- "As the present case illustrates, the tolling of these deadlines to allow ordinary 1.140 motion practice largely emasculates the summary procedure." (at 1099)

## Was the counterclaimant entitled to a default when the other side filed only a motion to dismiss, after five days but before the default hearing?

Topic: [[Eviction procedure]]

Rule:
- "Although what was filed was denominated a “motion to dismiss”, not an answer, it did contain the statement of a defense of law or fact. In essence, Diland filed a defective answer." (at 1100)
- "Diland did plead or otherwise defend prior to the hearing on the default motion. Unless given leave to amend by the trial court, however, it would be that single statement of defenses on which Diland would be obliged to defend at the expedited trial." (at 1100)
- "Having thus concluded that Crocker was not entitled to a default against Diland, we conclude the order under review is not subject to issuance of a writ of certiorari" (at 1100)

Reasoning:
- "Under the rule governing defaults, if a party who has appeared fails to timely plead, but does plead or otherwise defend before the hearing on the motion for default, a default is improper." (at 1100)

## Also said

- On defensive motions under 51.011: "Diland is correct that section 51.011 does contemplate that defensive motions may be filed in summary proceedings in addition to an answer. The difference contemplated by the legislature between a “defense of law or fact” and a “defensive motion” is unclear." (at 1099)
- "Most likely, the defensive motions contemplated by the statute are those that raise procedural issues, such as a motion to quash, the example given in the statute. We are not faced with this type of motion in the present case." (at 1100)
- In a footnote: "Having found no departure from the essential requirements of law, we do not need to reach the question whether the harm to petitioner would be irremediable on appeal." (at 1100)

## Statute read

- The court quoted the 1989 text of 51.011: "If there is a difference between the time period prescribed in a rule and in this section, this section governs." (at 1098)
- And of 51.011(1): "All defenses of law or fact shall be contained in defendant’s answer which shall be filed within 5 days after service of process." (at 1098)
- "No other pleadings are permitted. All defensive motions, including motions to quash, shall be heard by the court prior to trial." (at 1098)
