# Philpot v. Bouchelle

First District, Judge Ervin writing, on the lessor's appeal from a judgment on a jury verdict finding him in breach of a lease with an option to purchase his condominium unit. He argued his motions for directed verdict should have been granted.

## Facts

- "As to the merits of the appeal, the record reveals that on or about June 10, 1979, the parties entered into a 12-month lease agreement providing for the rental of Philpot’s condominium unit, which agreement, by its terms, extended from June 15, 1979 to June 14, 1980." (at 1342)
- Paragraph 15 of the contract: "The rights of the lessor under the foregoing shall be cumulative and the failure on the part of the lessor to exercise properly any rights given hereunder shall not operate to forfeit any of the said rights." (at 1343)
- "Although he admitted that his rent payments and the payment on the note had been late, he stated that the appellant had accepted all of his late payments “graciously.”" (at 1343)

## Did the lessor preserve the directed verdict issue for review?

Topic: [[Points not raised in the trial court]]

Rule:
- "Therefore, the issue has been preserved." (at 1342)

Reasoning:
- "The record reveals that the appellant made a motion for directed verdict both at the close of the appellee’s case-in-chief and at the close of the case. Additionally, he renewed his motion at the charge conference after all the the evidence had been presented." (at 1342)

## Did the lessor waive the conditions of the option by accepting late rent and the improvements, given the lease's non-waiver clause?

Topic: [[Accepting or refusing rent]], [[Interpreting the lease]]

Rule:
- "We consider that the parties contractually modified the common law rules of waiver and estoppel, and their modification does not conflict with any public policy." (at 1344)
- "To conclude, because Philpot acted consistently with paragraph 15 of the contract, he cannot be deemed to have waived the appellee’s failure of performance." (at 1345)
- "Therefore, Philpot was correct in his position that the conditions of the option to purchase had not been satisfied. Accordingly, the trial court should have granted Philpot’s motion for directed verdict as he was not in breach of contract." (at 1345)

Reasoning:
- "The difficulty in applying the above rule to the case before us is that the parties contractually agreed in paragraph 15 that the acceptance of late performance by the lessor would not constitute a waiver of the lessor’s rights. Thus, the lessor, by accepting appellee’s late performance, can hardly be considered as dealing inconsistently with the terms of the contract." (at 1344)
- "Similarly, in the instant case, the appellant/lessor did not expressly waive any of his rights, and the only substantial evidence of implied waiver was his acceptance of late rental payments, and acceptance of improvements to the condominium unit-actions which were not inconsistent with the language of paragraph 15 of the contract." (at 1344)
- "Other jurisdictions, however, have upheld the validity of clauses similar to those stated in paragraph 15." (at 1344)

## Also said

- "If not for paragraph 15 of the parties’ contract, we would agree that the issue as to whether the appellant waived the conditions necessary to the exercise of the option was a jury question and that there was competent, substantial evidence supporting the jury’s decision." (at 1343)
- "Because of our holding on the above point, appellant’s third point concerning rejected jury instructions is now moot." (at 1345)

(at 1344)

## Disposition

"Reversed and remanded for further consistent proceedings." (at 1345)
