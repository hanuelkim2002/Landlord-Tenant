# Berwick Corp. v. Kleinginna Investment Corp.

Third District, Chief Judge Pearson writing, on the landlord's appeal from a non-jury judgment returning a motel tenant's security deposit less certain charges. The tenant cross-assigned as error the denial of its damages.

## Facts

- "The Berwick Corporation was the landlord of the Kleinginna Investment Corporation by virtue of a written lease upon an ocean front motel." (at 685)
- "Because of this condition, the entire third floor was rendered uninhabitable; the walls were stained; a musty odor pervaded the motel and the air conditioning was not operative during the Christmas holiday period." (at 687)
- The trial judge found: "That on January 2, 1958, plaintiff-lessee closed the doors and erected a sign thereon with the legend ‘Closed—water damage’ and vacated the premises." (at 686)

## Was there evidence of a constructive eviction, though the motel stayed open and always had guests?

Topic: [[Constructive eviction]]

The issue in the court's words: "Appellant-landlord’s first point is that the chancellor erred in finding a constructive eviction." (at 686)

Rule:
- "There is an adequate basis for this finding." (at 687)

Reasoning:
- "During all of this time some water was admitted into the motel, and because of exceptionally heavy rainfall large quantities of water were admitted during the last eight days of December and the 1st day of January." (at 687)
- "In addition, water invaded the lobby and other public rooms of the motel." (at 687)
- "There is some evidence in the record that these conditions were occasioned by causes other than the faulty roof and the delayed repair thereof; but the trial judge found that by reason of the failure of the lessor to keep the roof sound and watertight, the leased premises in substantial part were rendered unuseable." (at 687)

## Must the damage be permanent to amount to a constructive eviction?

Topic: [[Constructive eviction]]

Rule:
- "In the light of this definition, we believe that the court correctly held the damages described were sufficient to constitute a constructive eviction. Appellant’s contention that the damages were not sufficient for eviction because they were not permanent in character is not tenable." (at 687)
- "We take the meaning of ‘permanent’ as used in this definition to be that the deprivation of use may not be of short duration." (at 687)

Reasoning:
- "This is so because a holding that repairable damage is not sufficient to constitute constructive eviction would mean that there could be no such result for less than total destruction of the premises." (at 687)
- "In the language of the Supreme Court of Florida, the damages must essentially interfere with the tenant’s beneficial enjoyment." (at 687)
- "Applying this construction there is evidence in this record which, if believed by the trier of fact, was sufficient to sustain a finding that the damages alleged were neither transitory nor temporary and essentially interfered with the tenant’s use of the property." (at 687)

## Did the lease clause putting the risk of water damage on the lessee bar the claim of constructive eviction?

Topic: [[Interpreting the lease]], [[Exculpatory clauses in a lease]]

Rule:
- "This provision of the lease cannot be construed to destroy the effect of the requirement that the landlord keep the roof sound and watertight." (at 688)
- "The last mentioned provision of the lease is properly construed as applying only to claims for damage to ‘property of the lessee’." (at 688)

Reasoning:
- "In construing a lease, as in other contracts, it is necessary for the court to gather the meaning of the parties from the whole instrument and not from a few lines taken here and there." (at 688)
- "Further, it is the duty of the court whereever possible to construe apparently conflicting provisions of a contract so that the provisions do not conflict." (at 688)

## Was it error to refuse to make the tenant elect between its claims?

The issue in the court's words: "Appellant’s last point claims error because the trial court refused its motion to require the plaintiff-tenant to elect upon which of its claims it would proceed." (at 688)

Rule:
- "Further, a wide discretion must be granted to the trial judge in determining whether the prosecution of the several claims in one action is prejudicial to the defendant." (at 688)

Reasoning:
- The court quoted Rule 1.8(g), Florida Rules of Civil Procedure: "A party may also state as many separate claims or defenses as he has, regardless of consistency, and whether a defense be based on legal or on equitable grounds, or on both." (at 688)

## Could the tenant recover a rebate of rent for each room made uninhabitable?

Topic: [[Tenant's damages for the landlord's breach]]

Rule:
- "The record will not sustain such a recovery for several reasons." (at 688)

Reasoning:
- "We need only point out that the rent specified by the lease was an annual rent for the entire premises and not on a daily or room basis." (at 688)
- "It is well recognized that annual rent may not be apportioned, Thompson, Real Property § 286 [2d Ed.1939], nor may rent for an entire premises be apportioned upon a room basis." (at 688)

## Did the tenant prove damages for the unamortized value of its improvements?

Topic: [[Tenant's damages for the landlord's breach]]

Rule:
- "Therefore, without passing upon the question of the recoverability of such damages in a situation such as the one before us, we hold that the trial judge correctly determined that damages for improvements made were not proved." (at 689)

Reasoning:
- "The record in this case does not afford a basis upon which the trial court could have determined what these improvements were, what was paid for them, or the proportion which was unused." (at 689)
- "It is well established that before damages may be awarded, there must be evidence authorizing or justifying the award of a definite amount." (at 689)

## Disposition

"Affirmed." (at 689)
