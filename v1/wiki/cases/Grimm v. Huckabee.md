# Grimm v. Huckabee

First District, Judge Van Nortwick, on the occupants' appeal from a default final judgment of eviction. They claimed to occupy the house under a contract to buy it, with an occupancy agreement pending closing; Judge Benton concurred in a separate opinion.

## Facts

- "By amendment to the contract, closing was extended to September 1, 2003, and the parties entered into an agreement pursuant to which appellants took occupancy of the dwelling on October 1, 2002 until closing, in return for the payment of $850 per month rent." (at 608)
- "Appellees did not appear at the scheduled closing and the purchase and sale was not consummated." (at 608)
- "On March 12, 2004, asserting that appellants were late in paying the holdover rent for March 2004, the Parkers filed a motion for default judgment of possession of property under section 83.60, which the trial court granted on March 15, 2004." (at 609)

## Could the court evict under 83.60(2) without an evidentiary hearing on whether the occupants held under a contract for sale?

Topic: [[Whether there is a tenancy]], [[Rent paid into the court registry]]

Rule:
- "Because the trial court erred in ordering eviction under section 83.60(2) without conducting an evidentiary hearing to resolve factual disputes concerning the basis of appellants’ right of occupancy and the payment of rent for March 2004, we reverse and remand for further proceedings." (at 608)
- "Similarly, here, because appellants were claiming a right of occupancy under a purchase and sales contract, an evidentiary hearing was required to determine the basis for appellants’ right of occupancy before the trial court ordered eviction." (at 610)

Reasoning:
- "We find the reasoning of Frey persuasive." (at 610)
- The court quoted Frey v. Livecchi: "However, section 83.60 does not apply when the occupancy is under a contract for sale of a dwelling unit or the property of which it is a part." (at 610)
- And: "The resolution of this factual dispute would determine whether section 83.60 is applicable." (at 610)

## Also said

- Judge Benton, concurring: "In the circumstances the Grimms allege, I agree that section 83.42(2), Florida Statutes (2003), renders the Florida Residential Landlord and Tenant Act, including section 83.60, Florida Statutes (2003), inapplicable." (at 611)
- Judge Benton, concurring: "They contend they executed these documents and occupied the property, not as tenants, but as vendees, and that Gayle Huckabee signed as vendor and (then) owner." (at 610)

## Statute read

- In a footnote, the court quoted the 2003 text: "Section 83.42(2), Florida Statutes (2003), provides that “This part does not apply to: ... [o]ccupancy under a contract of sale of a dwelling unit or the property of which it is a part.”" (at 610)
- In a footnote, the court quoted section 83.06(1), Florida Statutes (2003), in part I, on double rent: "When any tenant refuses to give up possession of the premises at the end of the tenant’s lease, the landlord, the landlord’s agent, attorney, or legal representatives, may demand of such tenant double the monthly rent" (at 609)

## Related

- Follows [[Frey v. Livecchi]]: "We find the reasoning of Frey persuasive." (at 610)
- Quotes, on double rent, Painter v. Town of Groveland, 79 So. 2d 765 (not in `cases/`): "In Painter v. Town of Groveland, 79 So.2d 765, 768 (Fla.1955), the court explained that to obtain double rent as a statutory penalty, an occupant “holding over must have been willful and without color of title, and ... if the holding over is under a bonafide claim of right based upon reasonable grounds, [occupant] cannot be held liable for the penalty.”" (at 610)
- Cites in a footnote Pensacola Wine and Spirits Distillers, Inc. v. Gator Distributors, Inc., 448 So. 2d 34 (not in `cases/`): "See also Pensacola Wine and Spirits Distillers, Inc. v. Gator Distributors, Inc., 448 So.2d 34 (Fla. 1st DCA 1984)" (at 610)

## Disposition

"Accordingly, the cause is REVERSED and REMANDED for proceedings consistent with this opinion." (at 610)
