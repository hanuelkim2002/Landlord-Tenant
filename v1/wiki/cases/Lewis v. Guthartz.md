# Lewis v. Guthartz

Florida Supreme Court (Ehrlich, J.), on a question certified by the Third District in [[Guthartz v. Lewis]].
Tenants of an FHA-insured building sued their landlord over rents and deposits above the FHA schedule.

## Facts

- "Lewis et al. (Tenants) are 132 tenants in Guthartz' (Landlord) Bay Terrace Apartments on Miami Beach." (at 222)
- "As part of the mortgage, an FHA Regulatory Agreement was recorded. Under this agreement, monthly rental rates were fixed by the FHA and security deposits limited to one month's rent." (at 222)
- "The Tenants sued, claiming inter alia, that the Landlord charged excessive rents and security deposits. They sought restitution, injunctive relief, and attorneys' fees." (at 222)
- "They also sought punitive damages based upon: illegal rent increases; illegal retention of excess security deposits; commingling of security deposits with the Landlord's own funds; intimidation and harassment of the Tenants; continual disobedience of federal regulations; repeated violations of court orders; and submissions of false statements to the FHA, which resulted in a criminal conviction in federal court for knowingly making a false statement to a federal agency." (at 222)
- "At a non-jury trial in September 1978, the Tenants prevailed. The trial judge awarded $105,828.12 actual damages to the Tenants. ... the trial court awarded the Tenants $1,000,000 in punitive damages ... The trial court also awarded attorneys' fees of $200,800." (at 223)
- "In the instant case, the Tenants' action was not bottomed on the rental agreement but rather the FHA regulatory agreement. The latter was one of the documents attached to the complaints and sued upon; it was upon the latter that the trial court imposed liability on the Landlord. No liability was urged or claimed or adjudicated against the Landlord predicated upon any obligation, covenant or promise under the rental agreements." (at 224)

## Must tenants plead and prove an independent tort to recover punitive damages for a landlord's breach of contract?

Topic: [[Punitive damages against a landlord]]

Rule:
- "We answer the certified question “yes” and approve the decision of the district court." (at 223)
- "It is now a well-settled rule in Florida that punitive damages are not recoverable in a breach of contract action, absent an accompanying independent tort." (at 223)
- "The fact that the trial court found that the Landlord acted intentionally, willfully, and outrageously as to the breach of contract does not by itself create a tort where a tort otherwise does not exist." (at 224)

Reasoning:
- "We reaffirm the rule and its underlying policy: an unwillingness to introduce uncertainty and confusion into business transactions as well as the feeling that compensatory damages as substituted performance are an adequate remedy for an aggrieved party to a breached contract." (at 223)

## Were fees recoverable under 83.48 for a suit on the FHA regulatory agreement?

Topic: [[Prevailing party fees under 83.48]]

Rule:
- "Concerning the award of attorneys' fees, we hold that they are not recoverable by statute." (at 224)
- "Section 83.48 requires the litigation to be “with respect to the rental agreement.”" (at 224)
- "In the present case, since the basis of the action is the regulatory agreement and not a rental agreement, the Tenants are afforded no rights under this statute." (at 224)

Reasoning:
- "Section 83.48 is applicable only where a landlord under a rental agreement is entitled by its terms to recover attorneys' fees from a tenant. A corresponding right is granted to the tenant if the tenant prevails." (at 224)

## Were fees recoverable under 83.49(3)(c) when the dispute was over how large a deposit could be required?

Topic: [[Fees and costs in a suit over the deposit]]

Rule:
- "Similarly, section 83.49(3)(c) is likewise not applicable because a right to a security deposit was not adjudicated." (at 224)
- "That section applies to suits instituted for the recovery of security deposits in which both the landlord and tenant claim entitlement thereto." (at 224)

Reasoning:
- "the issue that the statute addresses is the issue of a tenant's right to the return of his security deposit upon vacating the premises." (at 224)
- "In contrast, the issue in the instant litigation concerned whether or not a security deposit equal to two month's rent could be required under FHA regulations." (at 224)

## Statute read

The court quoted the 1977 text of 83.48 in a footnote: "If a rental agreement contains a provision allowing attorney's fees to the landlord when he is required to take any action to enforce the rental agreement, the court may also allow reasonable attorney's fees to the tenant when he prevails in any action by or against him with respect to the rental agreement." (at 224)

## Related

- Approves [[Guthartz v. Lewis]] on both punitive damages and fees: "The district court correctly held these two sections inapplicable." (at 224)

## Disposition

"In sum, we answer the certified question “yes” and hold that the pleading and proving of an independent tort are required in a breach of contract action in order for punitive damages to be recovered." (at 225)
