# Evaluation by Sam Patel (C_hot_water)

## In my own words

1. Did this help me? Yes, in the end, mostly because I got a letter I can actually hand to the leasing office, but I did
   not get a straight answer to my first question (how long can they leave me without hot water) and the first answer
   was so long I nearly gave up.

2. What I came for vs. what I got.
   I came for four things: how long they can leave us without hot water, whether I can get money back (rent off, or the
   rec center), whether the AC is a separate problem, and what I should actually do.
   - How long: "The statute gives no set number of days for restoring hot water." So the honest answer is that there is no
     number. I believe it, but it left me with nothing to count down.
   - Money back: it quoted a line that I "may recover the damages caused by the noncompliance", but "the statute gives no
     formula and the tool computes no amount", and whether rec center costs count, "the stored text and cases do not
     say". So basically unknown. This was the thing I cared about most, and it is the thing I got least on.
   - AC: "Your AC is separate", and my lease (Section 7.1) says they must keep the air-conditioning in good working order.
     That was clear.
   - What to do: after a lot of back and forth, a letter, by hand to the leasing office, asking for the water heater, the AC, and a
     reply about the rec center costs, with a reply-by date of October 13, 2026.
   Something I did not come for but am glad I learned: a portal request is "writing", but it does not start the legal
   7 days unless it says I intend to end the lease or not pay rent, and my lease lists hand, certified mail, or e-mail for
   notices (not the portal).

3. Ease of use.
   - Describing my problem in my own words was easy, like texting. The sheet (about 10 questions) was mostly fine and
     the suggested answers saved time. Showing me the words of my own lease (Section 7.1) at the "does your lease say the
     landlord must repair this" question was actually helpful.
   - Things that tripped me: Enter did not accept the first question; the sheet only takes one start date but I have two
     problems that started on different days (Sept 14 AC, Sept 28 hot water); "written notice" sounded like a letter
     so I answered "no" even though I had used the portal, and then the tool treated that as a fact until I corrected it
     later; the plumbing/structural question felt random; and the "eviction case" question scared me for a second.
   - The first reply was enormous: pages of old court cases about people slipping in hallways, a child with broken glass,
     a gas explosion, a rape. I skipped nearly all of it and had to scroll to the part that was for me. Even the later,
     shorter replies kept re-printing court cases and long blocks of statute text. The part written for me was
     good and plain; the blocks printed under it were not. It was a lot of text for an 18-year-old with a cold shower waiting.
   - The bits written to me were mostly in plain words, but some words were never explained: "possession", "registry of
     the court", "material noncompliance", "continuing noncompliance", "waived", "preserve / assert / escalate".
   - The letter was short, polite, and sounded like me. The "NOTE TO TENANT" under it was hard to follow
     ("ask in the chat to have this applied to your letter").

4. Trust. Mixed. I believed the parts that quoted my own lease, because I could see my lease words and check them. I
   liked that it said "not a lawyer's advice" and that it said what it did not know instead of guessing. But three times
   there was a line at the bottom like "[Check: the quote ... is not in 83.56; it is in 83.51. Check it against the
   source yourself before relying on it.]" and a tag "[not verified]" on every case plus "[Development run: some of
   these pages are not yet verified by the author.]". I do not have the source, and I don't know what I am supposed to do about that,
   so those lines made me trust it less. I feel safe acting on the letter (it just asks nicely and cites nothing
   scary) but I would not feel safe acting on anything about rent or ending my lease from this tool alone, especially
   the "not settled" parts (whether paying October rent means I gave up the right to end the lease). I would ask a real
   person before doing either.

5. What I am going to do now.
   - Print the letter, fix it by hand (cross out the two header lines "Problem started" and "Written notice date",
     since the date on top is wrong for one of the two problems), and hand it to the leasing office at 2600 SW
     Hartwell Lane.
   - Ask them to sign or stamp my copy with the date, and take a photo of it. (That was my idea; the tool only said to keep
     proof.)
   - Wait until October 13. Keep going to the rec center and keep the dates. Keep paying rent, because I do not want to say I
     will stop paying or leave: my aunt co-signed and the dorms were full.
   - Maybe call the campus legal aid or ask the student legal services office about the rec center money and the
     "ending the lease" question.
   The tool is the reason I have the letter and know to hand-deliver rather than just use the portal again. It is not
   the reason I will call someone about money, that is because it couldn't answer it.

6. Moments of confusion (quotes from the screen).
   - "No deadlines: none of the deadlines this tool computes applies to your confirmed facts." (the very first line of the answer; I
     asked how long they can leave us without hot water and it told me about "no deadlines".)
   - "You confirmed you have not delivered one" (written notice), when I had used the portal and answered "no" because I
     thought it meant a letter.
   - "Reading one: an unfixed problem is a continuing noncompliance, so paying does not waive it... Reading two: ... paying
     waives it". I did not know what to do with two readings.
   - "E-mail delivery has its own conditions (Fla. Stat. 83.505(1))" followed by a huge block about a signed addendum;
     I had to open my lease and check myself.
   - "The choice is yours; I cannot pick it for you." when I asked which of preserve, assert, and escalate to pick.
   - "[Check: the quote ... is not in 83.56 ... Check it against the source yourself before relying on it.]"
   - The sheet asking for one start date when I have two problems; it asked again on the last screen ("you confirmed:
     2026-09-28 your update says: 2026-09-14") and I just took the 14th, which makes the letter header wrong for the hot water.
   - "The 'Problem started' line still shows the date you confirmed earlier, because it comes from the sheet", and then
     nothing redrafted the letter for me.

7. What I expected and did not get.
   - A number: how many days, or how much rent off, or how much I could ask for the rec center. Not there.
   - Anything about my roommates. Kevin boils water for dishes and showers at the rec center too, and I never learned whether we should each send a
     letter, or whether the unit has a "shared" claim. Each bedroom is on its own lease, I did not know if
     that matters. The tool never said.
   - Anything about the management company other than the name on the letter: who to talk to, whether I can email the
     maintenance manager, or what to do if they ignore it. It also did not say what happens after October 13 if they do nothing, the "what's next" was missing.
   - A hotel, or my aunt (guarantor): I wondered whether my aunt gets in trouble if I stop paying rent. It was never mentioned.
   - Whether it is safe to complain to the landlord when I live there (retaliation). I said "I don't want to threaten
     them since I live here" and it did not respond to that worry.
   - A one-paragraph summary at the start: "here is what you can do today."

8. Ratings (1-5).
   - Usefulness: 3. I got a usable letter and learned the notice rules, but the money question stayed unanswered.
   - Clarity: 2. The pieces written to me were clear; the huge piles of cases and statute text, and the unexplained legal
     words, buried them.
   - Trust: 3. I trust it when it shows my lease and says it does not know; I trust it less with the "[Check: ...]" and "[not verified]" tags.
   - Ease of use: 3. The intake sheet was easy, the chat was easy to type into, but the replies are hard to read through.
   - Would recommend to a friend: 3. Yes for "get a proper letter written", with a warning that it prints a lot of legal stuff.

9. The one change that would make me use it again: start every answer with a short "what this means for you and what to do
   next" in plain words (a few lines), and put the court cases and statute text behind it where I can skip them.

  "cannot pick" among letter choices; no advice on proving delivery beyond "keep proof"; self-flagging "[Check: ...]" and "[not verified]" lines
  that I cannot act on; nothing on roommates, guarantor, retaliation, or what to do after the reply-by date.
