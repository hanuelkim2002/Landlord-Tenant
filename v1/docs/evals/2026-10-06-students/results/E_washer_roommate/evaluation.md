# Evaluation: Chris Nguyen (Unit 2215, Bedroom C), washer charge and leak

## In my own voice

**1. Did this help me?**
Kind of yes. I did not get the answer I came for (it never told me "you don't owe the $85"), but I learned things I'd never have
figured out on my own, and I walked away with a letter draft and a plan.

**2. What I came for vs. what I got.**
I came for three things: (a) not pay $85 for Devin's mistake, (b) get the washer fully fixed, (c) know if they can charge late
fees on the $85 or take it from my deposit.

- (a) The $85: I got "it turns on one lease clause and on your facts that point is not settled." Section 3.4 splits it four ways "when
  Landlord cannot determine which resident caused the damage", but Section 7.4 says I "shall pay for the repair of damage caused by Resident,
  a roommate, or their guests". It would not pick. So I still don't know if I owe it. What I did get: Devin's admission is "something you
  said here, not a confirmed fact" and the office may not know about it, and showing it might help on the $85 but could hurt me on getting the
  washer fixed.
- (b) The washer: I got a letter asking them to fix the leak with a deadline of Oct 13. And I learned my two portal requests are "a request
  for service" and "not by itself a notice" under the lease. Also an "active water leak" is an emergency and I'm supposed to call the
  after-hours line. I had no idea about either.
- (c) Late fees and deposit: I got "the late charge clause is written about rent installments" and I paid rent, so a late fee on the $85 "would not
  rest on what Section 3.2 says"; and the deposit can only be hit if I owe the $85, with a written notice within 30 days after the lease ends
  and 15 days for me to object. That's about as close to a straight answer as I got, and it's reasonably reassuring, but it's hedged.

**3. Ease of use.**
- The intake sheet was long (about 13 questions) but easy. Most were yes/no. The ones that tripped me: "Is the problem with the roof,
  windows, doors, floors... or with the plumbing?" (a washer leaks water, is that plumbing? I said no) and "Was the problem caused by you, a
  member of your household, or someone you let in?" (Devin has his own lease; I just accepted the suggested "yes" and that "yes" came back to
  bite me, the tool told me the options to end the lease or not pay rent were "closed" because of it, and I only fixed it when it came back and asked again).
- The notice date question only had room for one date, and I had two requests (Sept 15 and Oct 3).
- Replies: some were way too long. Three of the answers started with pages of court-case quotes about carpet falls, a gas explosion, a doorscope,
  commercial tenants, tagged "[not verified]". I skipped them every time and had to scroll to find the actual answer. The answers that were
  just the answer (the screenshots one, the letter one) were much better. The 5th answer, with no cases, was the best.
- The program-printed blocks (statute and lease text at the bottom) were long. I read only the lease sections that were about me (3.4, 3.2, 7.1,
  7.4, 7.2, 13.1). Having my own lease clauses quoted was actually the most useful part, since I never read the lease.
- Odd bracket lines like "[Check: the quote ... is not in 83.51. Check it against the source yourself before relying on it.]" appeared twice
  and I didn't know what to do with them.
- The reply was never "do this." It gave me options and trade-offs. I got used to it, but I wanted more steering.

**4. Trust.**
Mixed. I believed the lease quotes because they were word for word from my lease. I liked that it kept saying "I cannot say which reading holds"
and "the tool does not know the cause", it didn't pretend to be sure. That made me trust it more on the hard parts. But the second answer opened
with "I have to add a clause I had not found in my last answer, and it cuts against you", so the first answer was missing something big (7.4),
and that made me wonder what else it was missing. The "[not verified]" tags and "Check it yourself" lines also made me not want to lean on the case
stuff. I'd feel OK sending the repair letter (low risk), not OK relying on it alone for the $85 or the deposit. And it keeps saying "not a lawyer's advice".

**6. Moments of confusion (quotes).**
- "Part of what you described may not be covered by this tool yet (it has no playbook for it). The tool continues with what it does cover."
  Which part? It never said. I assumed the $85 dispute, which was my main thing.
- "Showing the office Devin's messages may not stop the split. I have to add a clause I had not found in my last answer, and it cuts against you."
- "The two options the tool checks for the washer, ending the lease and not paying rent, are both closed on your facts, because you confirmed the problem
  was caused on your side." I never wanted either of those, and my "yes" to the household question was kind of a throwaway.
- "The tool has no option that orders a repair." So what is this tool for if I just want the washer fixed?
- The paragraph about whether the lease's email line is "void" under 83.47(1)(a): I didn't understand it. I just chose hand delivery.
- In the letter: "Written notice date: September 15, 2026" even though it told me the Sept 15 portal request wasn't a notice. And "sent by by hand".
- The bracket "[NOTE TO TENANT ... ask in the chat to have this applied to your letter before you send it.]" "Applied" how? It sounded like I did something wrong.

**7. What I expected and did not get.**
- A clear "you owe it / you don't owe it" on the $85, or at least which way it leans.
- Anything on how to get Devin to pay me back. The tool said "The lease clauses I found do not say how roommates settle up with each other afterward." It
  couldn't help with the roommate part at all, but that was the real source of my problem.
- Anything about the management company's behavior: splitting four ways with no proof, ignoring my Oct 3 request. No option to dispute a charge ("The tool found no procedure in the lease's text for disputing a charge").
- A way to make them fix the washer short of ending the lease or withholding rent. I only got "request" and "damages" (money).
- Any money back for the soaked stuff or a rent reduction for an unusable washer. Damages was mentioned only in one line.
- A way to save or print the letter. It only appeared on screen.
- A short summary at the end: "here's what to do, in order."

**8. Ratings (1 to 5).**
- Usefulness: 3. The notice/portal point, the screenshot warning, and the late fee reading were worth it; it never settled the $85.
- Clarity: 2. The case dumps and long statute blocks hid the answers, and the answers hedged.
- Trust: 3. Honest about what it did not know, but it missed a clause the first time and flagged unverified material.
- Ease of use: 3. The sheet was easy; the replies took work to read.
- Would recommend to a friend: 3. I'd say "it's good if you'll scroll past the case stuff and want to know what your lease actually says."

**9. The one change that would make me use it again.**
Put a short "what this means for you and what to do next" at the top of every answer, and move the court-case quotes (and the long statute text)
to the bottom behind "show cases" or something.

## Short plain summary

- **Helped?** Partly. The tool gave a clear, usable letter and several new facts, but no verdict on the $85.
- **Best parts:** printing my own lease clauses; learning that portal requests are not legal notices; the warning that the Devin screenshots could cut against the repair; the honest "not settled" answers; the late-fee reading (the late charge clause is about rent); the last two replies, which were short.
- **Worst parts:** the pages of case quotes ("[not verified]") about unrelated facts; long statute blocks; the first answer missing Lease 7.4; the odd "[Check: ...]" lines; the "closed options" caused by a rushed "yes" on the household question; the letter's "by by" typo and "Written notice date: September 15, 2026" line; no help with Devin or a dispute option.
- **Biggest gaps:** no answer on whether the $85 is owed; nothing on getting a roommate to pay back; no way to compel a repair; no save/print for the letter; the "not covered by this tool yet" notice never said which part.
