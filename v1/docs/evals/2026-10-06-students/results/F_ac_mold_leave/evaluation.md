# Evaluation by Morgan Davis (nursing junior, Unit 3110, Bedroom A, Magnolia Row)

(in my own voice first, then a plain summary)

## 1. Did this help me?
Kind of yes: I got a letter I can actually hand in and I finally understand my options, but I still do not know if I owe
$1,578, $7,000, or nothing, and a lot of it was too much to read while my chest is tight.

## 2. What I came for vs. what I got
I came to find out how to get out of my apartment ASAP without paying a huge fee and without my mom getting stuck with a bill.
What I got:
- A list of "ways out," each marked "not settled" or "not known yet."
- Good news on the fee: I confirmed I did not sign a separate addendum, so "the landlord cannot charge a fee under that section."
  But my lease has its own $1,578 early termination option (Section 12.1) and a case "cuts against you," so that is "not settled."
- A clear NO on one thing: "Moving out and simply stopping rent is not a way out the statute gives."
- A number I did not know: my security deposit is $300, and the 7-day notice before leaving matters for it.
- A letter, dated Oct 6, with my reasons, the Student Health note, what I am asking for, the Oct 9 reply date, my mom's address for the
  deposit, sent by hand and email. After one fix it reads normally.
- NOT got: anything real about my mom. Three times: "The tool has not read it [the guaranty]". Nothing about what she would owe.
- NOT got: a "probably" or a "do this first." Over and over, "not settled," "the tool cannot say," "that is yours to decide."

## 3. Ease of use
- Intake sheet: about 20 questions. Most had a suggested answer so I just hit Enter. That part was easy. A few I did not understand:
  the "structural part of the building, or with the plumbing" question (my AC leaks water, is that plumbing?), the "separate addendum choosing
  to pay liquidated damages" question (I had never heard of that), and "written notice delivered" (which one, the portal
  requests or the email?). "asap" was not accepted as a move-out date. It asked for the lease end date twice before I
  looked it up.
- The replies: way too long. The first answer was many screens. Before the actual answer there were big blocks of court cases in
  the courts' "own words," with "Facts / Rule / Reasoning," some about falls, a gas explosion, a scalding, and the tool itself
  said none of them were about ending a lease. I skipped all that every time. The bits in plain words were good, mostly the "Short
  version first" and "Sorry I skipped those. Here they are in plain words." I wish every answer started like that.
- Printed blocks: the "Deadlines, computed by the tool" block was my favorite part. Real dates (Oct 3 and Oct 4, "passed") made it
  feel real. The lease and statute quotes were useful when short, not when the statute was a full paragraph.
- The weird "[Check: the quote ... is not in 83.xx. Check it against the source yourself before relying on it.]" lines at the bottom: I
  never understood them and they made me nervous.
- The letter: quick and good once it was asked for properly. It needed 6 things from me first (which letter, what I want, days to reply,
  how I send it, "posture", my address) and the "posture" question (preserve / assert / escalate) confused me and it would not tell
  me which to choose.

## 4. Trust
I trusted it more than a random website because it quoted my actual lease and said "not a lawyer's advice" and "the tool cannot say" instead
of pretending. It kept saying what it did not know, and that felt honest. But I did not feel safe acting on it alone for two reasons:
(1) the "[Check: ...]" lines suggested it got quotes wrong, and (2) the first draft of the letter had a garbled first sentence and "by by", which
makes me wonder what else is off. I would show the letter to someone at UF student legal services before relying on it. I do trust the
letter's facts and dates because they came from what I told it.

## 5. What I am going to do now
- Print the letter, hand it to the leasing office tomorrow (get a date/name or a stamp as proof), and also email it to office@magnoliarow.example.
- Text my mom to find the guaranty she signed and look through my move-in papers for any separate page about an early termination fee.
- Call UF Student Legal Services and probably the city/county code enforcement office about the mold and AC (the tool mentioned that).
- Probably still move out Oct 11 and risk the $300, since my asthma is worse each day.
- The tool is the reason I have the letter and know about the 7-day notice and the fee options. The calls and the move-out date are my own plan.

## 6. Moments of confusion
- "Is the problem with the roof, windows, doors, floors, steps, porches, exterior walls, foundation, or another structural part of the building,
  or with the plumbing?" No idea why it was asking.
- "Does your written notice say that you intend to end the lease because of the problem?" My email said "I'm going to have to move out" "if this
  isn't fixed."
- "If you did not, this route is closed under the statute." Is "closed" good or bad? I had to ask. (Answer: good for me on the fee.)
- "'closed' does not mean you owe nothing." Then the lease's own $1,578 option "cuts against you." I went up and down in one answer.
- "One reading... Other reading..." about whether paying October rent ruined my chance. I do not know which one to believe, and it said it is
  "for a court to settle, not something you can fix tomorrow."
- "The program's deadlines show the last day to give the 7-day notice before leaving has passed." I had not thought about the date I typed.
- "The tool does not pick one for you" on the letter posture. I asked "which is best for me?" and got no answer.
- The second letter printed again with no answer to my two questions. I had to ask a second time.
- "[Check: the quote 'I am ending my lease because...' (83.56(1)) is not in 83.56...]" It is my own letter. Why is it checking my own words?

## 7. What I expected and did not get
- My mom: what she would owe as guarantor. Nothing except "look at the guaranty."
- Money: no dollar estimate of the worst case. It would not confirm or deny my $7,000 figure.
- The management company: no idea what Dana or Hartwell will probably do, or whether the reassignment ($250) is a realistic route, only that it needs their approval.
- Roommates: nothing (not that I asked), though my unit has three other leases.
- Housing/medical help: nothing about a medical or accommodation request through UF, or the doctor's note being used for a different housing assignment. The Student Health note was in
  the letter but the tool never said how much it helps.
- Where to get a human: it said "not a lawyer's advice" but never named UF Student Legal Services or any free help.
- A simple "do these 3 things" list at the end. I had to build it myself.

## 8. Ratings (1-5)
- Usefulness: 4. I got a letter and the main options, but no clear "bottom line."
- Clarity: 2. The plain-words parts were good, but there were pages of court cases and "readings" on top of them.
- Trust: 3. It was honest about what it did not know, but the "[Check:...]" lines and the garbled first draft worried me.
- Ease of use: 3. The sheet was easy; the chat replies were way too long and I had to ask twice for answers.
- Would recommend to a friend: 3. Yes for the letter and the deadlines, with a warning to skip the case pages.

## 9. The one change that would make me use it again
Start every answer with a short "what to do next" list in plain words, and put the court case pages and quote-check lines
somewhere I can open only if I want them.
