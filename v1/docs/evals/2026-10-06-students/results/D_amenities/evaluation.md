# Evaluation by Taylor Brooks (D_amenities)

Note: the program ended by itself after one yes/no question. I never reached the chat prompt, so I sent zero chat messages.

## In my voice

1. Did this help me? Barely. It stopped me after one question and mostly just handed me three paragraphs of my own lease.

2. What I came for vs. what I got. I came to find out if I can get part of my $150 amenity fee and/or a Wi-Fi credit, whether
the ads (resort pool, 24-hour gym, "blazing fast Wi-Fi") count for anything, and whether the 40 of us with the petition can do
something together. I got: a list of ten situations the tool does handle (none is mine), and three lease sections quoted back
to me. Two of them (6.2 and 3.3) say, basically, "no refund for closed amenities, ever". The third (5.1) says Wi-Fi is
included in rent. Then: "This tool has no playbook for this situation, so it does not apply them to your facts." So I got
no answer to any of my three questions.

3. Ease of use. Starting was easy: it set everything up and asked me to describe the problem in my own words, which felt
natural. The "sheet" was one question: "This looks like: Something in the unit needs repair and the landlord has not fixed
it. Is that part of your situation? (yes/no)". I answered no because my unit works fine, only the pool, gym, study rooms
and Wi-Fi are broken. That "no" ended the whole thing ("Taken out of your matter; logged ... so it stops here"). Nothing
warned me my answer would end the session, so it felt like I failed a quiz I did not know I was taking. The ten-item
list was readable and the quotes were clear, with section number and page. But the tool never explained what the quoted
clauses mean, or whether a lease can really say that.

4. Trust. I trust the lease quotes since they show section and page. I do not trust it as a legal helper: it said it
"may not be covered" at the start, then asked me a question that did not fit, and then quit. Showing me the two "no refund"
lines with nothing next to them made me think it was telling me I'm out of luck, but it also said it was not applying them.
I would not act on anything beyond "my lease has these three clauses".

5. What I do now. (a) Email the leasing office, in writing, about the Wi-Fi, citing Section 5.1 (the tool is why I know
that section says Wi-Fi is included). (b) Ask the UF student legal services / a real person whether the "no refund" clauses
and the ads matter. (c) Keep the petition and collect the portal tickets and dates. The tool is only the reason for (a).

6. Moments of confusion.
- "Something in the unit needs repair and the landlord has not fixed it. Is that part of your situation?" Is the Wi-Fi in my
  unit? The pool is not. I did not know which way to answer, and I did not know the answer mattered so much.
- "Taken out of your matter; logged." What got taken out, what does logged mean?
- "None of the situations this tool covers applies, so it stops here." Then "open this matter again (choose existing) and
  describe that part." But none of the ten parts is mine. What am I supposed to describe?
- "This tool has no playbook for this situation." Playbook? I do not know what that is.
- The first warning ("Part of what you described may not be covered by this tool yet") was good, but it did not tell me
  which part was not covered.

7. What I expected and did not get.
- Any answer on whether the ads count. Nothing about the ads at all.
- Anything on the amenity fee and the closed amenities beyond quoting the lease.
- Money back: nothing, not even "this is how you ask for it".
- Anything on the petition or the other residents doing something together, and on roommates / the management company.
- Any hint of what to do next, or where to go (legal aid, the leasing office, a different kind of help).
- Wi-Fi: the one clause that helps me (5.1) was listed with the other two and not explained.
- A chance to ask a follow-up question. It just ended.

8. Ratings (1-5)
- Usefulness: 1. No answer to my questions; the lease quotes are the only value.
- Clarity: 2. The messages were readable, but the jargon (playbook, matter, logged) and the one yes/no question were unclear.
- Trust: 2. The quotes are sourced, but I would not rely on the tool for anything else.
- Ease of use: 3. Easy to start, but one wrong guess ended it and I could not go back.
- Would recommend to a friend: 1. For a problem like mine, no. I would tell them it does deposits, notices and repairs.

9. The one change: do not stop the conversation. If my problem is outside the ten situations, still let me chat and
tell me plainly what my lease says, what I can try (write the office, ask legal aid), and what the tool cannot tell me.

## Plain summary for the tool's author

- The session ended at the first sheet question. The tool suggested "[repair_not_made] Something in the unit needs repair
  and the landlord has not fixed it" from my description; I answered "no" (unit fine, only amenities and Wi-Fi are broken).
  The tool then said none of its situations applied and stopped (DONE). The chat was never reached.
- Nothing on screen warned that "no" would end the session, and this was the only question, so a user with an
  out-of-scope problem like amenities, ads or a group refund gets a dead end after one yes/no.
- Scope gap: amenity closures, advertised amenities, Wi-Fi/services included in rent, partial fee or rent refund, and a
  group petition by residents are not covered.
- What worked: easy setup, an honest up-front "may not be covered", and sourced lease quotes (6.2, 3.3, 5.1) with section
  and page. The program-printed lease quotes were the best part.
- Problems: the dead end, the unexplained jargon (playbook, matter, "Taken out of your matter; logged"), the
  quotes without any explanation, no next step, no follow-up chat, and the instruction to "open this matter again and
  describe that part" when no covered part fits. A student could also feel pushed to re-describe the problem as a
  "repair" just to continue.
- The tool did not assess the ads or the Wi-Fi under 5.1 at all, which were my main questions.
