# Evaluation: Alex Kim (B_ac_rent_strike)

## In my own voice

### 1. Did this help me?
Yes, in the end, but not the way I expected: it never gave me the "you can withhold rent" or "you're fine" answer I came for, it gave me a to-do list (pay, call the emergency line, send a better e-mail) after making me dig through a lot of text to find it.

### 2. What I came for vs. what I got
I came with four questions: is it legal to not pay until they fix the AC, can I get evicted, what about the late fee, and does it matter that my roommates are doing it too. And "should I just pay?"

What I got:
- Legal to not pay? "Short answer: on your facts, holding back rent is not safe, and the landlord can start an eviction over it. The law gives a tenant a defense in an eviction case, not a right to keep rent." That shot down what my friends told me. I needed to hear it.
- Can I get evicted? "Yes, that is the landlord's route for unpaid rent." It also said there is no 3-day demand in my record and no case filed, which calmed me down a little.
- Late fee: the lease says $50 plus $10 for each later day, up to $150 a month. I did not know it grew every day. That is what decided it for me. It would not say whether I could get out of it ("The lease and statute text I have do not answer that").
- Roommates: the clearest answer of the whole session. My lease is separate from theirs and says I am "not responsible for the rent of any roommate" (Section 3.4). I did not know that.
- Should I just pay? Never directly. "Whether to pay today is your choice." Twice.
- Bonus: it wrote me an e-mail to the leasing office that quotes the lease's AC promise, mentions the failed repair and the 87 degrees, and asks them to drop the late fee.

A couple were odd ("Is the problem with the roof, windows, doors, floors, steps, porches, exterior walls, foundation... or the plumbing?"). I answered "no" and did not know why it mattered until much later.

I did not read any of it. My actual answer was buried at the bottom, after maybe 40,000 characters. Answers 3 to 5 were much better because it said "printed above in this chat" and skipped the dump.

- When it wrote the e-mail, it was fast and did exactly what I asked.

### 4. Trust
Mixed. I believed the big points because the tool kept showing where they came from, especially the quotes from my own lease, which I could check. Being told over and over "this is not a lawyer's advice" and "the tool picks neither" was honest, but it made it hard to feel safe acting on the hard questions. The `[Check: the quote ... is not word for word ...]` lines at the bottom bothered me. Each one told me a quote it used was not in the place it said and that I should "check it against the source yourself". I am a finance major, I cannot do that. It showed the tool admits mistakes, but it also made me wonder what else might be off. The "[not verified]" tags and "[Development run: some of these pages are not yet verified by the author.]" did not help either.
I felt safe acting on: paying the rent, calling the emergency number, and sending the e-mail, since none of those can hurt me. I would not act on any of the legal reasoning about "waiver" or "material" on my own.

## Plain summary

- **Verdict:** Helpful in the end. Alex left with a concrete plan (pay, call the emergency line, send the e-mail) and one clear, well-grounded answer about roommates, but only after wading through very long replies.
- **Did well:** Said honestly that withholding rent is not safe and that the law gives a defense, not a right to keep rent. Quoted the tenant's own lease (repair clause, late-fee clause, roommate clause, the 85-degree emergency rule) so it could be checked. Explained the late fee grows daily. Wrote a usable e-mail, applied two follow-up changes correctly, and sent nothing itself.
- **Problems:**
  - Answers 1 and 2 opened with tens of thousands of characters of case-law summaries, much of it about injuries, not the AC. The real answer came last.
  - It never said "pay" or "do not pay," only "your choice," twice.
  - Repeated "one reading / the other reading, the tool picks neither" with no practical meaning for the tenant.
  - `[Check: ...]` warnings about its own quotes, a "[not verified]" tag on every case, and the jargon (83.51(1)(a), "waiver", "material", "Section 13.1") left a non-lawyer unsure what was solid.
  - The tool-note under the letter was hard to follow, and the letter has to be copied off the screen by hand.
  - It did not give today's late fee, said nothing on whether the fee can be dropped, did not suggest keeping the roommates' chat or saving evidence, and gave no next step if the landlord ignores the e-mail.
- **Missing for a student:** a one-screen "what to do today" summary, a clear dollar amount, a list of dates to watch (no timeline was ever shown to Alex), and an explanation of why some sheet questions (structure/plumbing) mattered.
- **Biggest single fix:** lead each reply with a short, plain "what to do today" section, then offer the cases and statute text on request.
