# Atlantis Estate Acquisitions, Inc. v. DePierro

Fourth District (Warner, J.), on the landlord's appeal. The tenants had paid a year's rent in one lump sum;
the landlord ended the tenancy after finding damage, and the tenants sued for the unused rent and their
deposits.

## Facts

- "Instead, the landlord agreed to accept $66,000 for the rent for the entire year, if the tenants paid it in one lump sum." (at 890)
- "The landlord sent the tenants a notice of termination of tenancy, indicating he was terminating the lease immediately." (at 891)
- "It found that the landlord had violated the notice requirements of section 83.49(3) with respect to the security and pet deposits and awarded the tenant the amount of those deposits plus interest thereon." (at 892)

## Was a year's rent paid in one lump sum advance rent that had to be held separately?

Topic: [[Advance rent]], [[How the landlord holds the deposit and advance rent]]

Rule:
- "Therefore, the $66,000 was not being held for a future rental period but was paid for the current year's rental. It did not constitute advance rent within the meaning of the Landlord and Tenant Act." (at 893)
- "Because it was not advance rent, the landlord did not have an obligation under section 83.49(1) to segregate the rental payment or to post a bond to secure the tenants' rent payment." (at 893)

Reasoning:
- "Section 83.43(9) defines “advance rent” as “moneys paid to the landlord to be applied to future rent payment periods, but does not include rent paid in advance for a current rent payment period.”" (at 893)
- "In this case, the lease provided for a rental period of one year, with the entire $66,000 due at the beginning of the rental period." (at 893)

## Even if it were advance rent, did the landlord have to return the unused part after ending the lease?

Topic: [[Advance rent]]

Rule:
- "In any case, even if this were advance rent, the landlord argues that it still would have been entitled to retain the unused portion of the money which the tenants paid. We agree." (at 893)
- "The landlord was entitled to keep the full amount of the rental payments based upon this well settled law." (at 893)

Reasoning:
- The court quoted Wagner v. Rice, 97 So. 2d 267 (Fla. 1957): "[W]here a lessee pays rent in advance and the lease is properly terminated by the lessor before the advanced rental payment is consumed by occupancy of the tenant the lessor is not obligated to return to the lessee the amount of the advance rental payment that would otherwise have applied beyond the date of the termination." (at 893)

## Could the tenants recover the rent as unjust enrichment?

Topic: [[Advance rent]]

Rule:
- "Unjust enrichment cannot apply where an express contract exists which allows the recovery." (at 893)
- "The failure to hold the rental payment in escrow for the tenant was neither required by statute, nor a ground for claiming unjust enrichment." (at 894)

Reasoning:
- The court quoted a First District case: "Florida courts have held that a plaintiff cannot pursue a quasi-contract claim for unjust enrichment if an express contract exists concerning the same subject matter." (at 894)

## Was the landlord owed the utilities for the rest of the term after ending the lease?

Topic: [[Landlord's remedies after a tenant's breach]]

Rule:
- "Termination under the first option also ends any further liability of the tenant under the rental agreement." (at 892)
- "As the trial court's determination that the landlord terminated the agreement was supported by competent, substantial evidence, we affirm the trial court's denial of the utility payments which came due after termination of the lease." (at 893)

Reasoning:
- "Although the landlord claims that it was exercising the second option, it did not make the tenant aware that it would hold the tenant liable for continuing obligations under the lease." (at 893)
- "It did not make any demands for payment on the tenant until it filed its counterclaim in this lawsuit." (at 893)

## Statute read

The court quoted 83.49(3)(a) in a footnote, a version with 15 days to return the deposit and 30 days for
the notice of claim: "If the landlord fails to give the required notice within the 30-day period, he or she forfeits the right to impose a claim upon the security deposit." (at 892)

## Related

- Cites Wagner v. Rice, 97 So. 2d 267 (Fla. 1957), not in `cases/`. Cites none of the other case pages.

## Disposition

- "Because we conclude that the trial court should not have awarded the return of the rental payment to the tenants as unjust enrichment, we reverse the final judgment for entry of a new judgment in accordance with this opinion by eliminating recovery of the rental amounts by the tenants." (at 894)
- "Because of our ruling on the appeal issues, we affirm on the tenant's claims of entitlement to attorney's fees raised in their cross-appeal as well as the other issues raised by the landlord." (at 894)
