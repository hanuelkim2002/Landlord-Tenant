# Department of Business Regulation v. Stein

Third District (Hendry, J.), on the state agency's appeal from declaratory judgments for the owners of an
apartment building. The agency had acted on tenants' complaints that interest was not paid on their
deposits.

## Facts

- "After receiving complaints from the tenants, appellant instituted administrative action against appellees alleging that they had failed to pay interest on the tenants' security deposits it had been holding since October 1, 1972, as required by s 83.49, Fla.Stat., F.S.A., of the Florida Residential Landlord and Tenant Act." (at 206)

## Did the interest requirement reach deposits under leases signed before October 1, 1972?

Topic: [[Interest on the deposit]], [[Applying an amended statute]]

Rule:
- "we agree that this Section ... does not require the payment of interest by appellees on security deposits created under written leases executed by them prior to October 1, 1972." (at 207)

Reasoning:
- "To hold otherwise, under the facts of these appeals, would give retroactive effect to s 83.49(2), Fla.Stat., F.S.A." (at 207)
- "In making this determination, we recognize the presumption that statutes operate prospectively unless legislative intent to the contrary clearly appears. Here no such clear intent appears." (at 207)

## Could the agency fine the owners or revoke their license?

Rule:
- "Also, we agree with the holding of the circuit court in its final judgment that appellant had neither the power to fine appellees nor to revoke their license for failure to comply with s 83.49(2), Fla.Stat., F.S.A." (at 207)

Reasoning:
- "Appellant, as an administrative agency of the State of Florida, is a creature of the statutes of this state and is empowered only with the authority given to it by such statutes." (at 207)
- "Although this amendment does not operate to affect the final judgments herein appealed by appellant, it does make the question moot for the future." (at 207)

## Statute read

The interest requirement the court read was in 83.49(2) of the 1973 statute. The opinion notes later
amendments and does not pass on them: "This opinion should not be construed as passing on the effect of this new law." (at 207)

## Disposition

"Therefore, for the reasons stated and upon the authorities cited, the final judgments appealed are affirmed." (at 207)
