# Guthartz v. Lewis

Third District (Daniel S. Pearson, J.), on the landlord's appeal from awards of punitive damages and
attorneys' fees to tenants of an FHA-insured building. The Florida Supreme Court answered the question this
court certified in [[Lewis v. Guthartz]].

## Facts

- "As the court below found, Guthartz wrongfully overcharged the tenants, adamantly refused to return the rents and deposits, and attempted to conceal this wrongdoing by making false statements to the FHA." (at 603)

## Can punitive damages rest on the landlord's breach of contract alone?

Topic: [[Punitive damages against a landlord]]

Rule:
- "It is the well-established rule in Florida that punitive damages are not recoverable for breach of contract, irrespective of the motive of the defendant." (at 602)
- "But no matter how unjustified was Guthartz's conduct, in the final analysis it constituted only a breach of contract; and no matter how unyielding and oppressive that breach may have been, an independent tort was neither alleged nor proved." (at 603)

Reasoning:
- "We are bound by a long-standing and unbroken line of authority to reverse the punitive damage award." (at 603)
- In a footnote: "Any re-examination of the continued efficacy of and justification for the rule requiring an independent tort as a basis for punitive damages in a breach of contract action cannot come from this court." (at 603)

## Did 83.48 authorize fees for a suit on the FHA regulatory agreement?

Topic: [[Prevailing party fees under 83.48]]

Rule:
- "Applying that rule of strict construction to the present case, and finding that the tenants' act was not one “with respect to the rental agreement,” we must agree that the attorneys' fee award was not authorized by Section 83.48, Florida Statutes (1977)." (at 604)

Reasoning:
- "It is apodictic that an award of attorneys' fees is in derogation of the common law, and statutes authorizing such fees are to be strictly construed." (at 604)

## Did 83.49(3)(c) authorize fees?

Topic: [[Fees and costs in a suit over the deposit]]

Rule:
- "Similarly, Section 83.49(3)(c) provides no basis for an award of fees." (at 604)

Reasoning:
- "The issue in this litigation was not the tenants' right to the return of their security deposits upon vacating the premises, which, as is so plainly seen by Sections 83.49(3)(a) and (b), is the underlying litigation contemplated by Section 83.49(3)(c), and the sine qua non of a fee award." (at 604)

## Statute read

The court quoted the 1977 text of 83.48: "If a rental agreement contains a provision allowing attorney's fees to the landlord when he is required to take any action to enforce the rental agreement, the court may allow reasonable attorney's fees to the tenant when he prevails in any action with respect to the rental agreement." (at 603)

## Related

- Approved by [[Lewis v. Guthartz]]: "The district court correctly held these two sections inapplicable." ([[Lewis v. Guthartz]] at 224)
- [[Florida Patient's Compensation Fund v. Rowe]] (Fla. 1985), on the phrase this opinion uses: "At the outset, we note that some of the decisions of this Court contain the historically incorrect statement that attorney fee statutes are “in derogation of the common law.”" ([[Florida Patient's Compensation Fund v. Rowe]] at 1147) Rowe does not cite this case.

## Disposition

"Accordingly, we reverse so much of the judgments which awarded punitive damages and attorneys' fees, and affirm that part of the judgment awarding the special master's fees." (at 605)
