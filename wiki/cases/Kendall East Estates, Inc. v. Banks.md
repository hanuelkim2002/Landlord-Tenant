# Kendall East Estates, Inc. v. Banks

Third District (Schwartz, J.), on the seller-builder's appeal and the buyers' cross-appeal from the part of a judgment that left each side to bear its own fees and costs. Not a landlord-tenant case: the seller sued the buyers of a new house for an underpayment at closing, and the buyers counterclaimed under a separate punch list agreement.

## Facts

- "The contract contained a clause which provided that “(i)f either party is required to retain an attorney in order to enforce any right hereunder or to assure compliance herewith by the other, the prevailing party in any such litigation shall be entitled to . . . reasonable attorney’s fees.”" (at 1246)
- "The trial judge entered summary judgment for the plaintiff on the complaint. After a nonjury trial, he found in favor of the Banks on the counterclaim in the amount of $2,428.44. A single final judgment was thereafter entered in favor of Kendall East for the $1,389.56 difference." (at 1247)

## Was the seller, whose judgment the buyers' counterclaim reduced, entitled to its costs under 57.041?

Topic: [[Determining the prevailing party]]

Rule:
- "We first agree with Kendall East that since it was the only “party recovering judgment” in this action at law for money damages, the trial court was obliged by the terms of Section 57.041, Florida Statutes (1979) to assess costs in its favor." (at 1247)
- "This result is required notwithstanding the fact that the Banks reduced the amount of the plaintiff’s net judgment by the successful pursuit of their counterclaim." (at 1247)

Reasoning: none stated beyond the statute and the cases it cites.

## Was the seller entitled to fees under the sale contract for prosecuting its claim for the unpaid price?

Topic: [[Fee provisions in a contract or lease]]

Rule:
- "Kendall East was also entitled to attorney’s fees for the services involved in the prosecution of the complaint." (at 1247)
- In a footnote: "For the reasons stated below no fees are assessable for the defense of the counterclaim." (at 1247)

Reasoning:
- "The action to recover an unpaid portion of the purchase price specified in the purchase and sale agreement ... was plainly one to “enforce a right (under) or to assure compliance” with a provision of that contract." (at 1247)
- In a footnote: "The agreement to pay a stated consideration for the property survived the delivery of the deed at the closing." (at 1247)

## Were the buyers entitled to fees under the sale contract for their counterclaim?

Topic: [[Determining the prevailing party]], [[Fee provisions in a contract or lease]]

Rule:
- "The Banks’ cross-appeal claims that they should have been awarded attorney’s fees under the July 20 agreement for the maintenance of the counterclaim. For two reasons, we disagree." (at 1247)

Reasoning:
- "In the first place, because they did not succeed in recovering a judgment in their favor, they were not the “prevailing party in (the) litigation” as required by the pertinent clause of the contract." (at 1247)
- "Secondly, the counterclaim was, as it specifically alleged, brought pursuant to the August 30, 1978 agreement, rather than to enforce rights or require compliance with the July 20, 1978 contract itself. The attorney’s fee provision, by its own terms, therefore did not apply." (at 1247)

## Also said

- In a footnote, on costs: "The rule is different in equitable actions in which the court has discretion as to whether, and in whose favor, costs should be assessed." (at 1247)

## Disposition

"The judgment below is affirmed with the exception of the denial of costs and attorney’s fees to the plaintiff-appellant and the cause is remanded for the assessment of those amounts by the trial court." (at 1247)
