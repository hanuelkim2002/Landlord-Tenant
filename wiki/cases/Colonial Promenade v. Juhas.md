# Colonial Promenade v. Juhas

Fifth District (Cobb, J.), on the landlord's appeal from a circuit court judgment in a commercial lease case; the tenants did not appear on appeal. The landlord had evicted the business tenants and sought rent for the rest of the term under the lease.

## Facts

- "Colonial Promenade (hereinafter Colonial) filed an eviction action in October 1987 against Jan and Darina Juhas, doing business as Express Photo, to evict them from property it owns in Orlando, Florida." (at 1313)
- "The parties entered into a joint stipulation whereby the Juhases agreed to surrender possession of the leased premises to Colonial." (at 1313)
- "It also argued that it was entitled to rent which would accrue after the Juhases vacated the premises because the lease provided for acceleration of the rent." (at 1313)

## Does a landlord who evicts a defaulting tenant lose a claim, under the lease, for rent for the balance of the term less what it recovers by reletting?

Topic: [[Landlord's remedies after a tenant's breach]]

Rule:
- "Contrary to the trial court’s determination, it is not the fact of eviction by the landlord that is determinative; rather, it is the nature of his use of the property after the defaulting tenant leaves, whether voluntarily or via eviction, that governs the availability of the landlord’s remedies." (at 1314)
- "If, in fact, the lessor resumes possession of the premises and utilizes them for purposes other than recovery of the rental due him under the contract, then—and only then—is he foreclosed from suit for the balance because such would inequitably constitute a double remedy." (at 1315)
- "Ordinarily, the nature of the lessor’s repossession is a question of fact." (at 1315)

Reasoning:
- "To say, as did the trial court, that the fact of eviction of the defaulting tenant forecloses a judgment against that tenant is illogical and inequitable. It discriminates against the cooperative tenant in default who voluntarily relinquishes possession and rewards the recalcitrant one who refuses to leave absent formal eviction proceedings." (at 1315)
- "In other words, the landlord cannot have the use of the property and the rent due for use of the property. That is what is referred to in Geiger as a double remedy." (at 1314)
- "Indeed, the Florida Supreme Court has expressly recognized that upon breach of a lease one of three courses of action available in Florida to the lessor is the re-taking of possession for the account of the tenant, holding the latter responsible in general damages measured by the difference between the stipulated rent and any amount the lessor is able to recover in good faith from his re-letting." (at 1315)

## Also said

- In a footnote, on the lease's wording: "Although paragraph 21. A. I. misleadingly says that the “Lease shall terminate,” it is clear from a reading of the entire subparagraph that it contemplates only termination of the defaulting tenant’s right to possession under the lease, not termination of the tenant’s obligation to pay the rent for the balance of the term." (at 1315)

## Related

- Explains, after the trial court relied on its quoted language Geiger Mutual Agency, Inc. v. Wright, 233 So. 2d 444 (not in `cases/`): "The quoted language from Geiger in the trial court’s opinion is misleading, and led the trial court into error." (at 1314)
- Cites for the lessor's three courses of action Wagner v. Rice, 97 So. 2d 267 (not in `cases/`): "See Wagner v. Rice, 97 So.2d 267, 270 (Fla.1957)." (at 1315)
- Cites on the nature of the repossession as a question of fact Babsdon Co. v. Thrifty Parking Co., 149 So. 2d 566 (not in `cases/`): "Ordinarily, the nature of the lessor’s repossession is a question of fact. Babsdon at 569." (at 1315)
- Cites on the double remedy Hulley v. Cape Kennedy Leasing Corp., 376 So. 2d 884 (not in `cases/`): "See Hulley v. Cape Kennedy Leasing Corp., 376 So.2d 884, 885 (Fla. 5th DCA 1979)." (at 1315)

## Disposition

"Accordingly, we reverse and remand for a new trial in regard to the issue of appellant’s entitlement to rent for the balance of the lease after repossession." (at 1315)
