# Durene v. Alcime

Third District, on the tenant's petition for certiorari to review a circuit court appellate panel. The
tenant sued for her deposit; the landlord counterclaimed for damages.

## Facts

- "Petitioner vacated the subject residential property the first week of September, 1982 and requested in writing the return of a $300 security deposit." (at 1209)
- "On that date, defendant requested and was granted leave to file a counterclaim for actual and punitive damages." (at 1209)

## Does a late notice of claim bar all of the landlord's damage claims, or only claims against the deposit?

Topic: [[Landlord's notice of claim on the deposit]]

The issue in the court's words: "The question before us is whether failure to comply with the notice requirements of section 83.49(3)(a) bars forever all claims for damages or only bars those claims made against the security deposit." (at 1209)

Rule:
- "By his violation of the statute, the landlord has forfeited his access to that particular, readily available fund of money." (at 1210)
- "The fact that the landlord may have an independent action for damages is immaterial." (at 1210)
- "The security deposit may not be used for purposes of a set-off." (at 1210)

Reasoning:
- "We agree with the appellate panel that the plain language of the statute, which speaks only of security deposits, cannot be read to state the legislature's intent to impose, in effect, a 15 day statute of limitation on all claims for damages a landlord may have against a former tenant." (at 1209)
- "We also agree with the panel's decision that the purpose of the statute is to assure tenants that their security deposits will be returned expeditiously or they will be promptly notified otherwise." (at 1210)
- "We feel this procedure is sufficiently burdensome to deter landlords from willful violation of the statute." (at 1210)

## Is the tenant who recovered the deposit entitled to fees?

Topic: [[Fees and costs in a suit over the deposit]]

Rule:
- "Petitioner prevailed below and is entitled to costs and attorneys' fees from the defendant landlord." (at 1210)

Reasoning:
- "Section 83.49(3)(c) clearly states that when a party is obligated to adjudicate his right to the security deposit and prevails, that party is entitled to costs and a reasonable fee for his attorney." (at 1210)

## Statute read

The court quoted the 1981 text: "If the landlord fails to give the required notice within the 15-day period, he forfeits his right to impose a claim upon the security deposit." (at 1209)

## Related

- Distinguished by [[Plakhov v. Serova]]: "The Tenant's reliance on Durene v. Alcime, 448 So.2d 1208 (Fla. 3d DCA 1984), is unavailing, since that case involved a situation where the landlord's obligation to give the section 83.49(3)(a) notice was not excused by the Tenant's failure to give the notice required by section 83.49(5)." ([[Plakhov v. Serova]] at 1223)
- [[Casey v. Picinich]] also reads 83.49(3)(a) on a late notice. It does not cite Durene.
- Disagrees with In re Hasty, 25 B.R. 429 (Bankr. M.D. Fla. 1982), a bankruptcy decision not in `cases/`, on a set-off: "We disagree, however, with the court's decision to allow even the $215 as a set-off against the security deposit, whether the damages were admitted or not." (at 1210)

## Disposition

"The petition is denied in part and granted in part." (at 1210)
