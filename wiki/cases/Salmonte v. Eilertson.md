# Salmonte v. Eilertson

First District, per curiam, on the tenant's appeal and the landlords' cross-appeal from a jury verdict in an eviction that left the tenant in possession and awarded him nothing on his counterclaim. The tenant appealed the denial of fees; the landlords cross-appealed the denial of possession and fees.

## Facts

- "In May 1986, they began inspecting more frequently than the tenant liked, so he began refusing entrance to the landlords and the pest control service." (at 180)
- "The landlords soon filed a complaint for eviction. The tenant asserted retaliatory eviction as an affirmative defense and as a basis for a counterclaim. The tenant admitted, however, the unauthorized pets, rug, and other violations." (at 180)

## Could the tenant defend the eviction as retaliatory after admitting that he breached the lease?

Topic: [[Retaliatory eviction]]

Rule:
- "The judgment below must be reversed. The tenant admitted breaching the lease." (at 180)
- "Because the landlords had good cause to evict, the trial court erred in denying the landlords’ motion for directed verdict." (at 180)

Reasoning:
- "In view of the tenant’s admissions in that regard, the jury’s failure to award possession to the landlords could only be based on the retaliatory eviction defense." (at 180)
- "That defense, however, could not be raised because Section 83.64(3), Florida Statutes, provides that the defense does not apply when the landlord proves that the eviction is for good cause, and further defines good cause to include “violation of the rental agreement.”" (at 180)

## Were the landlords entitled to attorney fees and costs?

Topic: [[Prevailing party fees under 83.48]]

Rule:
- "Attorney fees and costs are granted appellees, the amount to be fixed by the trial court on remand." (at 180)

Reasoning: none stated.

## Statute read

- The court quoted the words of 83.64(3) that define good cause: "violation of the rental agreement" (at 180)

## Related

- Cites none of the other case pages.

## Disposition

"The judgment below is reversed, and the cause is remanded with directions to enter judgment for appellees." (at 180)
