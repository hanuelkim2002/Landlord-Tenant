# Bennett v. Mattison

First District, Judge Shivers writing, on the appeal of a tenant and her husband from a judgment entered on a directed verdict for the apartment owners in a slip-and-fall suit. Judge Ervin specially concurred.

## Facts

- "Melena Bennett, a tenant and part-time employee of the Casa Marina Apartments, slipped and fell in a hallway at the apartment complex on July 9, 1978." (at 874)
- "Both the hallway and the patio were provided for the common use and benefit of the tenants." (at 874)
- "The apartment manager testified that she had received numerous complaints from tenants about the slippery condition of the hallway when it was wet and that she had actually seen people slip in the hallway before Melena’s accident." (at 874)

## Did the tenant need proof of how or when the water came to be on the hallway floor to reach the jury?

Topic: [[Landlord's notice of a dangerous condition]], [[Landlord's liability for injuries on the premises]]

Rule:
- "This evidence was not essential to appellant’s recovery." (at 875)
- "However, appellants were entitled to submit the case to the jury under the theory that (1) appellees failed to take reasonable care to prevent water from coming on the floor; (2) appellees failed to take reasonable care by not having an inspection procedure to discover water on the floor and to clean it up; or (3) appellees failed to take reasonable care by not making the floor less slippery when water came to be on it." (at 875)

Reasoning:
- "The repeated verbal and written warnings to the owners that the hallway was slippery and dangerous put them on notice of the recurring nature of the problem and created a jury question as to whether the landlords exercised reasonable care to guard against a foreseeable danger." (at 875)
- "The direction of a verdict should be cautiously given and all inferences of fact should be construed most strictly in favor of the nonmovant." (at 875)

## What bearing does 83.51(2)(a) 3 have on a negligence claim over a fall in a common hallway?

Topic: [[Landlord's maintenance duties under 83.51]]

Rule:
- "Since both the hallway and patio were provided for the common use and benefit of the tenants, appellees were required by Section 83.51(2)(a) 3, F.S. (1977) to keep these areas safe and clean." (at 875)
- "The violation of this statutory duty would be evidence of negligence." (at 875)

Reasoning: none stated beyond the statute it cites.

## Did the tenant's alleged superior knowledge of the slippery hallway bar her recovery?

Topic: [[Landlord's liability for injuries on the premises]]

Rule:
- "Appellees argue that Melena’s recovery is barred because of her superior knowledge of the slippery condition of the hallway. We reject this argument for two reasons." (at 875)

Reasoning:
- "First, whether Melena had superior knowledge is a question for the jury." (at 875)
- "Second, Melena’s knowledge, if any, would present an issue of comparative negligence and would not bar her recovery." (at 875)

## Also said

- Judge Ervin, specially concurring: "my concurrence is based primarily upon Section 83.51(2)(a), placing the burden upon a landlord to make reasonable provisions for the clean and safe condition of common areas." (at 875)
- Judge Ervin, specially concurring: "In my judgment the statute, to that limited extent, removes the common law rule absolving the landlord from any duty to his tenants for injuries caused to them by defects on the leased premises, unless his knowledge of the defect is superior to that of the tenant." (at 875)
- Judge Ervin, specially concurring: "This is a murky area of the law and hopefully one which will soon be resolved by the Florida Supreme Court." (at 876)
