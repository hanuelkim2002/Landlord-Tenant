# Prosperi v. Code

Florida Supreme Court (Grimes, J.), reviewing a Fourth District decision that certified two questions of great public importance; the owner had appealed the denial of his attorney's fees. Not a landlord-tenant case: a contractor sued an owner to foreclose a mechanic's lien and on the contract, and the owner counterclaimed.

## Facts

- "There was no contractual agreement for attorney’s fees to be paid to the prevailing party in the event of a lawsuit." (at 1361)
- "The court denied the claim for mechanic’s lien because the contractor had submitted false interim affidavits which failed to state that the contractor had actually withheld ten percent of the payments to the subcontractors." (at 1361)
- "As a consequence, the court entered judgment in favor of the contractor for $17,309.06 but denied the contractor’s claim for attorney’s fees." (at 1361)

## When a contractor fails to foreclose its lien but recovers a net judgment on the contract, must the net judgment rule decide who prevails for fees under 713.29, or does the Moritz test apply?

Topic: [[Determining the prevailing party]]

The issue in the court's words: "DOES THE TEST OF MORITZ V. HOYT FOR DETERMINING WHO IS THE PREVAILING PARTY FOR THE PURPOSES OF AWARDING ATTORNEY’S FEES APPLY TO FEES AWARDED UNDER SECTION 713.29, FLORIDA STATUTES?" (at 1361)

Rule:
- "The fact that the claimant obtains a net judgment is a significant factor but it need not always control the determination of who should be considered the prevailing party. We hold that in considering whether to apply the net judgment rule, the trial judge must have the discretion to consider the equities and determine which party has in fact prevailed on the significant issues." (at 1363)
- "The rationale of this opinion precludes us from giving an unqualified affirmative or negative answer to the first certified question. We answer the second certified question in the affirmative." (at 1363)
- "We disapprove of the cases cited herein in support of the net judgment rule only to the extent they may be read to require a strict application of the rule." (at 1363)

Reasoning:
- "As we see it, the net judgment rule itself was originated as a device to do equity. ... In some of the later cases, however, the net judgment rule appears to have been applied mechanically without regard to the equities. We believe that Moritz now requires a more flexible application." (at 1363)
- "However, Moritz bears significantly upon the instant case because it disapproved Casavan v. Land O’Lakes Realty, Inc., 542 So.2d 371 (Fla. 5th DCA 1989), and Daniels v. Arthur Johannessen, Inc., 496 So.2d 914 (Fla. 2d DCA 1986), each of which had held that the party obtaining the greater award was the prevailing party even though that party rather than the other had breached the contract." (at 1363)
- "We also believe it is significant that in 1977 the legislature amended section 713.29 to provide that the attorney’s fees awarded under that section should be taxed as part of costs “as allowed in equitable actions.”" (at 1363)

## Was the owner the prevailing party in this case, for trial and appellate fees?

Topic: [[Determining the prevailing party]]

Rule:
- "Under the net judgment rule as tempered by equitable principles according to the rationale of our opinion, it is equally clear that the trial judge would have found the owner to be the prevailing party." (at 1363)
- "We quash the order awarding the contractor appellate attorney’s fees and direct that appellate attorney’s fees also be awarded to the owner." (at 1363)

Reasoning:
- "In the instant case, the findings of the trial judge make it clear that he believed that the owner was the innocent party but felt constrained not to award him attorney’s fees under a strict application of the net judgment rule." (at 1363)
- The trial court's finding, as the court quoted it: "The Court finds that the greater weight of the evidence has proven that the attorneys fees and costs incurred in this action directly, naturally, and proximately, resulted from the filing of the false affidavits." (at 1361)

## Also said

- In a footnote: "For purposes of this opinion, the net judgment rule comes into play when the claimant fails to foreclose a mechanic’s lien but obtains a judgment for the underlying claim which exceeds any claim of the owner. As applied, the rule automatically precludes the owner from recovering attorney’s fees under section 713.29." (at 1362)
- "Section 713.29, Florida Statutes (1989), provides that in any action brought to enforce a mechanic’s lien, the prevailing party is entitled to recover a reasonable attorney’s fee. Therefore, had this suit been limited to a claim for a mechanic’s lien, there is no question that the owner would be entitled to recover his attorney’s fees." (at 1362)

## Related

- Follows, while noting a difference from, [[Moritz v. Hoyt Enterprises]]: "Moritz is not directly on point because it did not involve a claim calling for the payment of attorney’s fees to the prevailing party combined with another claim which did not provide for the payment of attorney’s fees." (at 1363)
- Quashes the decision under review, Prosperi v. Code, Inc., 609 So. 2d 59 (not in `cases/`): "We review Prosperi v. Code, Inc., 609 So.2d 59 (Fla. 4th DCA 1992), in which the court certified the following as questions of great public importance" (at 1361)
- Quotes at length, as the start of the net judgment rule, Emery v. International Glass & Manufacturing, Inc., 249 So. 2d 496 (not in `cases/`): "However, beginning with the case of Emery v. International Glass & Manufacturing, Inc., 249 So.2d 496 (Fla. 2d DCA 1971), the district courts of appeal have developed what is now known as the “net judgment” rule." (at 1362)
- Cites a special concurrence in M & P Concrete Products, Inc. v. Woods, 590 So. 2d 429 (not in `cases/`): "A legitimate argument can be made that he ought to recover his attorney’s fees anyway, regardless of the fact that the contractor prevailed on other claims for which no attorney’s fees are provided." (at 1362)
- Notes that Moritz disapproved Casavan v. Land O'Lakes Realty, Inc., 542 So. 2d 371 (not in `cases/`)
- Notes that Moritz disapproved Daniels v. Arthur Johannessen, Inc., 496 So. 2d 914 (not in `cases/`)

## Disposition

"Therefore, we quash the decision below and remand with directions that attorney’s fees be awarded to the owner." (at 1363)
