# Kaplan v. McCabe

Fifth District, Judge Cobb, on the landlord's appeal from a judgment that he take nothing on the lease and that the tenants recover damages, on a finding that they were wrongfully evicted. The tenants left after the landlord delivered a three-day notice to pay rent or quit.

## Facts

- "Notwithstanding when construction ended, a certificate of occupancy was granted to Kaplan, as owner, on November 15, 1985, evidencing the completion of the tenant improvements and allowing use of the premises as a bingo hall." (at 1355)
- "On December 20, 1985, a “3–DAY NOTICE TO PAY RENT OR QUIT” was delivered to Marto Enterprises demanding Marto Enterprises pay the November and December rent or surrender possession." (at 1356)
- "By January 6, 1986, McCabe had vacated the leased premises." (at 1356)

## When did the lease term begin under its certificate-of-occupancy provision?

Topic: [[Interpreting the lease]]

Rule:
- "No evidence in the record supports the trial court’s finding that the lease began in January." (at 1356)
- "The lease provision quoted above controls, and while the lease could have taken effect earlier than November 15, 1985, it must have taken effect on the day the certificate of occupancy was issued, or in this instance, November 15, 1985." (at 1356)

Reasoning:
- "Having failed to demand possession, McCabe cannot object on the grounds that his possession was somehow delayed." (at 1356)
- "The trial court’s finding of fact concerning the effective date of the lease is clearly erroneous because it fails to follow the plain meaning of the lease agreement." (at 1356)

## Was the three-day notice, which demanded November and December rent, legally sufficient?

Topic: [[Three-day notice for nonpayment of rent]]

Rule:
- "The notice to pay rent or quit was not proper or legally sufficient, because the rent became due when the lease commenced on November 15, 1985." (at 1357)

Reasoning:
- "This first month’s rent was prepaid by McCabe." (at 1357)
- "A demand for rent due must be made by the landlord or his agent, of the exact amount due, on the date it is due." (at 1357)

## Can a three-day notice to pay rent or quit be a constructive eviction?

Topic: [[Constructive eviction]]

The issue in the court's words: "This appeal involves the question of whether a three-day notice to pay rent or quit can constitute a constructive eviction." (at 1355)

Rule:
- "The facts of this action make it clear that a notice to pay rent or quit, standing alone and without other coercive or abusive-type conduct on the part of the landlord, cannot serve as a basis for constructive eviction." (at 1357)
- "Even assuming we would recognize an improperly filled-out notice to pay rent or quit as a basis for constructive eviction, McCabe would still not carry the day; timely notice must be given to the landlord of the objectionable act and a demand for rectification must be made." (at 1357)
- "Based on the facts contained in the record, there can be no wrongful or constructive eviction, and the damage award as set forth by the trial court is inappropriate." (at 1357)

Reasoning:
- The court quoted 14 A.L.R.2d 1450, 1451 (1950) for the majority view: "There is some authority to the contrary, but the prevailing view would seem to be that a mere notice to the tenant to quit, followed by his vacation of the premises, is not of itself sufficient to constitute an eviction and give the tenant a right to damages." (at 1357)
- "A notice to pay rent or quit does not give rise to a presumption that the landlord intends that the tenant should no longer enjoy the premises. When a landlord requests that a tenant pay rent, just the opposite is true." (at 1357)
- "Any other interpretation would permit a tenant who desires to avoid a lease obligation to do so when a demand for rent contains any technical defect such as the wrong date or an incorrect amount of money." (at 1357)

## Was an earlier July lease properly kept out of evidence?

Topic: [[Interpreting the lease]]

Rule:
- "While not the main issue on appeal, the trial court was within its discretion in excluding a July lease inasmuch as the September lease agreement was not ambiguous and appeared on its face to be the entire agreement between the parties." (at 1357)

Reasoning:
- "A provision of the September lease contains a merger clause entitled “Complete Agreement.”" (at 1357)

## Also said

- On the notice under section 83.20(2): "The statutory requirements are such that a tenant is able to withhold rent, if he feels he has a valid reason, without the fear of being summarily ejected without legal process." (at 1357)

## Disposition

"Accordingly, we reverse the judgment below and remand for a new trial on the issue of Kaplan’s damages based upon breach by McCabe and Marto Enterprises of the lease agreement commencing November 15, 1985." (at 1357)
