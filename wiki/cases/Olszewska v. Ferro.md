# Olszewska v. Ferro

Third District, per curiam, on the occupant's petition for a writ of prohibition to disqualify the trial judge, and her appeal from a final judgment for removal of tenant entered after a default.

## Facts

- "The plaintiff’s complaint did not claim rental payments in arrears." (at 12)
- "The alleged lease in this case provided a rental rate of five dollars per week, yet its holdover provision charges three hundred fifty dollars per week." (at 12)

## Did the exchanges between the trial judge and the occupant's attorney require the judge's disqualification?

Rule:
- "We grant the writ of prohibition. The verbal altercations that occurred between Olszewska’s attorney and the court require the judge to disqualify himself." (at 11)

Reasoning:
- "When a trial judge leaves the realm of civility and directs base vernacular towards an attorney or litigant in open court, there are sufficient grounds to require disqualification." (at 11)

## Was a default for not paying rent into the court registry proper when the complaint claimed no rent in arrears and the rate had not been determined?

Topic: [[Rent paid into the court registry]]

Rule:
- "On the merits, we find that the order of default was improper." (at 11)

Reasoning:
- "This disparity requires the trial court to determine the appropriate rental rate." (at 12)
- "There is no evidence that such a hearing took place, nor is there any evidence that Olszewska received adequate notice of the requirement to place the proper amount into the circuit court registry." (at 12)

## Also said

- In a footnote: "The writ of prohibition invalidates the final judgment for removal of tenant because the order was entered after the motion to disqualify was denied." (at 11)

## Statute read

- The court quoted the 1989 text of 83.60(2), noting emphasis added: "In an action by the landlord for possession of a dwelling unit, if the tenant interposes any defense other than payment, the tenant shall pay into the registry of the court the accrued rent as alleged in the complaint or as determined by the court and the rent which accrues during the pendency of the proceeding, when due. The court shall notify the tenant of such a requirement." (at 11)
- And: "Failure of the tenant to pay the rent into the registry of the court as provided herein constitutes an absolute waiver of the tenant’s defenses other than payment, and the landlord is entitled to an immediate default without further notice or hearing thereon." (at 12)

## Related

- Cites, on disqualification, Lamendola v. Grossman, 439 So. 2d 960 (not in `cases/`): "See, e.g., Lamendola v. Grossman, 439 So.2d 960 (Fla. 3d DCA 1983)" (at 11)
- Cites, on prohibition, Brown v. Rowe, 96 Fla. 289 (not in `cases/`): "Brown v. Rowe, 96 Fla. 289, 118 So. 9 (1928) (once a basis for disqualification has been established, prohibition is both appropriate and necessary)." (at 11)

## Disposition

"Accordingly, we grant petitioner’s writ of prohibition and reverse and remand the order of default to the trial court for further proceedings consistent with this opinion." (at 12)
