# Moskowitz v. Aslam

Third District, Chief Judge Schwartz, on the landlords' petition for certiorari after the circuit court, sitting as an appellate court, affirmed the county court's own-motion dismissal of an eviction and past-due rent action. The opinion was later clarified on April 8, 1991.

## Facts

- "The county court sua sponte dismissed an action for residential eviction and past due rent because the three day letter ... served pursuant to section 83.56(3), Florida Statutes (1989), incorporated a demand—that the rent be paid “in cash”—which does not explicitly appear in the form set out in the statute" (at 1367)
- "The letter stated: You are hereby notified that you are indebted to me in the sum of $395 for rent and use of the premises ... now occupied by you." (at 1367)
- "That rent was due on October 1, 1989, and I demand payment of the said rent in cash or possession of said premises within 3 days (excluding Saturdays, Sundays, or legal holidays) from the date of delivery of this notice, to wit: on or before October 16, 1989." (at 1367)

## Does a three-day notice that demands the rent be paid in cash depart from the form 83.56(3) sets out?

Topic: [[Three-day notice for nonpayment of rent]]

Rule:
- "It is clear that the “cash” requirement, far from rendering the notice invalid, amounted to no more than a redundancy: under the law any demand for payment itself implies that it may be met only by a discharge of the obligation “in money.”" (at 1368)
- "It is thus clear that the notice before us comports with the statutory admonition that “[t]he 3–day notice shall contain a statement in substantially ” the form stated." (at 1368)

Reasoning:
- From the circuit court dissent of Judge Green, which the court approved and adopted: "It is clear that the legislative intent of this statute is to require a landlord to formally place a tenant on notice of any rent owed and either exact payment for the same or possession within three (3) days prior to the institution of an eviction proceeding." (at 1368)
- From the same dissent: "More importantly, however, a demand for payment in cash is not prejudicial to the tenant and does not impose any greater obligation on the tenant than he already has under this law—i.e. satisfy his indebtedness or vacate the premises." (at 1368)
- From the same dissent: "I would thus reverse the trial court’s ruling and would find that a three (3) day notice requiring that payment be made in cash is in substantial compliance with the intent of F.S. 83.56(3)." (at 1368)

## Also said

- From the adopted circuit court dissent, on landlords and returned checks: "On the other hand, in disallowing a landlord to exact cash from a tenant in the three (3) day notice, this court is effectively eliminating a means whereby a landlord could limit his losses from “deadbeat” tenants who have had a history of tendering worthless checks to the landlord as payment for the rent." (at 1368)

## Statute read

- In a footnote, the court quoted the 1989 statutory form: "You are hereby notified that you are indebted to me in the sum of ____ dollars for the rent and use of the premises (address of leased premises, including county), Florida, now occupied by you and that I demand payment of the rent or possession of the premises within 3 days (excluding Saturday, Sunday, and legal holidays) from the date of delivery of this notice, to wit: on or before the ____ day of ____, 19__." (at 1367)
