# Bell v. Kornblatt

Fourth District, Judge Gross, on the tenant's petition for certiorari to review a circuit court appellate decision that affirmed a county court eviction judgment. The tenant argued that a defective three-day notice left the county court without subject matter jurisdiction.

## Facts

- "Petitioner, Nancy Bell, seeks certiorari review of an order entered by the circuit court sitting in its appellate capacity, which affirmed a final judgment for eviction entered by the county court." (at 113)
- "She argues that the eviction was predicated on a three-day notice that did not conform to section 83.56(3), Florida Statutes (1995)." (at 114)
- "Petitioner contends that the landlord’s failure to comply with the statutory notice requirement rendered the trial court without subject matter jurisdiction over the eviction complaint." (at 114)

## Is a three-day notice that conforms to 83.56(3) needed for the county court to have subject matter jurisdiction over an eviction?

Topic: [[Three-day notice for nonpayment of rent]]

Rule:
- "These cases incorrectly conclude that a proper three-day notice is essential to the subject matter jurisdiction of the county court in an eviction action." (at 114)
- "Compliance with the statutory notice requirement is merely a condition precedent to an eviction action under part II of Chapter 83." (at 114)
- "Failure of a plaintiff landlord to deliver any written notice under part II of Chapter 83 would not deprive the court of the power to adjudicate the case." (at 114)

Reasoning:
- "Subject matter jurisdiction is conferred on a court by the state constitution and applicable statutes." (at 114)
- "Based on these authorities, the trial court in this case had subject matter jurisdiction over the eviction action, even assuming that the three-day notice failed to substantially comply with the requirements of section 83.56(3)." (at 114)
- "As the circuit court observed in this case, such a holding is not reconcilable with a view that the notice is necessary to subject matter jurisdiction." (at 115)

## Also said

- In a footnote, on a waiver under Moskos v. Hand: "Section 83.47(1)(a), Florida Statutes (1995), renders a waiver under Moskos v. Hand, 247 So.2d 795 (Fla. 4th DCA 1971), inapplicable to residential tenancies, since it provides that a provision in a rental agreement is void to the extent that it “purports to waive ... the rights, remedies, or requirements set forth in this part.”" (at 115)
- In a footnote: "Section 83.20(2), Florida Statutes (1995), is substantially the same as the 1969 version of the statute, which did not contain any part expressly covering residential tenancies." (at 115)

## Statute read

- The court quoted the 1995 text of 83.56(3), on when a landlord may terminate the rental agreement: "fails to pay rent when due and the default continues for 3 days, excluding Saturday, Sunday, and legal holidays, after delivery of written demand by the landlord for payment of the rent or possession of the premises." (at 114)
- The court quoted 34.011(2), Florida Statutes (1995), on county court jurisdiction: "jurisdiction of proceedings relating to the right of possession of real property and to the forcible or unlawful detention of lands and tenements." (at 114)

## Disposition

"The petition for writ of certiorari is denied." (at 115)
