# Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (981 So. 2d 515)

Fourth District (Polen, J.), on the landlords' appeal from a non-final order finding a case or controversy and certifying a class of tenants charged lease fees. Stone, J. dissented. A later appeal in the same suit is Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558).

## Facts

- "Moss was a tenant in one of Appellants’ apartment complexes who exercised her right of early termination of her lease and was charged an early cancellation fee amounting to one month’s rent." (at 517)
- "Moss paid the charge and filed a class action complaint alleging that Appellants’ lease contained fee provisions violating Florida law, specifically Chapter 83, as the charges did not take into consideration when the apartment was re-rented, resulting in greater damages to the landlord than actually sustained." (at 517)
- One of the lease's conditions for cancelling early: "(c) paying on the date RESIDENT gives written notice of cancellation, an additional amount equal to one month’s rent as liquidated damages; plus" (at 518)

## Did the tenant who paid the cancellation fee have standing to bring the class action?

Topic: [[Tenants suing as a class]]

Rule:
- "We find Moss has met this burden and affirm the trial court’s holding regarding standing." (at 518)
- "Therefore, Moss fell with the category of individuals having a case or controversy regarding this issue." (at 518)

Reasoning:
- "In determining whether a case or controversy exists, the trial court is not required to determine the merits of the case, but rather is to determine whether sufficient facts have been alleged to establish that there is an issue to be decided." (at 518)
- "Moss fell into the first category, arguing she was charged an illegal cancellation fee that she was forced to pay or be faced with the consequence of being reported to a collections agency." (at 518)

Dissent:
- "In my judgment, the plaintiff’s lease was not invalid. I would hold that Moss lacks standing and that the complaint and affidavit on their face fail to demonstrate “typicality.”" (at 520)
- "But to allow a tenant to prevail under Moss’ circumstances is to recognize damages where none exist." (at 520)
- "Nothing in section 83.595, Florida Statutes, addresses the circumstance where a tenant is allowed, at the tenant’s election, to move out without breaching the lease." (at 521)

## Did the trial court abuse its discretion in certifying the class?

Topic: [[Tenants suing as a class]]

Rule:
- "We further find the trial court did not err in certifying the class below." (at 520)
- "We find the trial court correctly determined there was commonality and typicality among the proposed members of the class." (at 520)

Reasoning:
- "We disagree, as the issue is not whether the three types of fees were the same, but whether or not Appellants’ practice of charging liquidated damages rather than actual damages violated Florida law or whether Appellants were required to credit tenants’ accounts with rent it received from re-letting the premises." (at 520)
- "Further, with regards to typicality, Moss presented sufficient evidence proving that her claim is typical to the other proposed members in the class in that all the class members were charged liquidated damages rather than actual damages." (at 520)
- "Based on Moss’s expert’s testimony, the size of the class supports the trial court’s finding that separate joinder would be impractical." (at 519)

Dissent:
- "I further would not permit the class action to proceed on this showing of “typicality.”" (at 521)
- "Unlike the tenants charged for breaching their lease or failing to give notice, Moss was not violating any provisions of the lease." (at 521)

## Also said

- In the dissent: "I recognize that, under Chapter 83 of the Florida Residential Landlord Tenant Act, a landlord may not charge a penalty beyond the actual damage suffered, and having a liquidated damages clause and a compensatory damages clause will generally result in an unfair benefit to the landlord." (at 520)

## Related

- Quotes on standing Ferreiro v. Philadelphia Indemnity Insurance Co., 928 So. 2d 374 (not in `cases/`): "The issue of standing is a threshold inquiry which must be made at the outset of the case before addressing whether the case is properly maintainable as a class action." (at 517)
- Quotes at length on class certification, a circuit court order Welcome v. Arvida Community Sales, Inc., 2004 WL 2340249 (not in `cases/`): "“Impracticable” does not mean impossible, and numerosity is satisfied if it would be difficult to join all the members of the class." (at 519)
- Quotes on commonality and typicality Terry L. Braun, P.A. v. Campbell, 827 So. 2d 261 (not in `cases/`): "Merely pointing to common issues of law is insufficient to meet the typicality requirement when the facts required to prove the claims are markedly different between class members." (at 520)

## Disposition

"We disagree with Appellants’ arguments, affirm the trial court’s holding and write to explain our holding." (at 517)
