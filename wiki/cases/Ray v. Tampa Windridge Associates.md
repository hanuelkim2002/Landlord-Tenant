# Ray v. Tampa Windridge Associates

Second District, Judge Lehan writing, on the tenant's appeal from a summary judgment for the landlords and the managing agent in her suit for injuries from an intruder's attack at the apartment complex.

## Facts

- "The injuries are alleged to have been from a rape of plaintiff which was allegedly the proximate result of, among other things, inadequate locks provided on plaintiff’s apartment" (at 677)
- "Defendants are the landlords and the managing agent of the complex." (at 677)
- "That document includes a provision that “I hereby release ... [the landlord] from any claim whatsoever with respect to any personal injury ... which is in any way related either to my reliance on any of the ... [door locks] mentioned above or to any defect, malfunction, or inadequacy concerning any of them.”" (at 677)

## Did the tenant's signed security release dispense with the landlord's duty under 83.51(2)(a) to make reasonable provisions for locks?

Topic: [[Landlord's maintenance duties under 83.51]], [[Exculpatory clauses in a lease]], [[Landlord's liability for criminal attacks on tenants]]

Rule:
- "However, as plaintiff argues and as we conclude, even assuming that section 83.51(2)(a) permits a landlord-tenant agreement relieving the landlord of that duty, the Apartment Security Acknowledgement and Release document in this case does not do so but actually indicates that the landlord will make reasonable provisions for locks on the tenant’s apartment." (at 677)
- "Thus, it does not appear that the duty of the landlord in this case to make reasonable provisions for locks was dispensed with." (at 677)

Reasoning:
- "Aside from the language in the above-quoted provision in that document which indicates that the tenant may have relied upon the locks, the document, in another provision, contains an agreement by the tenant to inspect the locks to assure that they are “in good working order.”" (at 677)

## Are the release's provisions on the tenant's non-reliance, and on the landlord's not checking or guaranteeing the locks, prohibited by 83.47?

Topic: [[Exculpatory clauses in a lease]], [[Landlord's liability for injuries on the premises]]

Rule:
- "Provisions to that effect are prohibited by section 83.47 and, for the reason explained above, are not excepted from section 83.47 by section 83.51(2)(a)." (at 678)

Reasoning:
- "These provisions seem to mean that, regardless of a duty of the landlord to make reasonable provisions for locks, there is no reliance by the tenant upon the landlord’s fulfillment of that duty and, therefore, it is argued, there can be no liability of the landlord to the tenant for a breach thereof." (at 678)
- "The meaning seems to be, in other words, that, regardless of such a duty of the landlord, there is a duty of the tenant which, it is argued, would preclude liability of the landlord." (at 678)

## Also said

- "Defendants do not contend that no cause of action has been stated." (at 677)

## Statute read

- The court quoted section 83.47 in part (no year stated): "(1) A provision in a rental agreement is void and unenforceable to the extent that it: (a) Purports to waive or preclude the rights, remedies, or requirements set forth in this part. (b) Purports to limit or preclude any liability of the landlord to the tenant or of the tenant to the landlord, arising under law." (at 677)
- The court quoted section 83.51(2)(a), Florida Statutes (1985), in part: "Unless otherwise agreed in writing ... the landlord ... shall ... make reasonable provisions for: .... 2. Locks and keys. 3. The ... safe condition of common areas." (at 677)

"Reversed and remanded for proceedings consistent herewith." (at 678)
