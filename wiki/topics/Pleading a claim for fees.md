# Pleading a claim for fees

Cases on whether a party must ask for fees in its pleadings, and in which count, to recover them. Scruggs
reads 83.48 (2004); Anglia Jacs, a suit on a lease decided under the lease's fee terms, cites no section of
ch. 83.

## Cases

- [[Anglia Jacs & Co. v. Dubin]], Fla. 4th DCA 2002, no statute year stated: Did the landlords' failure to request fees in their answer, or in one count of the counterclaim, bar the award?
- [[Scruggs v. Sutton]], Fla. 3d DCA 2007, reading the 2004 statute: Must a party plead entitlement to fees under 83.48, which treats fees as costs?

## How they relate

- Both read Stockman v. Downs (Fla. 1991), which is not in `cases/`, and neither cites the other. Scruggs quotes it: "A party seeking attorney's fees pursuant to statute or contract must plead entitlement to such fees." ([[Scruggs v. Sutton]] at 854) Anglia Jacs: "In Stockman, the supreme court held that even if the party prevails on the significant issues, it waives its right to attorney’s fees unless fees are requested in the pleadings." ([[Anglia Jacs & Co. v. Dubin]] at 172)
- Which count carries the request. In Anglia Jacs the landlords asked for fees in each count of their counterclaim but the declaratory one, and not in their answer: "When counts are intertwined, the trial courts often have no choice but to award attorney’s fees as to time spent on all claims, whether fees are pled in all counts or not, as long as the prevailing party pleads entitlement sufficiently to give notice to the other party." ([[Anglia Jacs & Co. v. Dubin]] at 171) In Scruggs the occupant asked for fees only in his counterclaim count for a resulting trust: "Attorney's fees are not recoverable in an action for a resulting trust." ([[Scruggs v. Sutton]] at 855) "The fact that Sutton requested fees in his count for a resulting trust is insufficient to support an award of fees under section 83.48 for defeating the eviction action." ([[Scruggs v. Sutton]] at 855)
- Fees as costs, and the statute's wording. The occupant in Scruggs pointed to the wording of 83.48 that "the party in whose favor a judgment or decree has been rendered may recover reasonable court costs, including attorney’s fees, from the nonprevailing party." ([[Scruggs v. Sutton]] at 854) Scruggs: "As we interpret Stockman, the claim must be pled, even if the applicable statute treats attorney's fees as costs." ([[Scruggs v. Sutton]] at 854) Xanadu, in 1987, before Stockman, in a footnote: "attorney's fees may be taxed as costs in a motion which need be made only within a reasonable time after the appeal in the case has been concluded." ([[Xanadu of Cocoa Beach, Inc. v. Lenz]] at 519) Scruggs does not cite Xanadu. Today's stored text says the party "may recover reasonable attorney fees and court costs from the nonprevailing party." (Fla. Stat. 83.48) More on 83.48: [[Prevailing party fees under 83.48]].
