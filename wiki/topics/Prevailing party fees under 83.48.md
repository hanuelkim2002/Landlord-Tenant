# Prevailing party fees under 83.48

Cases on attorney's fees under the general fee section of part II: which suits it reaches, who may recover,
and how the claim is made. Its wording has changed more than once since the earliest of these cases.

Today's stored text:
- "In any civil action brought to enforce the provisions of the rental agreement or this part, the party in whose favor a judgment or decree has been rendered may recover reasonable attorney fees and court costs from the nonprevailing party." (Fla. Stat. 83.48)
- "The right to attorney fees in this section may not be waived in a lease agreement. However, attorney fees may not be awarded under this section in a claim for personal injury damages based on a breach of duty under s. 83.51." (Fla. Stat. 83.48)

## Cases

- [[Guthartz v. Lewis]], Fla. 3d DCA 1981, reading the 1977 statute: Did 83.48 authorize fees for a suit on the FHA regulatory agreement?
- [[Lewis v. Guthartz]], Fla. 1982, reading the 1977 statute: Were fees recoverable under 83.48 for a suit on the FHA regulatory agreement?
- [[Florida Patient's Compensation Fund v. Rowe]], Fla. 1985, reading the 1981 and 1983 statutes: on this topic outside the issues it decided; see the case page.
- [[Xanadu of Cocoa Beach, Inc. v. Lenz]], Fla. 5th DCA 1987, reading the 1983 statute: Did the 1983 version of 83.48 apply to a rental agreement signed before it took effect?
- [[Salmonte v. Eilertson]], Fla. 1st DCA 1988, no statute year stated: Were the landlords entitled to attorney fees and costs?
- [[Gilbert v. Jabour]], Fla. 3d DCA 1988, reading the 1983 statute: Can a tenant who wins a personal injury suit based on 83.51 recover fees under 83.48?
- [[Meli Investment Corp. v. O.R.]], Fla. 3d DCA 1993, reading the 1991 statute: Could the fee for the landlord-tenant part of the case carry a risk multiplier?
- [[Florida RS, Inc. v. Nelson]], Fla. 5th DCA 1998, reading the 1995 statute: May the fee cover work on a class action that was never certified?
- [[Scruggs v. Sutton]], Fla. 3d DCA 2007, reading the 2004 statute: Must a party plead entitlement to fees under 83.48, which treats fees as costs?

## How they relate

- The wording the courts read. In 1977: "If a rental agreement contains a provision allowing attorney's fees to the landlord when he is required to take any action to enforce the rental agreement, the court may also allow reasonable attorney's fees to the tenant when he prevails in any action by or against him with respect to the rental agreement." ([[Lewis v. Guthartz]] at 224) From 1983: "In any civil action brought to enforce the provisions of the rental agreement or this part, the party in whose favor a judgment or decree has been rendered may recover reasonable court costs, including attorney's fees, from the nonprevailing party." ([[Xanadu of Cocoa Beach, Inc. v. Lenz]] at 519) Today's text, above, adds a sentence on waiver in a lease and one on personal injury claims under 83.51.
- Personal injury. Gilbert, decided in 1988, states no year for 83.48: "Section 83.48 of the statute limits the recovery of attorney's fees to the prevailing party in an action brought to enforce the provisions of a rental agreement." ([[Gilbert v. Jabour]] at 951) Today's text, above, speaks to personal injury claims based on 83.51 in its own words.
- Fees as costs, and pleading them. Xanadu, in a footnote, in 1987: "attorney's fees may be taxed as costs in a motion which need be made only within a reasonable time after the appeal in the case has been concluded." ([[Xanadu of Cocoa Beach, Inc. v. Lenz]] at 519) Scruggs, in 2007, after the Supreme Court's Stockman decision of 1991: "As we interpret Stockman, the claim must be pled, even if the applicable statute treats attorney's fees as costs." ([[Scruggs v. Sutton]] at 854)
- How strictly fee statutes are read. Guthartz: "It is apodictic that an award of attorneys' fees is in derogation of the common law, and statutes authorizing such fees are to be strictly construed." ([[Guthartz v. Lewis]] at 604) Rowe, four years later: "At the outset, we note that some of the decisions of this Court contain the historically incorrect statement that attorney fee statutes are “in derogation of the common law.”" ([[Florida Patient's Compensation Fund v. Rowe]] at 1147) Rowe does not cite Guthartz.
- A fee award with no stated source. Salmonte, an eviction in which the tenant raised retaliatory eviction under 83.64(3), names neither 83.48 nor a lease clause when it awards the landlords fees: "Attorney fees and costs are granted appellees, the amount to be fixed by the trial court on remand." ([[Salmonte v. Eilertson]] at 180) The trial court had "denied the parties’ respective motions for costs and attorney fees." ([[Salmonte v. Eilertson]] at 180) Salmonte cites none of the other case pages.
- Who the prevailing party is. Today's text, above, speaks of "the party in whose favor a judgment or decree has been rendered" (Fla. Stat. 83.48). Cases on how courts decide who prevailed, all under contracts, leases, or other statutes and none reading 83.48: [[Determining the prevailing party]].
