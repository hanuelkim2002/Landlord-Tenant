# Exculpatory clauses in a lease

Cases on lease clauses and signed releases that free the landlord from liability or put a risk on the
tenant: how courts read them, and what 83.47 and public policy have to do with them. Lockamy and Ray are
residential cases; Ray and the Lockamy dissent read part II. Fuentes, an apartment lease, mentions 83.47 in a footnote; the lease
was made, and the events occurred, before that statute took effect. Berwick, a motel lease, was decided in
1962, before the 1973 Act. Ralston, for tenants who ran a business in a hotel, cites no section of ch. 83.
John's Pass is a commercial lease of a boat slip and mentions 83.47 only in passing.

Today's stored text:
- "A provision in a rental agreement is void and unenforceable to the extent that it ... Purports to limit or preclude any liability of the landlord to the tenant or of the tenant to the landlord, arising under law." (Fla. Stat. 83.47(1))
- "The landlord’s obligations under this subsection may be altered or modified in writing with respect to a single-family home or duplex." (Fla. Stat. 83.51(1))
- "Unless otherwise agreed in writing, in addition to the requirements of subsection (1), the landlord of a dwelling unit other than a single-family home or duplex shall, at all times during the tenancy, make reasonable provisions for:" (Fla. Stat. 83.51(2)(a))

## Cases

- [[Berwick Corp. v. Kleinginna Investment Corp.]], Fla. 3d DCA 1962, no statute year stated: Did the lease clause putting the risk of water damage on the lessee bar the claim of constructive eviction?
- [[Fuentes v. Owen]], Fla. 3d DCA 1975, no statute year stated: Was the lease's exculpatory clause void under a statute that took effect after the lease was made and the events occurred? Does the lease's exculpatory clause relieve the landlord or its agent of liability for an intentional tort?
- [[Ralston, Inc. v. Miller]], Fla. 3d DCA 1978, no statute year stated: Did the lease's printed clauses on water damage relieve the lessor of the typed covenant to keep the roof and structure in repair?
- [[John's Pass Seafood Co. v. Weber]], Fla. 2d DCA 1979, reading the 1977 statute: Can a commercial lessor's exculpatory agreement relieve it of liability to the lessee for failing to comply with a fire code?
- [[Lockamy v. Byrne]], Fla. 1st DCA 1985, reading the 1973 and 1977 statutes: Was summary judgment for the landlords correct on the tenants' claim for injuries from the fall on the steps?
- [[Ray v. Tampa Windridge Associates]], Fla. 2d DCA 1991, reading the 1985 statute: Did the tenant's signed security release dispense with the landlord's duty under 83.51(2)(a) to make reasonable provisions for locks? Are the release's provisions on the tenant's non-reliance, and on the landlord's not checking or guaranteeing the locks, prohibited by 83.47?

## How they relate

- A risk clause read against the landlord's repair covenant, in two cases that cite no section of ch. 83. Berwick: "This provision of the lease cannot be construed to destroy the effect of the requirement that the landlord keep the roof sound and watertight." ([[Berwick Corp. v. Kleinginna Investment Corp.]] at 688) and "The last mentioned provision of the lease is properly construed as applying only to claims for damage to ‘property of the lessee’." ([[Berwick Corp. v. Kleinginna Investment Corp.]] at 688) Ralston quotes Berwick and compares its clause: "As in the matter before this court, the landlord in Berwick sought to avoid damages by the defense of a provision of the lease that the lessee was required to take the risk of damage to the property by reason of water, bursting pipes, gas leaks, water system or sewerage leaking, etc." ([[Ralston, Inc. v. Miller]] at 1070) Ralston also sets the typed covenant over the printed clauses: "Paragraph 23 of the lease, especially written and inserted in the lease by the parties, is clear and unambiguous." ([[Ralston, Inc. v. Miller]] at 1069)
- Clear terms, and intentional torts. Fuentes: "However, in upholding the validity of said clauses the courts have recognized that they are not looked upon with favor and are enforceable only where and to the extent that such intention was made clear and unequivocal in the contract." ([[Fuentes v. Owen]] at 459) and "While the exculpatory clause would be effective to bar a count based on Negligence, it would not so operate with respect to the claims asserted in the complaint based on allegations of intentional tort." ([[Fuentes v. Owen]] at 460) John's Pass, which does not cite Fuentes: "When the terms are clear, Florida recognizes the validity of exculpatory agreements." ([[John's Pass Seafood Co. v. Weber]] at 617) Both cite Middleton v. Lomaskin, 266 So. 2d 678, which is not in `cases/`.
- Safety codes and public policy. John's Pass: "We simply hold that these clauses shall be ineffective to relieve the lessor from liability to the lessee where the lessee’s damages are caused by the lessor’s failure to comply with a penal statute or ordinance imposing a positive duty upon the lessor which is intended to protect the person or property of the lessee or the public generally." ([[John's Pass Seafood Co. v. Weber]] at 618) and "We also limit our holding to those violations which by their nature would constitute negligence per se. We need not pass on the question of whether the same rule would apply if the violation constituted only evidence of negligence." ([[John's Pass Seafood Co. v. Weber]] at 618)
- 83.47 and when it reaches a lease. Fuentes, in 1975, in a footnote: "Under ch. 73—330, s 2 (appearing as s 83.47, Fla.Stat., F.S.A.) the exculpatory provision of this lease would be void. However, by a subsection thereof it would appear the statute is to be applied prospectively." ([[Fuentes v. Owen]] at 459) John's Pass, in 1979, in passing: "With the passage of Section 83.47, Florida Statutes (1977), the validity of an exculpatory provision for any purpose in a residential lease is in serious doubt." ([[John's Pass Seafood Co. v. Weber]] at 617) Today's 83.47(2) speaks of the part's effective date: "If such a void and unenforceable provision is included in a rental agreement entered into, extended, or renewed after the effective date of this part and either party suffers actual damages as a result of the inclusion, the aggrieved party may recover those damages sustained after the effective date of this part." (Fla. Stat. 83.47(2)) The stored 83.47 lists only its 1973 enactment in its history. On new statutes and older leases: [[Applying an amended statute]].
- Lockamy. The majority affirmed in one sentence. Among the trial court's alternative findings, as the dissent quotes them: "Assuming arguendo that the steps were defective or dangerous, the Plaintiffs herein waived the defects and dangerous condition, as evidenced by the Plaintiffs’ testimony and the lease agreement between Plaintiffs and Defendant herein." ([[Lockamy v. Byrne]] at 373) Judge Ervin's dissent quotes John's Pass, notes in a footnote the limit John's Pass put on its holding and that a violation of the Act has been called evidence of negligence: "I am also aware that a landlord’s violation of the provisions of the Florida Residential Landlord and Tenant Act has been characterized as evidence of negligence." ([[Lockamy v. Byrne]] at 375) It goes on: "Nevertheless, given the strong indication of legislative disapproval of exculpatory language absolving a landlord completely from his duty to transfer a reasonably safe dwelling unit to his tenant, see Section 83.47, Florida Statutes, I would regard a clause such as the one before us as void and unenforceable." ([[Lockamy v. Byrne]] at 375) The dissent also quotes 83.45(1), on unconscionable provisions, with no year; its quote has the same words as today's stored 83.45(1).
- Ray, a signed security release, and the opening words of 83.51(2)(a). Ray: "even assuming that section 83.51(2)(a) permits a landlord-tenant agreement relieving the landlord of that duty, the Apartment Security Acknowledgement and Release document in this case does not do so but actually indicates that the landlord will make reasonable provisions for locks on the tenant’s apartment." ([[Ray v. Tampa Windridge Associates]] at 677) On the release's other provisions: "Provisions to that effect are prohibited by section 83.47 and, for the reason explained above, are not excepted from section 83.47 by section 83.51(2)(a)." ([[Ray v. Tampa Windridge Associates]] at 678) Ray cites none of the other cases here. The 1985 text Ray quoted opens with the same words as today's 83.51(2)(a), above: "Unless otherwise agreed in writing ... the landlord ... shall ... make reasonable provisions for: .... 2. Locks and keys. 3. The ... safe condition of common areas." ([[Ray v. Tampa Windridge Associates]] at 677) No case on this topic quotes the sentence of today's 83.51(1), above, on single-family homes and duplexes.
- Agreement and waiver in case pages not listed on this topic. [[Mansur v. Eubanks]], on the duty before the tenant takes possession: "This duty may be modified by agreement of the parties." ([[Mansur v. Eubanks]] at 1330) [[Smith v. Grove Apartments, LLC]]: "In the context of a landlord/tenant relationship, the law is well settled that after a tenant takes possession of a residential dwelling unit a landlord has a continuing statutory duty to maintain common areas in a safe condition and to repair dangerous, defective conditions upon notice of their existence, unless otherwise agreed to by the tenant." ([[Smith v. Grove Apartments, LLC]] at 586) Clauses waiving the statute's notice, under 83.47(1)(a): [[Waiving the statutory notice in the lease]].
