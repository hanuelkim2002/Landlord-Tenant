# Three-day notice for nonpayment of rent

Cases on the landlord's written demand for the rent or possession before an eviction for nonpayment: what
it says, how it is delivered, whether a lease can waive it, and what follows when a tenant says it was
defective. Three of the cases read 83.20(2), in part I, not part II: Moskos (decided in 1971, before the
1973 Act), Clark (a nonresidential lease of 638 acres of land), and Kaplan (a warehouse leased for a bingo hall).

Today's stored text:
- "If the tenant fails to pay rent when due and the default continues for 3 days, excluding Saturday, Sunday, and legal holidays, after delivery of written demand by the landlord for payment of the rent or possession of the premises, the landlord may terminate the rental agreement." (Fla. Stat. 83.56(3))
- "The delivery of the written notices required by subsections (1)-(3) shall be by mailing, delivering a true copy thereof, e-mailing in accordance with s. 83.505 , or, if the tenant is absent from the premises, by leaving a copy thereof at the residence." (Fla. Stat. 83.56(4))
- "if the tenant interposes any defense other than payment, including, but not limited to, the defense of a defective 3-day notice, the tenant shall pay into the registry of the court the accrued rent as alleged in the complaint or as determined by the court and the rent that accrues during the pendency of the proceeding, when due." (Fla. Stat. 83.60(2))

## Cases

- [[Moskos v. Hand]], Fla. 4th DCA 1971, no statute year stated: Was the three-day notice needed when the tenant had agreed in the lease to waive all notice?
- [[Investment & Income Realty, Inc. v. Bentley]], Fla. 5th DCA 1985, reading the 1983 statute: Does the civil procedure rule that adds time for service by mail apply to a three-day notice the landlord mails under 83.56(3)?
- [[Clark v. Hiett]], Fla. 2d DCA 1986, reading the 1983 statute: Did the lease's default and termination clause waive the statutory three-day notice? Having sued for eviction in county court, did the landlord have to give the three-day notice?
- [[Kaplan v. McCabe]], Fla. 5th DCA 1988, reading the 1985 statute: Was the three-day notice, which demanded November and December rent, legally sufficient?
- [[Moskowitz v. Aslam]], Fla. 3d DCA 1991, reading the 1989 statute: Does a three-day notice that demands the rent be paid in cash depart from the form 83.56(3) sets out?
- [[Bell v. Kornblatt]], Fla. 4th DCA 1998, reading the 1969 and 1995 statutes: Is a three-day notice that conforms to 83.56(3) needed for the county court to have subject matter jurisdiction over an eviction?
- [[Stanley v. Quest International Investment]], Fla. 4th DCA 2010, reading the 2009 statute: Must a tenant who claims the three-day notice was defective or missing deposit the rent into the court registry to raise that defense?
- [[Stephens-Williams v. Johnson]], Fla. 4th DCA 2016, reading the 2014 statute: Could the tenant have a hearing on her challenge to the three-day notice when she neither paid rent into the registry nor moved to determine rent within five days of service?

## How they relate

- Part I and part II. Moskos, Clark, and Kaplan read 83.20(2), in part I; Clark quotes its 1983 text: "Where such person holds over without permission as aforesaid, after any default in the payment of rent pursuant to the agreement under which the premises are held, and 3 days’ notice in writing, requiring the payment of the rent or the possession of the premises, has been served by the person entitled to the rent on the person owing the same." ([[Clark v. Hiett]] at 774) Bell, a residential case under 83.56(3), says in a footnote: "Section 83.20(2), Florida Statutes (1995), is substantially the same as the 1969 version of the statute, which did not contain any part expressly covering residential tenancies." ([[Bell v. Kornblatt]] at 115) The other five cases read part II.
- A condition precedent. Clark, under part I: "The three-day notice under section 83.20(2) was a condition precedent to relief." ([[Clark v. Hiett]] at 775) Bentley, under 83.56(3): "A statutory cause of action cannot be commenced until the claimant has complied with all the conditions precedent." ([[Investment & Income Realty, Inc. v. Bentley]] at 220) Bell cites Bentley on the point: "Compliance with the statutory notice requirement is merely a condition precedent to an eviction action under part II of Chapter 83." ([[Bell v. Kornblatt]] at 114)
- Subject matter jurisdiction. Bell disagrees with circuit and county court decisions that are not in `cases/`: "These cases incorrectly conclude that a proper three-day notice is essential to the subject matter jurisdiction of the county court in an eviction action." ([[Bell v. Kornblatt]] at 114) Stanley relies on Bell: "We have previously held the notice requirement unnecessary to establish subject matter jurisdiction." ([[Stanley v. Quest International Investment]] at 673)
- Delivery by mail. Bentley read the 1983 text of 83.56(4): "The delivery of the written notices required by subsections (1), (2) and (3) shall be by mailing or delivery of a true copy thereof or, if the tenant is absent from his last or usual place of residence, by leaving a copy thereof at the residence." ([[Investment & Income Realty, Inc. v. Bentley]] at 220) On the notice the landlord mailed: "Having elected to mail the notice, the landlord had to allow the mailing time required by rule 1.090(e)." ([[Investment & Income Realty, Inc. v. Bentley]] at 220) Today's text of 83.56(4), above, adds e-mailing under 83.505, which says of an e-mailed notice: "A notice sent electronically pursuant to this section is deemed delivered at the time it is sent, unless the e-mail is returned to the sender as undeliverable." (Fla. Stat. 83.505(4)) No case in the corpus reads 83.505.
- What the notice says. Moskowitz, on a demand for payment in cash under the 1989 form: "It is thus clear that the notice before us comports with the statutory admonition that “[t]he 3–day notice shall contain a statement in substantially ” the form stated." ([[Moskowitz v. Aslam]] at 1368) Kaplan, under part I, on a notice that demanded November and December rent: "A demand for rent due must be made by the landlord or his agent, of the exact amount due, on the date it is due." ([[Kaplan v. McCabe]] at 1357) Moskowitz does not cite Kaplan. Kaplan also asks whether such a notice can be a constructive eviction: [[Constructive eviction]].
- A defective notice and the court registry. Stanley, reading the 2009 text: "The statute plainly requires the payment of rent if the tenant chooses to assert any defense other than payment, and failure to make the necessary deposit constitutes an absolute waiver allowing for immediate default judgment in favor of the landlord." ([[Stanley v. Quest International Investment]] at 673) Stephens-Williams, reading the 2014 text, cites none of the other case pages: "The statute is unforgiving, and failure to timely file “constitutes an absolute waiver of the tenant’s defenses other than payment.”" ([[Stephens-Williams v. Johnson]] at 577) The 2009 text as Stanley quotes it: "In an action by the landlord for possession of a dwelling unit, if the tenant interposes any defense other than payment, the tenant shall pay into the registry of the court the accrued rent" ([[Stanley v. Quest International Investment]] at 673) Today's 83.60(2), above, names the defense of a defective 3-day notice in its own words. Today's 83.60(1)(a) also says: "The landlord must be given an opportunity to cure a deficiency in a notice or in the pleadings before dismissal of the action." (Fla. Stat. 83.60(1)(a)) More on the registry: [[Rent paid into the court registry]].
- Stephens-Williams cites 83.56(5)(a)(3), Florida Statutes (2014), for the three-day notice the tenant challenged; its case page does not quote that subsection. In today's stored text, that numbering falls in the sentences on partial rent accepted after the notice: "If partial rent is accepted after posting the notice for nonpayment, the landlord must ... Post a new 3-day notice reflecting the new amount due." (Fla. Stat. 83.56(5)(a))
- Waiver in the lease. Moskos, under part I in 1971: "Here the notice was not necessary because the tenant in the lease expressly waived all notice required by law." ([[Moskos v. Hand]] at 796) Today's text of 83.56(4): "The notice requirements of subsections (1)-(3) may not be waived in the rental agreement." (Fla. Stat. 83.56(4)) Clark, and Bell's footnote on 83.47(1)(a): [[Waiving the statutory notice in the lease]].
