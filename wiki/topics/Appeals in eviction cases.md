# Appeals in eviction cases

Cases on review of orders in eviction cases: which orders may be appealed before a final judgment, what a
circuit court may do when it sits as the appellate court over the county court, and a district court's
review of that circuit court decision by certiorari.

## Cases

- [[McGuire v. Nelson]], Fla. 5th DCA 1980, reading the 1979 statute: Did the circuit court depart from the essential requirements of the law by dismissing the tenant's appeal as raising no justiciable issue?
- [[First Hanover v. Vazquez]], Fla. 3d DCA 2003, reading the 2002 statute: Is an order denying a landlord's motion for a default and a writ of possession appealable before final judgment?
- [[Shell v. Foulkes]], Fla. 4th DCA 2009, reading the 2008 statute: Could the circuit court, as an appellate court, review a county court default entered without a final judgment? Was the county court's dismissal of the tenant's motion for relief from the default under rule 1.540(b) appealable, and did the county court have to hear the motion?

## How they relate

- Two layers of review. McGuire and Shell both reached a district court after a circuit court had sat as the appellate court over a county court eviction. McGuire: "Since the circuit court was sitting as the appellate court, we cannot review the merits of the case as a second appeal." ([[McGuire v. Nelson]] at 43) And: "The petitioner must demonstrate to this court that the circuit court exceeded its jurisdiction or departed from the essential requirements of the law in its order or proceedings." ([[McGuire v. Nelson]] at 43) Shell, though filed as an appeal, was treated as a petition for certiorari. Shell does not cite McGuire.
- What the circuit court did. In McGuire it had dismissed an appeal from an amended final judgment: "We think the circuit court improperly avoided its responsibility to review the county court’s amended final judgment." ([[McGuire v. Nelson]] at 43) In Shell no final judgment had been entered, and two circuit judges each decided an appeal from the default: "The Circuit Court should have dismissed the appeals for lack of appellate jurisdiction." ([[Shell v. Foulkes]] at 440) On the county court's later dismissal of the tenant's motion for relief from the default: "This was a departure from the essential requirements of law because it is appealable as a final order." ([[Shell v. Foulkes]] at 440)
- Orders before a final judgment. First Hanover, an appeal to the Third District from an order denying a landlord's motion for a default and a writ of possession: "Initially we observe that such orders, although denying relief, have been held to be orders determining the right to immediate possession of property, and as such, are appealable under Florida Rule of Appellate Procedure 9.130(a)(3)(C)(ii)." ([[First Hanover v. Vazquez]] at 1189) Shell, on an appeal from the county court to the circuit court from an order entering a default: "An order merely entering a default without a consequent final judgment is not a final order." ([[Shell v. Foulkes]] at 440) And: "As for general law, nothing in Chapters 26 or 83, part II, Florida Statutes, purports to give Circuit Courts appellate jurisdiction to review non-final orders merely entering a default." ([[Shell v. Foulkes]] at 440) The two concern different orders and different courts of review, and Shell does not cite First Hanover.
- A question Shell left open. In a footnote Shell described the 2008 text of 83.60(2) in its own words, not quoting it, and did not decide the tenant's reading: "Tenant raises a substantial argument that the text of the statute allows a default only if a tenant fails to file such a motion, not that documentary proof must be filed with the motion. Although we may conclude that such an argument presents a substantial basis to reverse a decision of the trial court, we refrain from actually deciding that issue herein." ([[Shell v. Foulkes]] at 440) And: "In so doing, the County Court is free to address the issue in the first instance as to the correct interpretation of § 83.60(2), Fla. Stat. (2008)." ([[Shell v. Foulkes]] at 441) Today's stored text reads: "If a motion to determine rent is filed, documentation in support of the allegation that the rent as alleged in the complaint is in error is required." (Fla. Stat. 83.60(2)) McGuire and First Hanover also concern the registry: [[Rent paid into the court registry]].
