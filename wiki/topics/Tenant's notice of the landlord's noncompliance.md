# Tenant's notice of the landlord's noncompliance

Cases on the written notice a tenant gives the landlord of a failure to comply, before ending the rental
agreement or withholding rent, and on claims and defenses a tenant raises without it.

Today's stored text:
- "If the landlord materially fails to comply with s. 83.51 (1) or material provisions of the rental agreement within 7 days after delivery of written notice by the tenant specifying the noncompliance and indicating the intention of the tenant to terminate the rental agreement by reason thereof, the tenant may terminate the rental agreement." (Fla. Stat. 83.56(1))
- "The defense of a material noncompliance with s. 83.51 (1) may be raised by the tenant if 7 days have elapsed after the delivery of written notice by the tenant to the landlord, specifying the noncompliance and indicating the intention of the tenant not to pay rent by reason thereof." (Fla. Stat. 83.60(1)(b))

## Cases

- [[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]], Fla. 5th DCA 1984, reading the 1981 statute: Could the tenants recover the lost value of their apartments in negligence without the written notice in 83.56, and did the evidence support the compensatory awards?
- [[Lakeway Management Co. of Florida v. Stolowilsky]], Fla. 3d DCA 1988, reading the 1985 statute: Could the tenants raise constructive eviction when their first written complaint came after the landlord's three-day notice?

## How they relate

- A claim for damages. K.D. Lewis, on the tenants' counterclaim for the lost value of their apartments: "To recover for diminishment in the value of the premises, it is necessary for the tenant to comply with section 83.56, Florida Statutes (1981), which provides as follows" ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1037) And: "To accept the tenants’ argument that the notice under section 83.56 is not required because they framed their claim in negligence would defeat the legislative requirement of notice." ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1038) On injury to the tenant or the tenant's property: "Of course, where a tenant or his property is injured by the negligence of the landlord, his cause of action requires no notice under the statute." ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1038)
- A defense to the landlord's suit. Lakeway, on a condominium lease, cites 83.60(1) for the tenant's written complaint and 83.20(2), in part I, for the landlord's three-day notice: "Only after the lessor sent a three-day notice pursuant to Section 83.20(2), Florida Statutes (1985) did the tenant make a complaint in writing which would meet the condition precedent to urging constructive eviction. See Section 83.60(1), Florida Statutes (1985)." ([[Lakeway Management Co. of Florida v. Stolowilsky]] at 951) Lakeway does not cite K.D. Lewis.
- The wording of 83.60(1). K.D. Lewis quotes the 1981 text: "The defense of a material noncompliance with s.83.51(1) [F.S.1973] may be raised by the tenant if 7 days have elapsed after the delivery of written notice by the tenant to the landlord as prescribed in s.83.56(4) [F.S.1973], specifying the noncompliance and indicating the intention of the tenant not to pay rent by reason thereof." ([[K.D. Lewis Enterprises Corp. v. Smith]] at 1034) Today's 83.60(1)(b), above, no longer names 83.56(4), and goes on: "Such notice by the tenant may be given to the landlord, the landlord’s representative as designated pursuant to s. 83.50 , a resident manager, or the person or entity who collects the rent on behalf of the landlord." (Fla. Stat. 83.60(1)(b))
- Kaplan, a part I case on a warehouse lease that is not listed on this topic, speaks of the tenant's notice in a constructive eviction claim: "Even assuming we would recognize an improperly filled-out notice to pay rent or quit as a basis for constructive eviction, McCabe would still not carry the day; timely notice must be given to the landlord of the objectionable act and a demand for rectification must be made." ([[Kaplan v. McCabe]] at 1357) More on constructive eviction: [[Constructive eviction]].
