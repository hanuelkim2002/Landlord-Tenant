# Tenant's liability for damage to the premises

Cases on a tenant's liability to the landlord for damage done to the premises: under the tenant's duty not to
commit waste, under the lease's terms, and who must prove which part of the damage. Neither case reads part
II on this question. Stegeman, a lessor's suit against a corporate lessee over a twenty-year lease, cites no
section of ch. 83; Multach is a commercial lease of a retail store and cites part II only on another issue,
waiver by accepting rent.

Today's stored text:
- "Not destroy, deface, damage, impair, or remove any part of the premises or property therein belonging to the landlord nor permit any person to do so." (Fla. Stat. 83.52(6))

## Cases

- [[Stegeman v. Burger Chef Systems]], Fla. 1st DCA 1979, no statute year stated: Did the lease termination agreement relieve the lessee of liability for waste done to the premises while its subtenant held over? Who bears the burden of proof on the damages, and on what part of them came from normal wear and depreciation?
- [[Multach v. Adams]], Fla. 4th DCA 1982, reading the 1981 statute: Was the tenant liable under the lease for the holes in the wall and the damaged floor?

## How they relate

- Multach cites Stegeman on the damage claim: "Multach is entitled to judgment on this account. Stegeman v. Burger Chef Systems, Inc., 374 So.2d 1130 (Fla. 1st DCA 1979)." ([[Multach v. Adams]] at 1255) Each court names a different source for the tenant's liability. Stegeman, where a lease termination agreement was at issue: "We reverse. Implicit in the landlord-tenant relationship is the obligation on the part of the tenant not to commit waste to the leased premises." ([[Stegeman v. Burger Chef Systems]] at 1131) Multach, on the lease: "Such damages were contrary to the express terms of the lease, and particularly Paragraph Twelfth." ([[Multach v. Adams]] at 1255)
- Who proves what. Stegeman: "The lessor has the burden of proving the damages claimed to have occurred since December 31, 1975. The burden of proving what portion, if any, of the damages and cost of restoration resulted from normal wear and depreciation, not recoverable by the lessor is on Burger Chef." ([[Stegeman v. Burger Chef Systems]] at 1131) Multach does not speak to the burden; its facts were these: "The evidence clearly showed that Adams placed two holes in the exterior wall, one for an air conditioner and one for a garage door." ([[Multach v. Adams]] at 1255)
- Part II. Multach reads 83.56(5) of 1981 on the separate question of waiver by accepting rent, and says of it: "However, this statute is applicable only to the rental of a dwelling unit and mobile home lot." ([[Multach v. Adams]] at 1256) That issue is on [[Accepting or refusing rent]]. Neither case reads 83.52, whose subsection (6) is quoted above.
