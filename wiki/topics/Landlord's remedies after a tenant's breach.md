# Landlord's remedies after a tenant's breach

Cases on the choices 83.595 gives a landlord once a tenant breaches and gives up possession, and what each
choice means for the rent and charges still to come. Two of the cases are not about a dwelling and draw on
the lessor's courses of action in earlier case law: Colonial Promenade, a commercial lease case that cites no
section of ch. 83, and Cupeiro, an oral lease of an aircraft, which cites 83.595(1)(a) of 1987 only with a see
also, in a footnote on a point the court did not need to reach.

Today's stored text:
- "Treat the rental agreement as terminated and retake possession for his or her own account, thereby terminating any further liability of the tenant" (Fla. Stat. 83.595(1))
- "Retake possession of the dwelling unit for the account of the tenant, holding the tenant liable for the difference between the rent stipulated to be paid under the rental agreement and what the landlord is able to recover from a reletting." (Fla. Stat. 83.595(2))
- "If the landlord retakes possession, the landlord has a duty to exercise good faith in attempting to relet the premises, and any rent received by the landlord as a result of the reletting must be deducted from the balance of rent due from the tenant." (Fla. Stat. 83.595(2))

## Cases

- [[Colonial Promenade v. Juhas]], Fla. 5th DCA 1989, no statute year stated: Does a landlord who evicts a defaulting tenant lose a claim, under the lease, for rent for the balance of the term less what it recovers by reletting?
- [[Cupeiro v. Baron]], Fla. 3d DCA 1989, reading the 1987 statute: on this topic outside the issues it decided; see the case page.
- [[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558)]], Fla. 4th DCA 2008, reading the 2007 statute: Was the lease's default provision, setting three months' rent as liquidated damages for a tenant who leaves before the end of the term, enforceable? Are the remedies in 83.595 available in addition to common law remedies, such as liquidated damages under a signed lease?
- [[Atlantis Estate Acquisitions, Inc. v. DePierro]], Fla. 4th DCA 2013, no statute year stated: Was the landlord owed the utilities for the rest of the term after ending the lease?

## How they relate

- Atlantis, on the first option: "Termination under the first option also ends any further liability of the tenant under the rental agreement." ([[Atlantis Estate Acquisitions, Inc. v. DePierro]] at 892)
- Olen, on a residential form lease and the 2007 statute: "We also find that the attempt to create a liquidated damage remedy violated section 83.595(1), which sets out the total universe of choices available to a landlord when a tenant has not completed the term of a lease." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558)]] at 560) Asked whether common law remedies are available too: "We reject this invitation to expand section 83.595 by judicial interpretation." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558)]] at 561) and "While Florida adopted some provisions of the Uniform Act, the legislature did not adopt section 1.103." ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558)]] at 561) The liquidated damages part of Olen is on [[Liquidated damages and early termination fees]].
- The wording changed. Olen quotes the 2007 second option: "(b) Retake possession of the dwelling unit for the account of the tenant, holding the tenant liable for the difference between rental stipulated to be paid under the lease agreement and what, in good faith, the landlord is able to recover from a reletting; or" ([[Olen Properties Corp. v. Moss (984 So. 2d 558)]] at 560) Today's 83.595(2), above, says rental agreement for lease, puts good faith in a separate sentence on a duty to relet, and defines that good faith. In the versions Cupeiro (1987) and Olen (2007) read, the options are lettered under 83.595(1), and Cupeiro cites the first as 83.595(1)(a); today's stored section numbers them (1) through (4), and the fourth, liquidated damages or an early termination fee, is not among the three Olen quotes.
- Colonial Promenade, on a commercial lease, takes the lessor's courses of action from the Supreme Court's Wagner v. Rice, not in `cases/`: "Indeed, the Florida Supreme Court has expressly recognized that upon breach of a lease one of three courses of action available in Florida to the lessor is the re-taking of possession for the account of the tenant, holding the latter responsible in general damages measured by the difference between the stipulated rent and any amount the lessor is able to recover in good faith from his re-letting." ([[Colonial Promenade v. Juhas]] at 1315) On eviction: "Contrary to the trial court’s determination, it is not the fact of eviction by the landlord that is determinative; rather, it is the nature of his use of the property after the defaulting tenant leaves, whether voluntarily or via eviction, that governs the availability of the landlord’s remedies." ([[Colonial Promenade v. Juhas]] at 1314) Atlantis also cites Wagner v. Rice, on advance rent, and does not cite Colonial Promenade.
- What the landlord did on retaking. Colonial Promenade: "Ordinarily, the nature of the lessor’s repossession is a question of fact." ([[Colonial Promenade v. Juhas]] at 1315) Atlantis: "Although the landlord claims that it was exercising the second option, it did not make the tenant aware that it would hold the tenant liable for continuing obligations under the lease." ([[Atlantis Estate Acquisitions, Inc. v. DePierro]] at 893) Cupeiro, on an aircraft, in a footnote on a point it did not need to reach: "Since there was no showing that Baron’s repossession of the aircraft and his subsequent efforts to dispose of it were taken on Cupeiro’s account, rather than his own, Baron’s damages are limited to $15,000, the one month’s rent due before Baron terminated the lease." ([[Cupeiro v. Baron]] at 371) None of the four cites another.
- Hefley v. Holmquist (Fla. 5th DCA 2025), on the case-pool list for 83.595, is not in `cases/`.
