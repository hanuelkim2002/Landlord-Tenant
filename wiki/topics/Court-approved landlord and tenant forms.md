# Court-approved landlord and tenant forms

Decisions of the Florida Supreme Court approving fill-in forms, including notices under part II, that
nonlawyers may help complete. The forms were drafted by the Bar. The Bar also prepared instructions,
whose publication the court authorized without passing on their correctness; the opinion does not say
whether a form's printed notes are part of those instructions. Also, a district court case on a
court-approved form lease signed together with the addendum whose form 83.595(4) sets out. Part II itself
gives the form of some notices and of that addendum.

Today's stored text:
- "The written notice must contain a statement in substantially the following form:" (Fla. Stat. 83.49(3)(a))
- "The 3-day notice shall contain a statement in substantially the following form:" (Fla. Stat. 83.56(3))
- "The tenant must indicate acceptance of liquidated damages or an early termination fee by signing a separate addendum to the rental agreement containing a provision in substantially the following form:" (Fla. Stat. 83.595(4))

## Cases

- [[Florida Bar re Approval of Forms (1991)]], Fla. 1991, reading the 1990 statute: What did the court approve?
- [[In re Revisions to Simplified Forms (2010)]], Fla. 2010, no statute year stated: What did the court approve?
- [[Wilson v. Terwillinger]], Fla. 5th DCA 2014, reading the 2012 statute: Is a liquidated damages addendum in the 83.595(4) form enforceable when the lease, a court-approved form, says nothing about liquidated damages?

## How they relate

- What the court said about the instructions: "While we authorize the publication of the instructions and information sheet, we do not express an opinion on the legal correctness of either." ([[Florida Bar re Approval of Forms (1991)]] at 595)
- The forms cite the 1990 statute, for example: "SOURCE: Section 83.49(3)(a), Florida Statutes (1990)." ([[Florida Bar re Approval of Forms (1991)]] at 596) The deposit form's note gives 15 days; today's 83.49(3)(a) speaks of "the 30-day period" (Fla. Stat. 83.49(3)(a)); see [[Landlord's notice of claim on the deposit]].
- The 2010 decision, on its forms and on the instructions: "We express no opinion as to whether the approved lease forms comport with current law." ([[In re Revisions to Simplified Forms (2010)]] at 503) "While we authorize the publication of the instructions, we do not express an opinion on their legal correctness." ([[In re Revisions to Simplified Forms (2010)]] at 503) The 1991 decision names its rule 10-1.1(b), the 2010 decision rule 10–2.1(a). The 2010 appendix of forms is not in `cases/`, since its changes are marked by formatting.
- Wilson's lease was a court-approved form, and Wilson cites the 2010 decision for it in a footnote: "See In Re Revisions to Simplified Forms Pursuant to Rule 10–2.1(A) of Rules Regulating The Fla. Bar, 50 So.3d 503, 503–11, 523–24 (Fla.2010)" ([[Wilson v. Terwillinger]] at 1123) Wilson does not cite the 1991 decision.
- What the court passed on. The 1991 court on its instructions is quoted above, and it held back the lease form: "However, we reserve ruling on form 1, the Residential Lease (For A Term Not To Exceed One Year), pending oral argument on that proposed form, which has been scheduled for Monday, February 3, 1992." ([[Florida Bar re Approval of Forms (1991)]] at 595) Wilson describes the 2010 decision as "setting forth the form contract without passing on its legal correctness" ([[Wilson v. Terwillinger]] at 1123)
- The form lease and the statutory addendum. Wilson: "Although the provisions in the body of the lease do not include this remedy, the addendum is attached to the lease." ([[Wilson v. Terwillinger]] at 1123) The question the county court certified: "[w]hether an addendum, providing for liquidated damages or an early termination fee, as provided in the rental agreement, in the form set forth in § 83.595(4), Fla. Stat., constitutes a valid, binding obligation, where the parties’ lease, in the form approved by the Florida Supreme Court and The Florida Bar, omits any reference to liquidated damages or an early termination fee." ([[Wilson v. Terwillinger]] at 1122) The answer is quoted on [[Liquidated damages and early termination fees]].
