# How a fee award is computed

Cases on the amount of an attorney's fee once a party is entitled to one: the hours and rate, a multiplier
for risk, a reduction for limited success, and time spent on related claims. Malt and Anglia Jacs, both suits
on a lease, cite no section of ch. 83; Malt's is a warehouse lease.

## Cases

- [[Malt v. R.J. Mueller Enterprises]], Fla. 4th DCA 1981, no statute year stated: On the tenant's cross appeal, was the attorney's fee award too low?
- [[Florida Patient's Compensation Fund v. Rowe]], Fla. 1985, reading the 1981 and 1983 statutes: How is a reasonable attorney fee computed?
- [[Malagon v. Solari]], Fla. 4th DCA 1990, reading the 1989 statute: May the fee be reduced for limited success?
- [[Meli Investment Corp. v. O.R.]], Fla. 3d DCA 1993, reading the 1991 statute: Could the fee for the landlord-tenant part of the case carry a risk multiplier?
- [[Florida RS, Inc. v. Nelson]], Fla. 5th DCA 1998, reading the 1995 statute: May the fee cover work on a class action that was never certified?
- [[Anglia Jacs & Co. v. Dubin]], Fla. 4th DCA 2002, no statute year stated: Could the landlords' fee award include time spent on claims the tenant won or that became moot?

## How they relate

- The method. Rowe: "The number of hours reasonably expended, determined in the first step, multiplied by a reasonable hourly rate, determined in the second step, produces the lodestar, which is an objective basis for the award of attorney fees." ([[Florida Patient's Compensation Fund v. Rowe]] at 1151)
- A later change to Rowe, as Malagon notes it: "modified on other grounds, Standard Guar. Ins. Co. v. Quanstrom, 555 So.2d 828 (Fla.1990)." ([[Malagon v. Solari]] at 354) Meli applies Quanstrom: "Quanstrom rejected the general use of risk multipliers in category 1 and 3 cases, but approved their use in category 2 cases, if certain criteria are met." ([[Meli Investment Corp. v. O.R.]] at 677) Quanstrom is not in `cases/`.
- Reduction for what was not won. Rowe: "The “results obtained” may provide an independent basis for reducing the fee when the party prevails on a claim or claims for relief, but is unsuccessful on other unrelated claims." ([[Florida Patient's Compensation Fund v. Rowe]] at 1151) Malagon: "Thus, the lack of success may provide an independent basis which a court can consider in reducing attorney's fees." ([[Malagon v. Solari]] at 354) Nelson: "Only on that issue did he prevail." ([[Florida RS, Inc. v. Nelson]] at 55)
- A tenant who is also the lawyer. Nelson, without deciding it: "Although we question the authority of granting a contingency fee multiplier when the attorney is also the plaintiff" ([[Florida RS, Inc. v. Nelson]] at 55)
- Review of the amount. Malt, decided in 1981, before Rowe, affirms the amount on the tenant's cross appeal without stating a method: "The award of attorney’s fees has not been demonstrated to have constituted an abuse of the trial court’s discretion and it is therefore affirmed." ([[Malt v. R.J. Mueller Enterprises]] at 1176) Anglia Jacs, in 2002: "Determination of attorney’s fees is within the sound discretion of the trial court and will not be disturbed absent a showing of abuse of discretion." ([[Anglia Jacs & Co. v. Dubin]] at 171) Neither cites Rowe.
- Time spent on claims won, lost, or moot. In Anglia Jacs the hours and rate were not in dispute: "Jacs does not dispute either the amount of hours expended by Dubin’s attorneys or the hourly rate claimed." ([[Anglia Jacs & Co. v. Dubin]] at 171) Anglia Jacs: "In other words, where the claims involve a “common core” of facts and are based on “related legal theories,” a full fee may be awarded unless it can be shown that the attorneys spent a separate and distinct amount of time on counts as to which no attorney’s fees were sought." ([[Anglia Jacs & Co. v. Dubin]] at 172) Rowe's sentence on results obtained, above, speaks of "other unrelated claims" ([[Florida Patient's Compensation Fund v. Rowe]] at 1151), and Meli sends its case back "to allocate the award of fees between the defense of the complaint and prosecution of the counterclaim" ([[Meli Investment Corp. v. O.R.]] at 677). Anglia Jacs cites neither. It is also on [[Determining the prevailing party]] and [[Pleading a claim for fees]].
