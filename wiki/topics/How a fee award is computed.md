# How a fee award is computed

Cases on the amount of an attorney's fee once a party is entitled to one: the hours and rate, a multiplier
for risk, and a reduction for limited success.

## Cases

- [[Florida Patient's Compensation Fund v. Rowe]], Fla. 1985: how is a reasonable attorney fee computed?
- [[Malagon v. Solari]], Fla. 4th DCA 1990: may the fee be reduced for limited success?
- [[Meli Investment Corp. v. O.R.]], Fla. 3d DCA 1993: could the fee for the landlord-tenant part of the case carry a risk multiplier?
- [[Florida RS, Inc. v. Nelson]], Fla. 5th DCA 1998: may the fee cover work on a class action that was never certified?

## How they relate

- The method. Rowe: "The number of hours reasonably expended, determined in the first step, multiplied by a reasonable hourly rate, determined in the second step, produces the lodestar, which is an objective basis for the award of attorney fees." ([[Florida Patient's Compensation Fund v. Rowe]] at 1151)
- A later change to Rowe, as Malagon notes it: "modified on other grounds, Standard Guar. Ins. Co. v. Quanstrom, 555 So.2d 828 (Fla.1990)." ([[Malagon v. Solari]] at 354) Meli applies Quanstrom: "Quanstrom rejected the general use of risk multipliers in category 1 and 3 cases, but approved their use in category 2 cases, if certain criteria are met." ([[Meli Investment Corp. v. O.R.]] at 677) Quanstrom is not in `cases/`.
- Reduction for what was not won. Rowe: "The “results obtained” may provide an independent basis for reducing the fee when the party prevails on a claim or claims for relief, but is unsuccessful on other unrelated claims." ([[Florida Patient's Compensation Fund v. Rowe]] at 1151) Malagon: "Thus, the lack of success may provide an independent basis which a court can consider in reducing attorney's fees." ([[Malagon v. Solari]] at 354) Nelson: "Only on that issue did he prevail." ([[Florida RS, Inc. v. Nelson]] at 55)
- A tenant who is also the lawyer. Nelson, without deciding it: "Although we question the authority of granting a contingency fee multiplier when the attorney is also the plaintiff" ([[Florida RS, Inc. v. Nelson]] at 55)
