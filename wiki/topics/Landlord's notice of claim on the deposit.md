# Landlord's notice of claim on the deposit

Cases on the written notice a landlord gives of an intention to impose a claim on the deposit: what follows
when it is late or missing, and when it is not required.

Today's stored text:
- "If the landlord fails to give the required written notice within the 30-day period, he or she forfeits the right to impose a claim upon the security deposit and may not seek a setoff against the deposit but may file an action for damages after returning the security deposit to the tenant." (Fla. Stat. 83.49(3)(a))
- "Failure to give such notice relieves the landlord of the notice requirement of paragraph (3)(a) but does not waive any right the tenant may have to the security deposit or any part of it." (Fla. Stat. 83.49(5))

## Cases

- [[Durene v. Alcime]], Fla. 3d DCA 1984, reading the 1981 statute: does a late notice bar all of the landlord's damage claims, or only claims against the deposit?
- [[Casey v. Picinich]], Fla. 4th DCA 1990, reading the 1987 statute: what follows from an untimely notice?
- [[Malagon v. Solari]], Fla. 4th DCA 1990, reading the 1989 statute: could the landlord's counterclaim damages be set off against the deposit?
- [[Florida Bar re Approval of Forms (1991)]], Fla. 1991: the approved form for the notice, citing the 1990 statute.
- [[Plakhov v. Serova]], Fla. 4th DCA 2012, reading the 2009 statute: was the notice required when the tenant moved out early without the 83.49(5) notice?

## How they relate

- Malagon follows Durene: "As discussed in Durene v. Alcime, 448 So.2d 1208 (Fla. 3d DCA 1984), failure to give the required notice penalizes a landlord by forfeiting his access to the security deposit in an independent action for damages, and by making the landlord liable for attorney's fees and costs under section 83.49(3)(c)." ([[Malagon v. Solari]] at 354)
- Plakhov distinguishes Durene: "that case involved a situation where the landlord's obligation to give the section 83.49(3)(a) notice was not excused by the Tenant's failure to give the notice required by section 83.49(5)." ([[Plakhov v. Serova]] at 1223)
- Using the deposit against the landlord's damages. In Durene, the notice was late: "The security deposit may not be used for purposes of a set-off." ([[Durene v. Alcime]] at 1210) In Malagon, the landlord won on a counterclaim: "the damages awarded on the counterclaim cannot be construed as a setoff against the security deposit" ([[Malagon v. Solari]] at 354) In Plakhov, the tenant had left without the 83.49(5) notice: "The lease did not limit how the security deposit could be applied, so the trial judge properly applied it to reduce the amount of damages owed by the Tenant." ([[Plakhov v. Serova]] at 1223)
- The statute's period changed. Durene read a 15-day period: "If the landlord fails to give the required notice within the 15-day period, he forfeits his right to impose a claim upon the security deposit." ([[Durene v. Alcime]] at 1209) The 1991 form's printed note, the Bar's wording, also speaks of 15 days: "If the landlord does not send the notice within the 15 day period he cannot keep the security deposit." ([[Florida Bar re Approval of Forms (1991)]] at 596) Today's text, above, gives 30 days and also speaks of a setoff and of an action for damages.
- Casey does not cite Durene.
