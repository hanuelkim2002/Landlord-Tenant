# Tenant's damages for the landlord's breach

Cases on the damages a tenant sought for a landlord's breach: rent, the value of improvements, lost
business, and damages in tort. None of them reads a section of part II: Berwick (1962) predates the 1973
Act; Berwick, Malt, and Caso are commercial leases; Clemente names 83.67 (1995) only as the ground of a
count the tenants dropped.

Today's stored text:
- "If either the landlord or the tenant fails to comply with the requirements of the rental agreement or this part, the aggrieved party may recover the damages caused by the noncompliance." (Fla. Stat. 83.55)

## Cases

- [[Berwick Corp. v. Kleinginna Investment Corp.]], Fla. 3d DCA 1962, no statute year stated: Could the tenant recover a rebate of rent for each room made uninhabitable? Did the tenant prove damages for the unamortized value of its improvements?
- [[Malt v. R.J. Mueller Enterprises]], Fla. 4th DCA 1981, no statute year stated: On the tenant's cross appeal, were the damages for breach of the sublease and for overpaid rent too low?
- [[Caso v. Stanley Nelson, Inc]], Fla. 4th DCA 1982, no statute year stated: Could the tenant prove damages to the business his corporation ran on the premises, when the lease was with him individually? On rehearing, should the jury's damage award be reinstated, or the damages retried?
- [[Clemente v. Horne]], Fla. 3d DCA 1998, reading the 1995 statute: Did the landlords' alleged failure to keep the home in line with safety and health laws, ending in a constructive eviction, state a claim for intentional infliction of emotional distress?

## How they relate

- What was leased. Berwick, decided before the Act: "The Berwick Corporation was the landlord of the Kleinginna Investment Corporation by virtue of a written lease upon an ocean front motel." ([[Berwick Corp. v. Kleinginna Investment Corp.]] at 685) Malt: "The building in question was a warehouse which the tenant (Mueller) rented for $38,500 per year." ([[Malt v. R.J. Mueller Enterprises]] at 1175) Caso: "The tenant leased the space to operate a shoe business." ([[Caso v. Stanley Nelson, Inc]] at 670) Clemente concerns a rented home; its count under 83.67 was dropped before the appeal: "The lower court dismissed count two with prejudice based upon the economic loss doctrine, and the appellants voluntarily dismissed count one." ([[Clemente v. Horne]] at 866) None of the four cites 83.55 or another of them.
- Rent. Berwick, on a rebate of rent for each room made uninhabitable: "We need only point out that the rent specified by the lease was an annual rent for the entire premises and not on a daily or room basis." ([[Berwick Corp. v. Kleinginna Investment Corp.]] at 688) "It is well recognized that annual rent may not be apportioned, Thompson, Real Property § 286 [2d Ed.1939], nor may rent for an entire premises be apportioned upon a room basis." ([[Berwick Corp. v. Kleinginna Investment Corp.]] at 688) In Malt, the tenant's cross appeal challenged the amounts awarded for the landlord's breach of a sublease back from the tenant and for rent the tenant overpaid: "Under the complex facts of this case, we conclude that the amount of these damages was in dispute and that the trial court did not err in the amounts actually awarded." ([[Malt v. R.J. Mueller Enterprises]] at 1176) Malt's reformation of the lease, on the building's size, is on [[Interpreting the lease]].
- Proof of an amount. Berwick, on the tenant's improvements, left their recoverability open: "Therefore, without passing upon the question of the recoverability of such damages in a situation such as the one before us, we hold that the trial judge correctly determined that damages for improvements made were not proved." ([[Berwick Corp. v. Kleinginna Investment Corp.]] at 689) "It is well established that before damages may be awarded, there must be evidence authorizing or justifying the award of a definite amount." ([[Berwick Corp. v. Kleinginna Investment Corp.]] at 689)
- Lost business. Caso, on a lease made with the tenant individually for a store his corporation ran: "Under these circumstances we do not believe the tenant was foreclosed from establishing damages to the business despite the fact that the tenant operated the business in a corporate form." ([[Caso v. Stanley Nelson, Inc]] at 670) On rehearing, the court sent the amount back for a new trial: "We agree with the appellee that this cause should be remanded for a new trial solely on the issue of appellants’ damages rather than for reinstatement of the damage award returned by the jury." ([[Caso v. Stanley Nelson, Inc]] at 670)
- Damages in tort after a constructive eviction. Berwick and Caso are also on [[Constructive eviction]]. In Clemente, tenants who alleged a constructive eviction from their home sought damages for intentional infliction of emotional distress: "The anxiety and/or stress associated with being constructively evicted from one’s residence under the circumstances presented in this case, and not having suitable alternative housing is certainly understandable. It is not, however, the type of conduct that is so outrageous in character and so extreme in degree as to go beyond the bounds of decency and be deemed utterly intolerable in a civilized community." ([[Clemente v. Horne]] at 867) In the same passage the court quoted State Farm Mutual Automobile Insurance Co. v. Novotny, 657 So. 2d 1210 (not in `cases/`), which speaks of punitive damages: "It is not enough that the intent is tortious or criminal; it is not enough that the defendant intended to inflict emotional distress; and it is not enough if the conduct was characterized by malice or aggravation which would entitle the plaintiff to punitive damages for another tort." ([[Clemente v. Horne]] at 867) Punitive damages: [[Punitive damages against a landlord]].
