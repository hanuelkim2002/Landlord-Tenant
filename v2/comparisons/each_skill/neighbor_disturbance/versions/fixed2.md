# Neighbor disturbance

Rests on 83.52 (the duty not to disturb), 83.56 (the landlord's remedy, ending the lease, notices, rent paid), 83.51 (what the landlord must keep up), 83.60 (holding back rent), 83.55 and 83.54 (damages, suits), 83.43 and 83.44 (good faith), 83.47 (void clauses), 83.48 (attorney fees), 83.49 (the 7 days' notice), 83.59 (getting the home back), 83.64 (retaliation), and 83.505 (e-mail notices). Read a section in full with get_statute_section.

When the answer turns on a point marked not settled, the first sentence says it is not settled and leans neither way (never "probably yes"). Every point marked not settled gets both readings, and you choose neither. When the tenant asks "can I" and the answer turns on such a point, write "You can if X, and X is not settled", never a plain yes.

## First, whether this applies

- Load who_the_law_covers, unless the facts already hold school_housing.
- The landlord sent this tenant a notice about their own noise or guests: load notice_received_violation too.
- The tenant wants to move out and option 2 does not fit: load wants_to_leave_early too.
- Threats of harm, violence, or a crime: say to call the police. That is outside this tool.
- The landlord or its workers are the ones disturbing: the points below about a neighbor do not fit, but options 2 and 3 still reach a lease promise the landlord itself breaks.

## What the lease adds

Read the whole lease, rules and addenda included, for anything about quiet, noise, other residents, or who enforces the rules. Quote each clause and cite it.

- **A promise that the landlord will act** (it will enforce the rules, or act on complaints about other residents): a lease duty. Options 2 and 3 can reach it. Set what the landlord has done beside the promise's words.
- **Rules that bind every resident** (quiet hours, no loud parties), with no word about the landlord acting: they give the landlord a ground against the neighbor. Whether they also promise this tenant that the landlord will enforce them is not settled. One: rules that bind every resident are part of what this tenant was promised, and "Every rental agreement or duty within this part imposes an obligation of good faith in its performance or enforcement." (83.44). The other: they bind the resident who signs and promise nothing from the landlord.
- **Words like "quiet enjoyment":** not settled whether they reach what a neighbor does. One: the tenant was promised quiet use of the home, so a landlord that does nothing about its own tenant fails that promise. The other: the promise is about what the landlord itself does, and a neighbor's noise is not the landlord's act.
- **No such words:** say the lease makes no promise about other residents. Part II adds none, and whether other law reads such a promise into a lease without words is outside this tool; say so in one line.
- **A clause saying the landlord is not responsible for other residents:** say what it says, and set it beside any promise above. A clause is void "to the extent that it" (83.47(1)) "Purports to limit or preclude any liability of the landlord to the tenant or of the tenant to the landlord, arising under law." (83.47(1)(b)). Whether this one does is not settled. One: it only says what the lease promises, so it stands. The other: it takes away a remedy part II gives for a lease promise, so it is void to that extent.

## Facts the answer turns on

Read the lease first. Ask the tenant only plain facts, one or two at a time. Note each fact with note_fact under these names, dates written YYYY-MM-DD; a date counts only once the tenant has confirmed it.

- disturbance: what it is and when it happens, in the tenant's words. disturbance_started: the date it began.
- who_disturbs: who is causing it, as far as they know. same_landlord: whether that person rents from the same landlord.
- complaint_date: the date of their first complaint to the landlord, whether it was in writing, and what the landlord said or did.
- end_notice_delivered: the date a written notice saying they will end the lease over this reached the landlord. Also note how it went and its words.
- rent_paid_since: whether they paid rent after they knew the landlord had not acted.
- planned_move_out: the date they plan to move out, if they do.

## Must the landlord do something?

- **The neighbor's duty.** Each tenant must "Conduct himself or herself, and require other persons on the premises with his or her consent to conduct themselves, in a manner that does not unreasonably disturb the tenant’s neighbors or constitute a breach of the peace." (83.52(7)).
- **The landlord's remedy, against its own tenant.** "If the tenant materially fails to comply with s. 83.52 or material provisions of the rental agreement, other than a failure to pay rent, or reasonable rules or regulations, the landlord may:" (83.56(2)) send that tenant a notice, and the list of what gets no chance to fix includes "a subsequent or continued unreasonable disturbance." (83.56(2)(a)).
- **No duty in part II.** Apart from shared spaces (below), part II has no sentence requiring a landlord to act on one tenant's complaint about another, and it sets no deadline for one. Say so plainly. Never say the landlord must stop it or evict the neighbor. The lease can add a duty (above).
- **A neighbor who rents from someone else, or owns their home.** 83.56(2) is a landlord's remedy against its own tenant, so this landlord has none against them. Say so plainly. Their landlord, an owners' association, and the city are outside this tool.
- **The word "unreasonably" (83.52(7)) is not defined.** Set what the tenant describes (how loud, how late, how often, in their words) and any quiet hours in the lease beside the word. State no test, no hours, and no noise level of your own.
- **In a hallway, courtyard, or other shared space.** For a unit other than a single-family home or duplex, "Unless otherwise agreed in writing" (83.51(2)(a)), the landlord must make reasonable provisions for "The clean and safe condition of common areas." (83.51(2)(a)). Whether that reaches how people behave there is not settled. One: a place is not safe if people make it unsafe. The other: the words are about the condition of the place. Either way it reaches only option 3, since options 2 and 4 speak of 83.51(1), not (2).

## Option 1: ask the landlord in writing

The tenant can do this now. Part II sets no deadline from it, so count no date. The complaint describes what happens, with dates and times, and asks what the landlord will do. It helps to keep a log, any recordings with their dates, and every complaint and reply. A complaint that only asks is not option 2's notice; say so if the tenant may want option 2.

## Option 2: end the lease

The rule: "If the landlord materially fails to comply with s. 83.51 (1) or material provisions of the rental agreement within 7 days after delivery of written notice by the tenant specifying the noncompliance and indicating the intention of the tenant to terminate the rental agreement by reason thereof, the tenant may terminate the rental agreement." (83.56(1)).

All four conditions must be met. Settle each one from the facts: met, not met, the tenant can tell us, not settled, or outside the tool.

1. **A lease promise the landlord failed.** A neighbor's noise is not on 83.51(1)'s list unless a code covers it, and the codes are not stored. So this rests on the lease (see "What the lease adds"). No promise in the lease: not met on the words the tool holds; say so plainly, with the one line on what is outside this tool. A promise: whether the failure is "material" (83.56(1)) is not settled, since the stored text does not define the word. One: failing a lease promise is enough. The other: it turns on how serious the problem is and how it affects living there. Set the tenant's facts beside the word.
2. **A written notice, delivered a way the statute names, that names the problem and says they will end the lease.** "The delivery of the written notices required by subsections (1)-(3) shall be by mailing, delivering a true copy thereof, e-mailing in accordance with s. 83.505" (83.56(4)). Read the lease's clause on notices too; a way that meets both is safest.
   - Handed to the landlord or the office: delivered that day.
   - Mailed: a way the statute names, but not settled whether the mailing or the arrival counts. Say so, and that waiting 7 days from the arrival meets both. Note the arrival date as end_notice_delivered.
   - E-mail: only "if the parties have signed an addendum to the rental agreement that the parties specifically agree to the electronic delivery of notices and have each provided a valid e-mail address for such purpose, and the addendum conspicuously advises the parties that such election is voluntary and that they may revoke such agreement or update their e-mail addresses at any time." (83.505(1)). Then it is "deemed delivered at the time it is sent, unless the e-mail is returned to the sender as undeliverable." (83.505(4)).
   - A portal message or a text: not one of the ways 83.56(4) names. Sending it again by hand or by mail meets the rule.
   - The words: set the tenant's notice beside the statute's. A complaint that asks the landlord to act but does not say they will end the lease is not this notice. Say so in one sentence.
3. **Seven days passed and the landlord still failed the promise.** Call count_days(end_notice_delivered, 7). If the date has not passed, the landlord still has time; give the date. What the landlord had to do in those days comes from the promise's words; state no test of your own. If the landlord says it is trying and cannot stop it, the rule changes where the failure is "due to causes beyond the control of the landlord and the landlord has made and continues to make every reasonable effort to correct the failure to comply" (83.56(1)): load repair_not_made, option 5, for those terms.
4. **Not given up by paying rent.** If "the tenant pays rent with actual knowledge of a noncompliance by the landlord" (83.56(5)(a)), the tenant "waives his or her right to terminate the rental agreement or to bring a civil action for that noncompliance, but not for any subsequent or continuing noncompliance." (83.56(5)(a)). The noncompliance is the landlord's, not the neighbor's.
   - No rent paid since they knew the landlord had not acted: met.
   - Rent paid while the failure goes on: not settled. One: a failure still going on is a "continuing noncompliance" (83.56(5)(a)), so paying rent gives up nothing. The other: paying rent with knowledge gives up ending the lease over that failure, and only a new failure starts over.
   - Rent coming due: not paying is no safe way to keep this right, since it can bring a demand and a suit (option 4). Deciding before the rent is due avoids the question. Say both.

If all four are met and no point is left open, say plainly that the tenant may end the lease (83.56(1)). If "material" or rent paid is the only point left open, say what follows under each reading. Where the ending holds: "If the rental agreement is terminated, the landlord shall comply with s. 83.49 (3)." (83.56(6)), and deposit_not_returned covers the deposit after the move. Where it does not hold: a tenant who moved out may still owe rent, and wants_to_leave_early has the landlord's choices.

Before moving, whichever way, and "Except when otherwise provided by the terms of a written rental agreement" (83.49(5)): the tenant "must give at least 7 days’ written notice by certified mail or personal delivery to the landlord before vacating or abandoning the premises, which notice must include the address where the tenant may be reached." (83.49(5)). The last day: count_days(planned_move_out, -7).

## Option 3: damages

"If either the landlord or the tenant fails to comply with the requirements of the rental agreement or this part, the aggrieved party may recover the damages caused by the noncompliance." (83.55). This reaches a lease promise the landlord failed, and no notice is named. Rent paid with knowledge may give up the right "to bring a civil action for that noncompliance" (83.56(5)(a)) too; give option 2's two readings. A suit cuts both ways: the winner may recover attorney fees and court costs (83.48). Amounts and court steps are outside this tool.

A suit against the neighbor, if they are a tenant: not settled whether part II reaches it. One: 83.52(7) is a duty in this part, "Any right or duty declared in this part is enforceable by civil action." (83.54), and the disturbed neighbor is "the aggrieved party" (83.55). The other: 83.55 speaks of a landlord and a tenant under one agreement, and these two have none. Any other law on such a suit is outside this tool.

## Option 4: holding back rent is not a right here

Say so plainly when asked. The defense part II names covers only "a material noncompliance with s. 83.51 (1)" (83.60(1)(b)). A neighbor's noise is not on that list unless a code covers it (outside this tool), and a lease promise does not make it so.

A tenant sued for unpaid rent "may raise any other defense, whether legal or equitable, that he or she may have" (83.60(1)(a)). That does not give a right to stop paying, and it does not change the answer above. Whether a failed lease promise is such a defense is not settled. One: the words are wide enough to reach it. The other: the named defense shows rent is excused only for an 83.51(1) failure.

The risk: a written demand (83.56(3)) and a suit. Once sued: "Failure of the tenant to pay the rent into the registry of the court or to file a motion to determine the amount of rent to be paid into the registry within 5 days, excluding Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays, after the date of service of process constitutes an absolute waiver of the tenant’s defenses other than payment" (83.60(2)). Give those 5 days and what they leave out, but never a calendar date for them.

## Option 5: the police or the city

A report makes a record and may stop the noise. Local noise rules are not stored, so what they require is outside this tool. Part II does not require a report before any option here.

## If the landlord turns on the tenant for complaining

"It is unlawful for a landlord to discriminatorily increase a tenant’s rent or decrease services to a tenant, or to bring or threaten to bring an action for possession or other civil action, primarily because the landlord is retaliating against the tenant." (83.64(1)).

- **What the tenant did.** The named examples include a complaint to a code agency (83.64(1)(a)) and option 2's notice (83.64(1)(c)). An ordinary complaint about a neighbor is not named, but the examples "include, but are not limited to" (83.64(1)) those. Not settled whether it counts. One: the list is only examples, so a complaint made in good faith can count. The other: the examples are all steps of a certain kind, and a complaint about another tenant's conduct is not like them.
- **Good faith** is required (83.64(1)): "“Good faith” means honesty in fact in the conduct or transaction concerned." (83.43(8)). So is being treated differently in "the rent charged, the services rendered, or the action being taken by the landlord" (83.64(4)).
- **How it is used.** It "may be raised by the tenant as a defense in any action brought against him or her for possession." (83.64(2)). It falls away "if the landlord proves that the eviction is for good cause." (83.64(3)), whose examples include "good faith actions for nonpayment of rent, violation of the rental agreement or of reasonable rules" (83.64(3)).
- **A threat does not put anyone out.** "The landlord shall not recover possession of a dwelling unit except:" (83.59(3)) through a court case, or when the tenant gave up or abandoned the home (83.59(3)).
- A step that followed a complaint is not retaliation for that reason alone. Give the dates as the tenant gave them; never count the days between them.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Saying the landlord must stop the disturbance or evict the neighbor, or stating a deadline for it to act.
- Calling the disturbance unreasonable, or not, or stating a test for it.
- Saying a tenant may end the lease with no lease promise, or treating a complaint that only asks as the notice in 83.56(1).
- Saying the tenant has a right to hold back rent over a neighbor, even with a lease promise.
- Treating a neighbor who rents from someone else as someone this landlord can act against under 83.56(2).
- Saying a step the landlord took after a complaint was retaliation because it came after.
- Writing any date count_days did not give you, or counting how long something has lasted or how long until a date. Setting a date from the facts beside a date count_days gave is not counting.
