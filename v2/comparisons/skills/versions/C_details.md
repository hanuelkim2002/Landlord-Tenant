# Deposit not returned: details

## The statute's words, by point

- Whether 83.49 applies: "This section does not apply to transient rentals by hotels or motels as defined in chapter 509 or in those instances in which the amount of rent or deposit, or both, is regulated by law or by rules or regulations of a public body" (83.49(4)).
- College housing: part II does not apply to "Residency or detention in a facility, whether public or private, when residence or detention is incidental to the provision of medical, geriatric, educational, counseling, religious, or similar services." (83.42(1)). Read 83.42 before telling the tenant either way.
- The notice of a claim: "If the landlord intends to impose a claim on the deposit, the landlord must, within 30 days after the termination of the rental agreement, provide the tenant written notice by certified mail to the tenant’s last known mailing address or by e-mail in accordance with s. 83.505 of his or her intention to impose a claim on the deposit and the reason for imposing the claim." (83.49(3)(a)).
- E-mail: only "if the parties have signed an addendum to the rental agreement that the parties specifically agree to the electronic delivery of notices" (83.505(1)).
- Missing it: "If the landlord fails to give the required written notice within the 30-day period, he or she forfeits the right to impose a claim upon the security deposit and may not seek a setoff against the deposit but may file an action for damages after returning the security deposit to the tenant." (83.49(3)(a)).
- No claim intended: "if the landlord does not intend to impose a claim on the security deposit, the landlord must return the security deposit, together with interest if otherwise required, within 15 days after the termination of the rental agreement" (83.49(3)(a)).
- Leaving early or a periodic tenancy, who: "Except when otherwise provided by the terms of a written rental agreement, any tenant who vacates or abandons the premises before the expiration of the term specified in the rental agreement, or any tenant who vacates or abandons premises which are the subject of a tenancy from week to week, month to month, quarter to quarter, or year to year," (83.49(5)).
- How: "must give at least 7 days’ written notice by certified mail or personal delivery to the landlord before vacating or abandoning the premises, which notice must include the address where the tenant may be reached." (83.49(5)).
- Without it: "Failure to give such notice relieves the landlord of the notice requirement of paragraph (3)(a) but does not waive any right the tenant may have to the security deposit or any part of it." (83.49(5)).
- After a notice of a claim: "Unless the tenant objects to the imposition of the landlord’s claim or the amount thereof within 15 days after receipt of the landlord’s notice of intention to impose a claim, the landlord may then deduct the amount of his or her claim and shall remit the balance of the deposit to the tenant within 30 days after the date of the notice of intention to impose a claim for damages." (83.49(3)(b)).
- Not objecting: "The failure of the tenant to make a timely objection does not waive any rights of the tenant to seek damages in a separate action." (83.49(3)(b)).
- Costs and fees, only if someone sues: "If either party institutes an action in a court of competent jurisdiction to adjudicate the party’s right to the security deposit, the prevailing party is entitled to receive his or her court costs plus a reasonable fee for his or her attorney." (83.49(3)(c)).

## Not settled: give each reading, choose none

1. Which date ends the rental agreement when the tenant moved out before the lease's end date. The lease end date: 83.49(3)(a) begins "Upon the vacating of the premises for termination of the rental agreement" (83.49(3)(a)), naming vacating and termination as two things. The move-out date: the required disclosure says "WITHIN 30 DAYS AFTER YOU MOVE OUT" (83.49(2)(d)).
2. A notice of a claim that came another way (regular mail, a text, a portal message, by hand). One reading: it is not the required notice, so the claim is forfeited under 83.49(3)(a). The other: a notice actually received in time is enough, since 83.505 "does not preclude service of notices by any other means permitted by law." (83.505(6)). Say what each reading means for the tenant's money.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Saying the landlord forfeits before count_days shows the 30 days passed, or when a proper notice came.
- Stopping at the forfeiture without saying the landlord may still sue for damages.
- Leaving out 83.49(5) when the tenant left early or had a periodic tenancy.
- Promising court costs or fees without the condition in 83.49(3)(c).
- Working out a date yourself.
