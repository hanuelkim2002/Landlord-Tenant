# Evaluation by Taylor Brooks (D2_amenities_yes: second visit, I said "yes" to "repair")

## In my voice

1. Did this help me? Yes, more than I expected: I got a letter I can almost send, I learned my portal tickets are not real notice, and I got a partial answer on the petition. But it still did not answer the ads question or tell me how much credit to ask for.

2. What I came for vs. what I got.
- Came for: (a) a partial refund of the $150 amenity fee and/or a Wi-Fi rent credit; (b) do the ads count; (c) can the 40 of us do something together.
- Got on (a): fee, a straight "No, not on what this tool can find" with the lease clauses 3.3 and 6.2. For the Wi-Fi credit, the closest rule (83.56(1)(b), rent reduced "in proportion to the loss of rental value") with three honest "not known yet" points, plus a drafted request letter asking for the credit.
- Got on (b): nothing. "Whether the ads count is outside this tool." It said it searched my lease for advertising, marketing, promise and entire agreement and found nothing.
- Got on (c): "it is not just you, but each path has limits": retaliation protection (maybe, if a petition is a "tenant organization"), a class suit (courts split), and each tenant's own notice and rights.
- Extra things I did not ask for but needed: the portal tickets do not count as formal notice (Lease 7.2, 13.1) and where and how to deliver one; a letter that only asks for a credit does not start any 7-day clock; leasing office address and e-mail.

3. Ease of use.
- The sheet: after my "yes" it asked about 14 one-line questions. Each was easy to answer and several had suggested answers I could accept with Enter. But they are all written for a broken thing in my apartment (structure, plumbing, "intend to end the lease", "intend not to pay rent", "eviction case") and I answered them for a closed pool and Wi-Fi. I only got here because I changed my answer to "yes" on the first question the second time.
- The replies: the answer is in there, but each reply is preceded or followed by a lot of material. Replies 1 and 6 start with 60 to 100 lines of court cases ("exculpatory clauses", a case about a rape and door locks, a case about a tenant with pets and carpets) before the answer. Replies 2 to 5 end with 4 to 8 printed statute and lease blocks. I read the first paragraphs and skimmed the rest.
- The letter was easy: it asked three clear questions (days, how I send it, which kind: preserve, assert, escalate with a one-line description each) and then produced a letter. It listened when I said "7.1 doesn't list wifi so I guess my answer is no" and put that on the sheet.
- The "[Check: ...]" lines at the bottom of four of the replies made no sense to me.

4. Trust.
- I believe the parts that quote my lease or the statute, because they show the exact words, section and page. I believed the "No" on the fee.
- I trust it less because: the "[Check: the quote ... is not in 83.51]" lines flagged words that were actually from my lease, or from the tool's own menu ("Adds the 83.51(1) duty, quoted."), so I stopped reading them; the "[not verified]" tags on every case; the letter printing "Problem started: August 15" and "Written notice date: September 03" which the tool itself said did not count; and the "two readings" answers where it never says which is more likely.
- Would I act on it? On sending a request letter, yes, because it is only a request and it was honest that nothing legal starts. On anything about stopping rent or ending my lease, no, I'd ask a real person first.

5. What I do now.
- Fix the two wrong date lines, add the 8 to 11 pm detail and the three portal ticket dates, maybe the gym AC, then hand-deliver the letter to the leasing office (2600 SW Hartwell Lane) and e-mail a copy to office@magnoliarow.example. The tool is why I know to deliver it on paper and not just use the portal.
- Tell some of the 40 petition signers to send their own letters, since the tool said each tenant's rights turn on their own notice.
- Look up my county/city code enforcement office, since the tool said a local code could matter, and ask UF student legal services about the ads and about the "reasonable" pool closure.
- Keep paying rent (the tool made it clear that stopping is a different, risky path).

6. Moments of confusion.
- Sheet: "Does your lease say the landlord must repair this kind of problem...?" with Lease 7.1 about heating, AC, plumbing "serving the Unit". I did not know and skipped it, and then the program said "Skipped for now: lease_promises_repair. Deadlines wait until these are answered." No idea what lease_promises_repair or "deadlines" meant.
- "Only you can answer it, and the tool does not assume either way... is your reading to give." I wanted it to say what it thinks.
- "On one reading it turns on degree... On the other, materiality comes from a duty 83.51(1) lists..." and "On the other reading, paying with that knowledge waived it." I do not know which reading is right for me.
- "[Check: the quote "high-speed internet (Wi-Fi)" (83.56(1)) is not in 83.56...]" That quote is from my lease.
- Letter note: "It is such a notice only if it states one of those intentions; ask in the chat to have this applied to your letter before you send it." Applied how?
- "I cannot fix those two lines from here. The letter is built from the sheet." So I cannot get a letter with the right dates, even though I said in the chat what is wrong.

7. What I expected and did not get.
- Anything on whether the ads count: only "outside this tool". That was a big part of why I came.
- A number: how much credit is "in proportion to the loss of rental value", what a normal Wi-Fi credit looks like.
- Anything on the management company beyond the notice rules (who actually owns it, who to complain to), and anything on UF or city resources.
- A clear "send the petition or not". It never said whether sending the petition helps or hurts, or what to do with it.
- The amenity fee: no path at all (not even "ask nicely for goodwill"). Just "no basis".
- Roommates: nothing about my roommates in the 4-bedroom unit (each has their own lease, so I'm not sure how it matters).
- A short summary at the end: what to do first, second, third.

8. Ratings (1-5)
- Usefulness: 3. A usable letter and the notice rules; no ads, no amount.
- Clarity: 2. Clear first paragraphs, but buried under cases and statute text and the odd Check lines.
- Trust: 3. Sourced quotes and honest "not known" help; the Check lines and wrong dates in the letter hurt.
- Ease of use: 3. The sheet is quick but it is a repair form; the chat is fine; answers are too long.
- Would recommend to a friend: 3. For a friend with Wi-Fi or a broken thing, yes. For a pure amenity-fee refund, I'd say it will tell you no.

9. The one change: put a short plain "what this means for you and what to do next" at the top of every answer (3 or 4 sentences), and move the cases and statute printouts to the bottom or behind a "show me the law" command. And if the answer is "outside this tool", say who can answer instead.

## Plain summary for the tool's author

Compared with D (where the "no" to the repair question ended the session in one question), saying "yes" let the tool run a full session: a 14-question repair sheet, six chat answers, a drafted letter, and a petition answer. The tool is much more useful when the student gets past the first question, so the first-question dead end in D is the worst problem.

What worked:
- Straight, quoted "No" on the amenity fee (Lease 3.3, 6.2), with the honest "no section of part II gives a refund of an amenity fee".
- 83.47(1) explained as "a lease cannot take away every right", and the "not known yet" points for the Wi-Fi rent credit under 83.56(1)(b).
- Notice rules: the portal tickets are not a notice (Lease 7.2, 13.1), where and how to send one, a request letter does not start the 7-day clock.
- A letter offered with three clear questions; it took 7 days, hand delivery plus e-mailed copy, and a rent credit request, and it put my chat answer on the sheet.
- The petition answer was honest and balanced (retaliation protection, class case split, each tenant's own notice).

Problems for the author:
1. The sheet is a repair form. Students with amenity problems answer questions about structure, plumbing, "intend to end the lease", "intend not to pay rent", and eviction. The first question's "no" ends the session (see D).
2. Replies 1 and 6 open with 60 to 100 lines of case text (exculpatory clauses with a rape and door locks case; retaliation with a pets and carpet case), tagged "[not verified]", before the answer. Replies 2 to 5 end with 4 to 8 statute and lease blocks. The answer is findable but buried.
3. The "[Check: the quote ... is not in ...]" lines are wrong in these runs. They flagged a lease quote attributed to a statute section (replies 1, 2, 3) and the tool's own letter-menu text (reply 4). Students learn to ignore them, so a real one would be missed.
4. The letter prints "Problem started: August 15, 2026" and "Written notice date: September 03, 2026" from sheet answers. The Wi-Fi started at a different time, and the tool itself says the portal date is not a notice, so the letter contradicts the advice. The tool says it cannot fix them in the chat because only the sheet changes confirmed answers.
5. The letter has only two sentences and no details: no 8 to 11 pm, no portal ticket dates, no amount. No offer to include the other amenities.
6. The skipped sheet question was labelled with a code name (lease_promises_repair) and "Deadlines wait until these are answered", and the tool would not give an opinion on it ("your reading to give").
7. The ads question was only ever "outside this tool". No hint who to ask. No mention of the petition's practical use.
8. No end-of-session summary or next steps.
